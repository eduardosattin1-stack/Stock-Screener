# nLIGHT, Inc. (LASR) — Forensic Dossier

*Prepared 2026-08-04. Price used throughout: $78.44 per share (Nasdaq, US dollars), the live quote on 2026-08-04, up 11.9% on the day. The screener's input file carried $70.11, which was the prior close. Market value about $4.42 billion; enterprise value (market value less the company's net cash) about $4.13 billion.*

*Transcripts reviewed: the five consecutive quarterly earnings calls from 2025-05-08 (first quarter 2025) through 2026-05-09 (first quarter 2026). Financial evidence: the screener metrics block, quarterly filings through the quarter ended 2026-03-31, the 2025 annual report, and dated news through 2026-08-04.*

---

## 1. Narrative arc

Over five calls nLIGHT has executed one of the cleaner narrative pivots in small-cap technology: from a struggling industrial-laser maker with a shrinking commercial book into a defense photonics supplier with genuine operating leverage. The arc is unusually consistent — the story did not lurch, it compounded.

**May 2025 (first quarter 2025).** The frame was "defense is now the majority of us." Chief executive Scott Keeney opened with record defense revenue at "more than 63% of total sales in the quarter, up from 49% in the same quarter a year ago." The commercial business was described as "challenging"; the guide for aerospace and defense growth in 2025 was "at least 25%." Tariffs occupied a full section of the prepared remarks — a defensive note. The single named program was HEL-TD (later renamed HELSI-2 in the transcripts), a $171 million contract to build a one-megawatt laser.

**August 2025 (second quarter 2025).** The defense guide was raised from "at least 25%" to "at least 40%" in one quarter. New elements entered: a first international directed-energy customer, and an operational sub-story — moving amplifier production "out of the hands of our R&D teams and into the hands of our manufacturing folks." That transition is the most important thing management said all year, because it is the mechanism behind the margin expansion that followed.

**November 2025 (third quarter 2025).** First hard commercial evidence beyond a single program: "a new $50 million contract for an existing long-running missile program." Analyst Greg Palm pressed the right question — whether the programs replacing HELSI-2 in 2026 were booked or merely in the pipeline. Chief financial officer Joe Corso answered flatly: "Those are programs that have been booked."

**February 2026 (fourth quarter and full year 2025).** Two structural decisions landed together. First, an equity offering raising "over $190 million after expenses," explicitly to build a 50,000 square foot plant in Longmont, Colorado and to "invest ahead of demand." Second, the decision to exit the legacy cutting and welding business entirely — a deliberate revenue amputation of roughly $25 to $30 million.

**May 2026 (first quarter 2026).** The story reaches product form. Management launched HADES, a branded family of scalable high-energy laser weapon modules, and reframed the company from component supplier to "system-level partner." Keeney also introduced a budget argument: roughly $400 million per year for directed-energy prototypes and procurement in the 2027 and 2028 federal budgets, and about $1 billion per year including science-and-technology funding.

**What was quietly dropped.** Cutting and welding, obviously — but also the tariff discussion, which dominated the May 2025 call and has not been revisited in dollar terms since. The commercial recovery narrative also disappeared: through 2025 management repeatedly told investors not to read sequential improvement as a demand turn, and by 2026 the segment is simply no longer part of the thesis.

**Where the story is richer than the trajectory justifies.** Three places. First, "pipeline" has been doing heavy narrative work for five straight quarters while the contractually funded order book shrank. Second, management stopped giving order-book updates in May 2025 — "we're not in the position, you know, today where we're gonna provide that update quarter over quarter" — which conveniently removed the one metric that would have shown the shrinkage. Third, the customer base is concentrating, not diversifying: the top ten customers were 75% of 2025 revenue, up from 72% in 2024.

**Where the story is poorer than the trajectory justifies.** The gross-margin achievement is genuinely understated in management's own telling. Total gross margin went from 22.4% in the third quarter of 2024 to 33.1% in the first quarter of 2026 — an eleven-point improvement — on revenue that grew 55%. Expanding margin on growing volume is the textbook signature of pricing power. Management describes this as "leverage" and "mix," which undersells it.

**The deterministic proxy versus the company's own figures.** The screener's margin-direction proxy reads gross margin as expanding, with a three-year improvement of about 8.8 percentage points, ending at roughly 29.8% for fiscal 2025. That agrees with what the company reported (Corso: gross margin "increased to approximately 30% in 2025, up from 17% in 2024"). The proxy is directionally right but stale — it does not yet capture the 33.1% total and 43.6% product margins printed in the first quarter of 2026. The screener's other automated fields are less usable: the discounted-cash-flow fair value of $2.50 and the uniform negative margins of safety across every valuation model are artifacts of a company with near-zero accounting earnings, not information. The screener's net-debt field is also wrong — the company holds net cash of $297 million as of 2026-03-31, not net debt.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, without a break, across all five calls.**

- May 2025: hedged and tariff-preoccupied. "Who knows what the assumption should be for where these tariffs will land." Growth guide: "at least 25%."
- August 2025: "strong execution across the board." Guide raised to "at least 40%." The amplifier transition is described as "not without risk" — a genuine, unforced admission.
- November 2025: "another solid quarter." A record 41% product gross margin. Keeney on the Washington environment: "we are seeing very good engagement across all levels."
- February 2026: "2025 was an exceptional year." Capital raised, capacity doubled, a business line euthanized.
- May 2026: "an exceptional quarter." A branded product family launched. Directed energy described as "the most strategic and highest growth opportunity for nLIGHT."

**Specificity moved in the right direction on programs and the wrong direction on the order book.** Program detail improved markedly — named power levels, named programs (HELSI-2, HELCAP, DE M-SHORAD, JLWS), named budget lines. Order-book detail went the other way, from a quarterly update to a refusal.

**Offensive versus defensive language.** Decisively offensive from August 2025 onward. The defensive vocabulary of the first quarter of 2025 — tariffs, mitigation, uncertainty, "some pause" — is entirely absent by 2026, replaced by capacity, investment ahead of demand, and opportunistic acquisitions. That is the correct posture for a company whose end market is genuinely inflecting; it is also the posture that historically precedes a disappointment, and there is no longer any margin of conservatism in the language.

**One notable moment of self-awareness.** Asked in February 2026 what worries him, Keeney answered: "arrogance is what kills companies... lasers are hard, right?" That is an unusually unpolished answer for a chief executive at a share price up several hundred percent, and it counts in his favor.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Reliability: High.**

The track record is close to unblemished over the reviewed period:

- Guided 2025 defense growth of "at least 25%" in May 2025, raised to "at least 40%" in August 2025, delivered 60%.
- Guided the second half of 2025 to be "better than the first" in May 2025 — first half revenue $113.4 million, second half $147.9 million.
- Guided product gross margin of "about 30%" at the midpoint for the second quarter of 2025 in May 2025 — delivered 38.5%.
- Guided development-work gross margin to "remain in the 8% range" repeatedly; it printed 13.1%, then 6.4%, averaging near the guide.
- First quarter 2026 adjusted earnings per share of $0.20 against a $0.08 street estimate; the shares rose about 15% the next day. The screener records a beat in six of the last seven quarters.

**Is this sandbagging?** Partly, and management does not hide it. The product gross margin guide has been set below the prior quarter's actual print in several consecutive quarters and then beaten. The guide currently outstanding for the second quarter of 2026 — revenue of $75 to $81 million and product gross margin of 37% to 41% — sits below the first quarter's $80.2 million and 43.6%. On the pattern, that is a conservative guide. But it is also the first guide in six quarters that implies sequentially flat revenue, and an investor paying fourteen times revenue is not paid to assume the sandbag repeats.

The one credibility deduction is not about the numbers but about the disclosure choice: withdrawing the order-book update in May 2025 and then describing the pipeline as "growing" for five consecutive quarters, while the funded order book in fact declined, is selective framing.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

**The order book — deflected, four calls running.** Ruben Roy of Stifel asked directly in May 2025 for "an update on funded and unfunded backlog progression." Corso: "No major update. I think we're not in the position, you know, today where we're gonna provide that update quarter over quarter." Analysts have largely stopped asking. The annual report shows why they should not have.

**The HELSI-2 hole — pressed hard, answered adequately.** Greg Palm of Craig-Hallum pushed twice in November 2025 to establish whether the replacement programs were booked or hoped for, and got a clear answer: booked, in both directed energy and sensing. This was the right question and management met it.

**Timing of new prototype awards — deflected with an honest excuse.** In May 2026 Palm asked when the promised prototype awards would land. Keeney: "I'd like to predict how Congress will work this year, but I've got enough experience to know that there are error bars around that... those that can be delayed." The company's 2027 and 2028 budget numbers are a presidential budget request, not appropriated money, and management said so.

**Capital spending on the new plant — not quantified.** Troy Jensen of Cantor Fitzgerald asked in February 2026 for the capital cost of the Colorado expansion. Corso: "No, we haven't quantified it specifically... it will be expected to be higher than where we were in fiscal 2025, but we're not talking about needing 2 or 3x." For a company that had just raised $190 million partly for that purpose, the absence of a number is a gap.

**What analysts are not asking about.** Nobody on any of the five calls asked about stock-based compensation, which at roughly 13.2% of revenue is larger than the entire adjusted profit figure management highlights. Nobody asked about the 26% growth in the diluted share count over eight quarters. And nobody asked about customer concentration at 75% of revenue in the top ten names.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Red flags**

- **The funded order book shrank while the story grew.** $161.6 million at 2025 year-end versus $167.0 million a year earlier, on revenue up 32%. That implies bookings roughly equal to revenue — an order intake ratio of about one, not the accelerating build the commentary implies. Coverage fell from about ten months of revenue to about seven.
- **Insiders sold heavily into the strength, and bought nothing.** Chief executive Scott Keeney sold roughly 102,000 shares between 2026-05-28 and 2026-06-04 at prices from $71.67 to $81.06. Chief financial officer Joe Corso sold roughly 30,000 shares between 2026-05-19 and 2026-06-04 at $68.48 to $77.99. The chief accounting officer sold as well. Across the trailing window the screener records zero open-market purchases against 75 sales totalling 717,360 shares.
- **Equity issued at the highs.** The February 2026 offering raised $190 million net. Selling stock into strength to fund capacity is defensible capital allocation; it is also the clearest statement management can make about where they think the shares are relative to value.
- **Dilution is structural, not incidental.** Diluted share count went from 47.7 million in mid-2024 to 60.0 million in the first quarter of 2026, up 26%. At roughly $38 million a year, share-based compensation exceeds the company's entire 2025 adjusted profit of $23.5 million.
- **Customer concentration rising**, top ten at 75% of 2025 revenue versus 72% in 2024, with the counterparties being primes and the government itself — a single program cancellation is material.
- **The second-quarter 2026 guide implies the margin record was a peak, at least temporarily**: product gross margin of 37% to 41% against 43.6% delivered.

**Green flags**

- **Pricing power is real and measurable.** Product gross margin from 28.8% in the third quarter of 2024 to 43.6% in the first quarter of 2026, on revenue growing 55%. Expanding margin on rising volume in a supply-constrained niche is the honest signature of a differentiated product; it is the opposite of buying share with price.
- **A genuine industrial achievement behind it.** The amplifier line moved from research staff to manufacturing staff over 2025 — the unglamorous work that converts a laboratory capability into a product.
- **Cash and no leverage.** Net cash of $297 million against $36 million of total debt at 2026-03-31, current ratio above seven. There is no financing risk in this story.
- **Management amputated a losing business.** Exiting cutting and welding cost roughly $25 to $30 million of revenue and improved the company. Few small-cap managements do this.
- **The reported profit line finally turned.** First quarter 2026 net income of $0.6 million, against a $25.0 million loss in the fourth quarter of 2024.
- **Concrete external validation since the last call.** On 2026-07-09 the company was selected for a Joint Laser Weapon System agreement with an initial award of $44 million and a program ceiling of up to $627 million.

## 7. Hidden signals

**A vocabulary shift from component to system.** Through 2025 the company described itself by its vertical integration — "from chips to full laser systems." By May 2026 the language is "increasingly engaged not only as a laser supplier, but also as a system-level partner," and the HADES launch attaches a brand to that claim. Moving up the value chain is where the margin is, and the margin data agrees. But it also puts nLIGHT into partial competition with the primes that are currently its largest customers — and on the very Joint Laser Weapon System award it just won, its co-awardee is Lockheed Martin's Aculight unit.

**"Pipeline" replaced "backlog" as the unit of account.** This substitution happened in May 2025, the same quarter the order-book update was withdrawn. A pipeline is unauditable. In a sector where promoters routinely inflate capacity and order books, the substitution of an unverifiable metric for a verifiable one is the pattern worth watching — even at a company whose delivered results have so far been excellent.

**Programme naming drifted.** The one-megawatt effort is called "HEL-TD" in the May 2025 transcript and "HELSI-2" from August 2025 onward, with the same $171 million value and the same 2026 completion date. This appears to be a transcription artefact rather than a substantive change, but it is worth noting that the completion date also slid from "2026" to "late 2026" between May 2025 and February 2026.

**A quiet clarification about what HELSI-2 actually is.** In May 2026 Keeney corrected an analyst: "it's not a delivery of a product. It's a demonstration of that technology." The largest named program in the story is a technology demonstration on a cost-plus basis, not a production contract. That is a materially different thing from an order book.

**The internal inconsistency on capacity.** In May 2026 Corso said "we are not capacity constrained today" and "what we need to continue to break through that $80 million threshold is demand signals from our customers." Three months earlier the company raised $190 million partly to double capacity. Both statements can be true across different time horizons, but read together they say the near-term constraint is demand, not supply — which is not how the equity was marketed.

**A small reporting discrepancy worth flagging.** In the May 2026 call, Keeney's prepared remarks cite defense revenue of "$55 million, which grew 69% year-over-year" while Corso says "$51.1 million." Total revenue of $80.2 million less commercial revenue of $25 million gives $55.2 million, and 69% growth on the prior-year $32.7 million gives $55.3 million. Corso's figure appears to be a transcription error, not a restatement.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict (for the director)

**Trajectory: strengthening.** Every operating measure that matters is moving the right way and has been for six consecutive quarters: revenue, gross margin, adjusted profit, reported profit, cash generation, and the mix shift toward the highest-margin end market. The management team guided conservatively and beat, repeatedly. The February 2026 equity raise and the Colorado plant are the correct decisions for a company that believes its end market is inflecting, and the cutting-and-welding exit is evidence of discipline rather than empire-building.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing.** Not the business — the price, and specifically what the price already assumes about an order book the company will not show you. At $78.44 the enterprise is valued at about 14.4 times the last twelve months of revenue. Coherent, a far larger and profitable photonics peer riding the artificial-intelligence optics cycle, trades at about 10.2 times; Novanta at 5.5 times; Kratos, the closest listed analogue for an unprofitable high-multiple defense-technology supplier, at about 6.0 times; IPG Photonics, the industrial-laser incumbent, at 3.4 times. nLIGHT is the most expensive name in its own comparison set on the metric that does not depend on adjusted definitions. Against that, the contractually funded order book is $161.6 million — about seven months of revenue, and smaller than it was a year ago — and management has declined to update it quarterly since May 2025. The director is being asked to pay a scarcity multiple for visibility that has not been demonstrated in the one number that would demonstrate it.

**Economic moat: narrow, and widening.** The moat is a combination of intangible technical assets and regulatory or qualification-based switching costs. Two decades of coherent beam-combining work, a fully domestic manufacturing base at a moment when domestic content is a procurement requirement, and qualification into long-running classified sensing programs together make nLIGHT genuinely hard to displace inside the programs it already sits on. The evidence for widening is the margin structure: product gross margin rose from 28.8% to 43.6% over six quarters while revenue grew 55%, which is pricing power, not mix luck. The reason it is narrow rather than wide is threefold — returns on capital are still negative (return on invested capital of about negative 3.7% for the trailing twelve months), the top ten customers are 75% of revenue, and on its flagship new award the company is one of two selected suppliers alongside a Lockheed Martin unit. A supplier that can be second-sourced by a prime does not own a wide moat.

**Secular threat: none material.** Directed energy is a structurally growing category, not a declining one: low cost per shot against cheap drones is a genuine and durable economic argument, and the budget direction is up. No artificial-intelligence displacement, disintermediation, or channel-shift force impairs the terminal value of a company that makes physical high-power laser hardware — if anything the same forces expand the addressable market. The risk here is cyclical and political (appropriation timing, program cancellation, a peace dividend) and valuation-driven, not terminal.

**Is the price a fear-print or a value trap?** Neither: it is the opposite problem. This is a good business at a full price, not a cheap business with a hidden flaw. Nothing in the forensic file argues the company is worth less than it earns; everything argues that the current quote already contains several years of the growth management is describing, plus the optionality on a program ceiling that has been announced but not funded.
