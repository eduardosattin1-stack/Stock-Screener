# Flowserve Corporation (FLS) — Forensic Dossier

**Prepared 2026-08-04 · Price used: $79.37 (NYSE, US dollars, intraday quote) · Transcripts covered: Q1 2025 through Q1 2026, with the Q2 2026 release (2026-07-29) verified separately from filings and the press release**

Flowserve makes the pumps, valves, mechanical seals and actuators that move fluids through
power plants, refineries, chemical works and mines. Roughly 53% of its revenue is
aftermarket — spare parts and servicing on equipment it already installed — and 47% is
original equipment. It is one of a handful of suppliers holding the nuclear quality
certifications needed to sell into a reactor, with content in more than three-quarters of
the roughly 400 reactors operating worldwide.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story management tells has changed shape twice in six quarters, and both changes were
in the right direction.

**Early 2025 — "we can defend."** The first-quarter 2025 call was dominated by tariffs.
Management quantified an annualised gross exposure of "$90 million to $100 million," walked
through mitigation levers, and reaffirmed rather than raised guidance. The emphasis was
resilience: a $2.9 billion backlog described as "another advantage in this environment,"
an aftermarket business "over 50% of sales" that "provides some insulation."

**Mid-2025 — "we can compound."** After walking away from the proposed merger with Chart
Industries in July 2025 and collecting a $266 million termination payment, the narrative
turned from defence to self-improvement. The internal operating programme — the "Flowserve
Business System," with its 80/20 complexity-reduction pillar — moved to centre stage. The
tariff estimate was cut to "$50 million to $60 million" and then declared fully mitigated.
Guidance was raised twice in 2025, and by the fourth quarter the company had hit its 2027
operating-margin target two years early.

**Late 2025 into 2026 — "we are a power and nuclear company."** The third-quarter 2025 call
introduced an entirely new growth pillar with dedicated slides: content in over 75% of the
global reactor fleet, "40 new large nuclear reactors to be under construction in the next 10
years," "as many as 30 SMRs" (small modular reactors) in five years, and roughly $100 million
of Flowserve content per large reactor — raised to "$115 million to $120 million" after the
Trillium valves acquisition. Record nuclear bookings of over $140 million landed in the third
quarter of 2025; $400 million for the full year.

**What got quietly dropped:** the original-equipment growth story in energy. Management now
frames large engineered projects as "a mid-single-digit percentage of our bookings" versus
"20-plus percent" ten years ago, and presents that shrinkage as resilience rather than as
lost revenue. It is both.

**What got added without fanfare:** a recurring-revenue digital arm. The RedRaven monitoring
product signed a memorandum of understanding with Honeywell in the second quarter of 2025 to
integrate into Honeywell's Forge platform. Note the wording — a memorandum of understanding
is a statement of intent, not a contract, and no revenue from it has been disclosed since.

## 2. CLAIMS versus FINANCIALS (forensic)

| Management claim | The numbers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "12th consecutive quarter of year-over-year margin expansion" (Q4 2025), extended to a 14th straight quarter in Q2 2026 | Adjusted gross margin 35.9% in Q2 2026, up 100 basis points. But **reported** gross margin was 32.9%, **down about 175 basis points** from 34.7% a year earlier | **Diverges.** The streak is real only on the adjusted measure |
| "Achievement of our long-term margin targets 2 years ahead of plan" | Adjusted operating margin 16.8% in Q4 2025 against a 2027 target range of 14–16% | **Confirmed.** Genuine over-delivery |
| "Growth" is the objective | Revenue: 2023 $4.32B, 2024 $4.56B, 2025 $4.73B, then Q2 2026 sales **down 1.6%** reported and **down 3.3% organic**; year-to-date sales down 4.1%; full-year 2026 organic sales guided to **about −1%** | **Diverges materially.** Margins are expanding on a shrinking revenue base |
| Bookings and backlog are accelerating | Q2 2026 bookings $1.35B, up 25.5%; backlog $3,336M, up 16.9% — but $225M of that backlog was **acquired** with Trillium. Organic backlog growth is 9%; book-to-bill 1.15x | **Confirmed with a correction.** Real acceleration, but the headline is flattered by an acquisition |
| Nuclear is a major growth driver | $400M of nuclear awards in 2025; over $110M in Q1 2026 and again in Q2 2026; first-half 2026 nuclear awards up 34%. But power is only **~7% of revenue** | **Confirmed as an order-book fact, not yet as an earnings fact** |
| Balance sheet is healthy, "about 1x net levered" | Net debt at 2026-06-30 is $1.59B including capitalised leases ($1.40B excluding them) against roughly $865M of expected 2026 adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — closer to **1.8x** after the Trillium purchase | **Diverges.** The 1x figure was accurate at end-2025 and is now stale |
| Cash generation is improving | Year-to-date 2026 operating cash flow $86.2M, **down $18.0M** year on year; free cash flow margin 8.9% on a trailing basis | **Diverges near term.** Working capital is absorbing the backlog build |

The single most important divergence: **the adjustment gap has widened**. In the second
quarter of 2025 the reported and adjusted gross margins were within 25 basis points of each
other (34.7% versus 34.9%). In the second quarter of 2026 the gap is 300 basis points
(32.9% versus 35.9%), driven by $40.7 million of realignment charges in that quarter alone
and $69.7 million year to date. The Flow Control Division shows this most starkly: a reported
operating margin of 3.1%, down 710 basis points, against an adjusted operating margin of
12.6%, up 40 basis points — a 950 basis-point gap in a single segment.

Restructuring charges of this size, recurring for three consecutive years under a programme
management describes as running "a 4- to 5-year period," are not one-off items. They are the
cost of the margin programme, and excluding them overstates the true earnings power by
roughly $0.35 to $0.45 per share annually.

## 3. TONE AND CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising, with one honest wobble.**

- *Q1 2025 — defensive.* "We are reaffirming our full-year guidance and remain focused on
  navigating the current environment." Heavy hedging language: "dynamic," "uncertainty,"
  "we will monitor the next 90 days."
- *Q2 2025 — a step up.* "We delivered exceptional second quarter earnings." Guidance raised.
  Incremental margins of 94% in the quarter — the share of each extra revenue dollar that
  reached operating profit.
- *Q3 2025 — most confident of the sequence.* "Exceptional results across bookings, margin
  expansion, earnings and cash flow," incremental margins of 115%, guidance raised a second
  time, and the nuclear franchise unveiled with a dedicated slide package.
- *Q4 2025 — assured.* "Achievement of our long-term margin targets 2 years ahead of plan."
- *Q1 2026 — the wobble, handled well.* Middle East conflict disrupted logistics and site
  access across a region employing roughly 800 Flowserve staff. Management quantified the hit
  (about $50 million of delayed bookings, later about $60 million of lost year-to-date sales,
  a three-percentage-point drag on organic sales), maintained the earnings guidance, and did
  not blame the macro for anything it had not measured. That is the behaviour of a credible
  team.

A subtle shift worth noting: the offensive language has migrated from *revenue* growth to
*margin* and *bookings* growth. Chief executive Scott Rowe's revenue framing has softened
from a target to an aspiration — "over time, a 5% growth rate feels right for me" (Q3 2025).
That is a hope, not a commitment, and revenue has gone backwards since he said it.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY — **High**

The delivery record on earnings is close to unblemished:

- 2025 guidance opened at a range implying 18–25% earnings growth, was raised to $3.25–$3.40
  in July, raised again to $3.40–$3.50 in October, and the year closed with adjusted earnings
  per share up 38%.
- The 2027 adjusted operating margin target of 14–16% was reached in the fourth quarter of
  2025, two years early — and both divisions individually cleared their 16–18% segment targets.
- The screener records a beat in 6 of the last 7 quarters (86%).
- In 2026 the low end of the earnings guide has been *raised* (to $4.05–$4.20) even as the
  business absorbed a war.

There is a mild sandbagging pattern — chief financial officer Amy Schwetz's Q1 2025 framing
of the low end as still delivering "18% earnings growth year-over-year" was setting a floor
she clearly expected to beat.

**The offsetting fact, and it matters:** organic *revenue* guidance has been cut twice in
2026, from a range of −1% to +2% down to about −1%. Management is reliable on the earnings
line and unreliable on the revenue line. An investor buying the margin story is buying the
part they deliver; an investor buying the growth story is buying the part they keep revising.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**The recurring, never-fully-answered question is the link between bookings and revenue.**
Andrew Obin of Bank of America put it directly in October 2025: "last year, bookings grew 9%.
This year, bookings are sort of flattish. So how do we think about the relationship... how
did this year's bookings translate into hitting the 5% revenue target next year?" The answer
was a discussion of commercial initiatives, not a conversion bridge. Ten months later, with
book-to-bill at 1.15x and backlog up 9% organically, revenue is *down* 3.3%. The analyst was
asking the right question and did not get an answer.

**Backlog conversion is the mechanism, and it is quietly deteriorating.** Schwetz conceded in
October 2025 that the share of backlog converting within twelve months had ticked "down to
kind of mid-80s," with "slight pressure on backlog conversion with the nuclear content."
Nuclear orders have long lead times. A book-to-bill above 1.0 driven by nuclear translates to
revenue more slowly than one driven by aftermarket. Management has not sized this.

**The Mogas acquisition under-delivered and was pressed on.** Joe Giordano of TD Cowen in July
2025: the $200 million revenue expectation "is way lower than that." Management reaffirmed the
$200 million as a goal rather than a result. This is directly relevant to the far larger
Trillium purchase now being integrated.

**What analysts are not yet pressing on, and should be:** the size and permanence of the
realignment charges, and the fact that the 80/20 programme is itself suppressing revenue —
management put the drag at "approximately 200 basis points of headwind in the first half"
of 2026.

## 6. RED FLAGS AND GREEN FLAGS

**Concerning**

- The gap between reported and adjusted gross margin widened from 25 basis points (Q2 2025)
  to 300 basis points (Q2 2026). The celebrated margin streak lives entirely inside the
  adjustment. *(Q2 2026 press release)*
- Organic revenue has now declined for multiple consecutive quarters, and the full-year
  organic guide has been cut twice. *(Q1 and Q2 2026)*
- The Flow Control Division's reported operating margin fell to 3.1% in the second quarter of
  2026, down 710 basis points. *(Q2 2026)*
- Operating cash flow year to date is down $18.0 million against last year while the backlog
  and inventory build absorb cash. *(Q2 2026)*
- Leverage rose to roughly 1.8x after the $490 million all-cash Trillium purchase, from the
  "about 1x" management cited in February 2026.
- The Honeywell arrangement for the RedRaven monitoring product is a memorandum of
  understanding, not a contract, and no revenue has been disclosed against it since it was
  announced in July 2025.
- The nuclear slide deck contains classic sector promotion — market-size projections of "40
  new large reactors" and "as many as 30 SMRs." These are industry forecasts, not orders.

**Positive**

- Management walked away from the Chart Industries merger in July 2025 because "further
  pursuing the merger would have been value diminishing to Flowserve shareholders," and
  collected $266 million for doing so. Very few management teams decline a transformative
  deal they have already sold internally.
- They then spent $255 million buying their own stock in 2025 at an average of $53 a share.
  The stock is $79.37 today. That is capital allocation done in the right direction at the
  right time — the opposite of the buy-high pattern that defines most industrial cycles.
- Legacy asbestos liabilities were divested in the third quarter of 2025, removing an
  open-ended claim from the balance sheet.
- Insiders bought 3,500 shares on the open market with zero open-market sales. Small in
  dollars, but unambiguous in direction.
- Share count fell from 132.7 million diluted (Q1 2025) to 128.4 million (Q2 2026). This is
  the opposite of the serial-dilution pattern that plagues this sector.
- Tariff exposure was cut from a $90–100 million estimate to $50–60 million and then fully
  offset, exactly as promised, with "no noticeable impact to demand."
- Backlog of $3,336 million is up 9% organically with a 1.15x book-to-bill, and record
  aftermarket bookings of $695.8 million marked an eighth consecutive quarter above $600
  million.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**The vocabulary migrated from "cycle" to "resilience."** Rowe's October 2025 framing —
engineered projects down from "20-plus percent" of bookings to "mid-single-digit" — reframes a
lost business line as a structural improvement. It is genuinely both, but the reader should
notice which half gets the airtime.

**The SMR partner list is a real disclosure hiding in plain sight.** Rowe said they have hired
a former Nuclear Regulatory Commission and White House staffer and "narrowed that list down to
kind of 10 to 12 players," adding "I don't think we'll be 100% right there. But if we're at
least 60% or 70% right... then we're in a really, really good place." That is unusually candid
handicapping from a company selling into a hyped end market — and it is the correct posture,
since most announced small-reactor developers will not build anything.

**The acquisition rationale shifted from products to certifications.** Rowe in October 2025:
the aim of programmatic acquisitions is to "acquire other companies that may have the
certifications and may have that installed base already in there where you're not having to
work through some of those barriers to entry." Flowserve is explicitly buying regulatory moat.
Trillium — with content in 115 operating reactors and an installed base above 200,000 units,
bought for $490 million at 12.3 times its 2025 adjusted earnings before interest, tax,
depreciation and amortisation — is exactly that trade.

**The 80/20 programme is a deliberate revenue shrink and management now says so.** "We reduced
SKUs by nearly 80%" at one site; a 45% product-code reduction in industrial pumps. Investors
reading the revenue line as demand weakness are partly reading a management choice. Investors
reading it as *entirely* a management choice are ignoring a real war and a soft chemical
market.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.**

This is a genuinely improving industrial franchise run by people who have repeatedly chosen
the disciplined path when the promotional one was available — declining a merger they had
already championed, buying back stock at the bottom of its range rather than the top, and
retiring an open-ended legal liability. The operating programme is delivering: adjusted
operating margin cleared the 2027 target in 2025, returns on invested capital have climbed
from roughly 6% to 8.5%, and the order book is genuinely accelerating with a 1.15x book-to-bill
and 9% organic backlog growth.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing:** *the margin expansion is being
bought with revenue, and the accounting for it is getting less clean.* Reported gross margin
fell about 175 basis points in the most recent quarter while the adjusted figure rose 100.
Organic revenue is declining. If the 80/20 pruning headwind abates in the second half as
management promises and the backlog converts, this is a compounding franchise that deserves a
peer multiple. If the pruning keeps eating more revenue than the nuclear order book adds, an
investor owns a shrinking business with an expanding adjusted margin — which is a slow
liquidation dressed as an improvement. The second half of 2026 will settle it, and the
evidence to watch is organic revenue turning positive **and** the realignment charge run-rate
falling below roughly $15 million a quarter.

**Moat: narrow but real, and widening.** The type is regulatory-licence plus switching-cost.
Nuclear quality certifications and customer approvals are slow, expensive and jurisdictional —
they are why Flowserve is "one of a few preferred vendors" for reactor flow control worldwide,
and why buying Trillium's certifications was cheaper than earning them. The 53% aftermarket
share compounds this: once a pump is bolted into a nuclear island, the spares come from the
maker. The width judgement is capped by the returns evidence rather than the qualitative story
— an 8.5% return on invested capital is only modestly above the cost of that capital, and 14
straight quarters of adjusted gross-margin gains have moved it from poor to adequate, not to
excellent. The direction, though, is unambiguously widening: rising returns, rising margins,
a growing certified installed base.

**Secular threat: manageable.** Flow control for power generation and process industry is
about as insulated from structural displacement as industrials get. Artificial intelligence
does not replace a pump; it increases demand for the electricity that pumps help generate.
The plausible long-run threat is the energy transition eroding the oil, gas and refining
share of the installed base — but management has been diversifying away from that for a
decade, power and nuclear are growing fastest, and the same transition creates the nuclear
demand. This is a cyclical and geopolitical risk, not a terminal one. The cheapness relative
to peers is a fear-print about revenue direction, not a shrinking-base value trap.
