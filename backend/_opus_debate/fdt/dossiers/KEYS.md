# Keysight Technologies (KEYS) — Forensic Dossier

*Transcripts reviewed: fiscal Q2 2025 (2025-05-20), Q3 2025 (2025-08-19), Q4 2025 (2025-11-24), Q1 2026 (2026-02-23), Q2 2026 (2026-05-19). Financials from the screener scan plus company filings through the quarter ended 30 April 2026. Price used throughout: $342.48 per share (New York Stock Exchange, US dollars, 4 August 2026).*

Keysight makes the instruments engineers use to prove a design works before it ships — oscilloscopes, signal and network analyzers, wafer-probe systems, and the software that emulates a whole data-centre network in a lab. It sells into two reporting segments: the Communications Solutions Group (wireline and wireless test plus aerospace, defence and government) and the Electronic Industrial Solutions Group (semiconductors, automotive, general electronics and design software).

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story across these five calls is one of a slow, credible build turning into an unusually violent acceleration — and the acceleration is real, not rhetorical.

**Spring 2025 — recovery, cautiously framed.** The May 2025 call is defensive in structure. Chief executive Satish Dhanasekaran opens with "the resilience of our business and the durability of our financial operating model," stresses a "flexible cost structure" and a supply chain "largely based in Southeast Asia with minimal exposure in China," and spends real time on tariffs. The Electronic Industrial segment had just returned to growth "after six quarters of decline." Orders grew 8%. The language is that of a management team defending a trough, not surfing a boom.

**Summer 2025 — the first thesis statement.** By August 2025 the frame changes. Dhanasekaran claims credit for foresight: "our early recognition of AI as a transformative technology shift led us to make strategic investments a few years ago to align our portfolio to the multiyear innovation road map we saw unfolding." This is the moment the AI data centre moves from a demand line item to the organising idea of the company. Named proof points appear alongside it — a partnership with AMD on PCI Express generation 6 compliance, the industry's first protocol-layer solution for validating 1.6-terabit performance.

**Autumn 2025 — the portfolio is bought, not just built.** The November 2025 call announces three closed acquisitions in a single quarter: Spirent, the Synopsys Optical Solutions Group, and ANSYS PowerArtist. Free cash flow hits a record $1.3 billion, and the board authorises a new $1.5 billion buyback. Wireline sets a revenue record. The strategy is explicitly restated as "software-centric."

**Winter 2026 — the crossover.** The February 2026 call contains the single most informative sentence in the set: "Wireline delivered record orders, surpassing wireless for the first time." A company built on mobile-phone and radio test has been structurally re-based on AI infrastructure. Management lays out four named demand pillars (AI infrastructure scaling, speed transitions to 1.6 and 3.2 terabit, optical interconnect and silicon photonics, system-level emulation) and has repeated them verbatim ever since — a sign of an internally settled thesis rather than a rotating one.

**Spring 2026 — the blow-off quarter.** May 2026: "the best quarter in company history." Orders above $2 billion, up 56%. Revenue up 31%. Earnings per share up 69%. Free cash flow of $472 million. The full-year revenue growth outlook is raised to "high 20s percent."

**What was quietly dropped:** tariffs, which dominated the May 2025 call, have essentially vanished from the prepared remarks. Automotive has been demoted from a growth driver to a stabilisation story ("we're not calling an inflection"). Sixth-generation wireless, once a headline, is now correctly parked at "commercialization by 2030."

**What is conspicuously absent throughout:** a backlog number, an AI revenue number, and any customer-concentration disclosure. Management describes AI-related business only in relative terms ("in the first half of fiscal 2026, our AI-related business has already surpassed the levels achieved in all of 2025"). For a company whose stock has more than doubled on the AI thesis, the refusal to size the AI base or disclose the order book is the most important thing management is not saying.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising — decisively, and in four visible steps.**

- **Q2 2025 (defensive):** "Even as we are monitoring the overall macro environment…"; "Despite the near-term uncertainty…"; the demand environment is "solid," the Electronic Industrial environment "remains mixed." Hedged throughout.
- **Q3 2025 (constructive):** "we're once again raising our outlook for the full fiscal year." The word "constrained" is retired: last year's supply chain was "constrained… and what's really played out over the last few quarters is that the unlocking of the supply chain."
- **Q4 2025 (offensive):** "outstanding fourth quarter results," "surpassing our expectations and our long-term model." A new $1.5 billion buyback authorisation.
- **Q1 2026 (assertive):** "further acceleration in demand," "this unprecedented time." Management now claims vindication for investments made three years earlier.
- **Q2 2026 (peak):** "the best quarter in company history."

The shift from efficiency language (flexible cost structure, supply chain diversification, tariff mitigation) to investment language (scaling capacity, rebuilding the acquisition funnel, higher research spending) is complete. Chief financial officer Neil Dougherty is the counterweight and stays consistently measured: he took "a pretty conservative approach in terms of how we plan for those businesses," and on the Q2 2026 call he declined to quantify full-year earnings growth at all — "I think we've given you enough information to get close."

That refusal is worth flagging. A management team confident in the second half usually quantifies it.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**High.** This is the strongest part of the file.

Every quarter in the set delivered revenue and earnings above the high end of the company's own guidance range: Q2 2025 ("both of which exceeded the high end of our guidance"), Q3 2025 (same phrase), Q4 2025 ("exceeding the high end of our guidance"), Q1 2026 ("exceeding the high end of our guidance range"), Q2 2026 (a record). The screener records a beat in seven of seven quarters. The full-year outlook was raised in Q3 2025, again implicitly in Q1 2026, and explicitly to "high 20s percent" in Q2 2026.

There is a sandbagging pattern, and it is the ordinary, benign kind: the company guides to what its short-cycle book supports and lets upside fall through. Dougherty's Q1 2026 remark that he planned the acquisitions "conservatively" is the tell.

**One caution that cuts the other way.** The Q3 2026 guide — revenue of $1.730–1.750 billion against the April quarter's $1.717 billion, but non-GAAP earnings per share of $2.43–2.49 against the April quarter's $2.87 — tells you management itself does not expect the 72.3% gross margin to repeat. Higher revenue, lower earnings, is an explicit statement that the April quarter's profitability was flattered. Investors extrapolating that margin are extrapolating something the company has already walked back.

**Reliability score: High.**

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

Analysts have circled the same three questions for five straight quarters, and management has deflected all three.

**1. How big is the AI business, actually?** Priyanka Thapa (JPMorgan, Q3 2025) asked directly for the customer-segment split of the data-centre business. The answer: "I don't know that we've split up the business quite the way you've asked us to." Andrew Spinola (UBS, Q2 2026) asked whether the research-versus-manufacturing mix had moved from 70-30; the answer was "it's in the 70-30 range… you could see that number move a bit." The best disclosure ever offered is Dhanasekaran's Q4 2025 line that "roughly half of our wireline business" is AI-affected, and wireline is "a little under half" of commercial communications. That is a chain of approximations, not a number.

**2. Is this a linear growth curve or a cycle?** Spinola put it precisely in Q2 2026: "there's this very linear growth that seems to be happening… I think things tend to evolve and customers can get more efficient." Dhanasekaran's answer was a genuinely thoughtful essay on heterogeneity — optical *and* electrical, open *and* closed, pluggable *and* integrated — that never addressed the actual question of what happens when the build-out digests. Rob Mason (Baird) asked the same thing from the recapitalisation angle in Q1 2026 and received the same treatment.

**3. What is the order book worth?** This is the most important exchange in the file. Pressed on the durability of the record orders, Dougherty volunteered in Q2 2026 that the long-lead systems orders "were not the same as the long-dated orders we were talking about a few years back… still largely within the 6-month order acceptance window of our standard product portfolio." Read plainly: the backlog is short-cycle. Two quarters of orders, not three years of contracted revenue. Management offered this candidly and unprompted, which speaks well of them — and it is the single most consequential fact for anyone underwriting this stock at a record multiple.

**The topic analysts are not pushing on, and should be:** customer concentration. Management has said the AI customer count "doubled" and the ecosystem is "broadening," but has never disclosed how much revenue the top handful of hyperscalers represents. In Q3 2025 Dhanasekaran conceded that a year earlier "this is a highly concentrated business for us because we had a few customers driving the action." Concentration that was acknowledged in 2024 and is now described only as "broadening" remains, functionally, undisclosed.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

🟢 **Gross margin expanding on 31% revenue growth (Q2 2026).** Non-GAAP gross margin of 72.3% versus 64.6% a year earlier. In a components business, growing revenue while margins compress means you bought the volume with price. The opposite happened here. This is the clearest evidence of genuine pricing power in the file.

🟢 **The strategic call was made early and is documented.** The wireline and silicon-photonics investments were described as being made "about 18 months ago" as of May 2025 — that is, in late 2023, before the AI networking wave was consensus. The payoff arrived in fiscal 2026. Management gets credit for a forecast, not a reaction.

🟢 **Named, dated customer proof points rather than vague partnerships.** AMD on PCI Express generation 6 (Q3 2025), Broadcom on 1.6-terabit networking silicon (Q4 2025) and the first public Ultra Ethernet Consortium interoperability demonstration (Q2 2026), Meta on large-scale graphics-processor validation (Q4 2025), Samsung on satellite-band connectivity (Q1 2026), Qualcomm on radio-frequency digital twins (Q2 2026), the United States Air Force on flightline testing (Q2 2026), the European Space Agency naming Keysight prime contractor on a three-year non-terrestrial-network programme (15 July 2026). These are verifiable and specific.

🟢 **Balance sheet strength.** Net debt of $347 million including capitalised leases against trailing EBITDA of $1.44 billion — leverage of about 0.24 times. Solvency score 8.1. There is no financing risk here.

🔴 **The order book is short-cycle, by management's own admission.** "Still largely within the 6-month order acceptance window" (Q2 2026). There is no multi-year contracted backlog to cushion a demand pause.

🔴 **The company has a documented air-pocket in living memory.** Fiscal 2024 revenue fell from $5.46 billion to $4.98 billion (down 8.7%) and earnings per share fell from $5.91 to $3.51 (down 41%). Dhanasekaran acknowledged it directly in Q3 2025: "the business did experience a decline in '24… it was not immune." That is what the down-leg looks like at this company, and it happened two years ago.

🔴 **Insiders are selling only.** Twenty-one open-market sales totalling 125,494 shares against zero open-market purchases. The buy ratio in the raw scan is grant-inflated and should be ignored; the open-market record is unambiguous.

🔴 **Buying back stock at the high.** $204 million repurchased in the April 2026 quarter with the shares in the $300s, after a year in which they more than doubled. The $1.5 billion authorisation was announced in November 2025 near the start of the re-rating. Management's own capital is being deployed at peak valuation — the classic top-of-cycle capital-allocation error, even at a company otherwise disciplined.

🔴 **Growth is partly purchased.** Four acquisitions in three quarters (Spirent, Synopsys Optical Solutions Group, ANSYS PowerArtist in Q4 2025 for roughly $2.019 billion; VPIphotonics closed 9 June 2026). Core order growth of 48% against reported 56% quantifies the gap. Integration expense will pressure cash conversion in fiscal 2026 by Dougherty's own admission.

🟡 **Research spending is climbing toward 19% of sales** with software assets that "typically run north of our company average." Correct strategically; a margin headwind if revenue stalls.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**The vocabulary migrated from "supply" to "demand."** In 2025 the constraint was the supply chain ("constrained… from a supply chain perspective"). By 2026 the constraint is the customer's urgency ("a stronger sense of customer urgency that manifested in stronger-than-expected bookings"). Companies at the top of a cycle talk about demand; companies at the bottom talk about cost.

**A quiet management refresh.** Investor relations changed hands (Paulenier Sims to Liz Morali, by Q1 2026), and segment presidents Kailash Narayanan (Communications) and Jason Kary (Electronic Industrial) were added to the call roster from Q4 2025. Steve Yoon, head of global sales, now speaks to the funnel directly — and his Q2 2026 contribution is the most promotional language in the entire file: "our funnel intake and our total overall pipeline remains very, very strong… funnel velocity and conversion rates are increasing." Repeated intensifiers from a sales executive, on a record quarter, on an undisclosed metric, is the one passage in these transcripts that reads as selling rather than reporting.

**Competition is discussed almost entirely in the abstract.** Across five calls there is no substantive discussion of Anritsu, Rohde & Schwarz, Viavi, Tektronix or Advantest. Asked in Q2 2025 whether the shift to semiconductor-level testing changes the competitive landscape, Dhanasekaran answered about his own capabilities and said "if we need more capabilities, we can acquire talent." Either the moat is wide enough that competition genuinely is not the constraint — the margin data supports that reading — or management is not being pressed on it.

**The quantum line is a footnote, not a business.** The only quantum-specific disclosures across five calls are the AIST 1,000-qubit platform collaboration in Japan (Q3 2025) and a passing reference to "advanced research spending in semiconductor, 6G, quantum, and photonics" (Q4 2025). Quantum control and readout electronics sit inside the advanced-research-and-education sub-line of the general electronics business, itself a slice of a segment running under $2 billion a year. Anyone underwriting Keysight as a quantum-computing exposure is underwriting a rounding error. The actual engine is artificial-intelligence data-centre test, and secondarily defence.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.** Revenue accelerating from 8% to 31%, gross margin expanding by 770 basis points year over year, record orders and record free cash flow, a balance sheet at 0.24 times leverage, and a management team that has beaten its own guidance seven quarters running while the mix shifts toward software and services (roughly 40% of revenue after the acquisitions). This is a demonstrably better business than it was two years ago.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing.** Not whether the business is good — it is — but that the entire visible order book converts inside six months, by the chief financial officer's own description, while the shares are priced as though the revenue were contracted for years. Keysight has no structural protection against an artificial-intelligence capital-spending digestion, and it has a documented precedent for exactly that outcome: earnings per share fell 41% in fiscal 2024. At $342.48, roughly 34 times the fiscal 2026 consensus of $10.17 and 29 times the fiscal 2027 consensus of $11.86, the price already pays for continuation. The margin of safety is in the business quality, not in the valuation.

**Economic moat: wide, and widening.** The moat type is a combination of switching costs and intangible technical assets. Keysight's instruments become the calibration reference inside a customer's design flow; measurements taken on a competitor's box are not directly comparable, so a change of vendor means requalifying a workflow. The company is designed in at the research stage — management's own framing, "strategically, the R&D business is more valuable to us" (Q2 2025) — which is the stickiest possible position, because the tool that validated generation N is the default for generation N+1. The evidence supports width and widening: gross margin rising from 62.1% for fiscal 2025 to 63.7% on a trailing basis and 68.6% on a GAAP basis in the latest quarter, return on equity of 20.0%, and pricing power sufficient to add 770 basis points of non-GAAP gross margin in a single year. Return on invested capital of 12.4% looks modest, but that is the arithmetic of $2 billion of freshly capitalised acquisition goodwill, not a deteriorating core.

**Secular threat: manageable.** No structural force is eroding the terminal value here. Artificial intelligence is a demand driver for Keysight rather than a displacement risk — more design complexity means more validation, and the company sells the validation. The credible long-term threats are ordinary industry threats: customers internalising test (hyperscalers designing their own validation rigs), a shift of value from instruments toward software where the competitive set is broader, and the arrival of a lower-cost Asian challenger at the high end. None of these is visible in the current numbers. **The risk in this name is cyclical amplitude, not terminal impairment** — and cyclical amplitude is not priced at 34 times forward earnings.
