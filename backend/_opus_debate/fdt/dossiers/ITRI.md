# Itron, Inc. (ITRI) — Forensic Dossier

*Prepared 4 August 2026. Five consecutive earnings calls reviewed: Q2 2025 (31 Jul 2025), Q3 2025 (30 Oct 2025), Q4 2025 (17 Feb 2026), Q1 2026 (28 Apr 2026), Q2 2026 (28 Jul 2026). Price used throughout: $104.01, the last traded price on the Nasdaq on 4 August 2026, in US dollars.*

---

## 1. Narrative arc

Itron sells the meters, radio networks and software that let electricity, gas and water utilities see and control their distribution systems. Across these five calls the story does not change direction so much as it changes **which lever management asks you to watch**, and the switch is unusually clean to trace.

**Summer 2025 — the growth story stumbles, and management pivots the scorecard to margin.** On the July 2025 call the chief executive, Tom Deitrich, cut the full-year revenue midpoint by roughly 3% and in the same breath raised the earnings midpoint by 13%. The framing was explicit: "customers and regulators face a more complex environment, leading to slower project deployments," but "growing customer demand for high-value solutions, along with operational efficiencies has raised our full year EPS outlook." That sentence is the hinge of the entire two-year arc. From that quarter forward, every call leads with a margin, profit or cash-flow record and reports revenue as a timing artefact.

**Autumn 2025 — the order book stops cooperating and the language softens.** Third-quarter bookings came in at $380 million against $582 million of revenue. Management had promised a book-to-bill ratio (new orders divided by revenue billed) of at least 1:1 for 2025 in July; by October that had become "while achieving a 1:1 book-to-bill ratio for 2025 remains possible, we anticipate current dynamics to persist this quarter, resulting in bookings below that target." The defensive vocabulary arrives here too — "lumpy bookings are a familiar pattern," "these changes are not due to competitive dynamics" — alongside a new deflection to a metric that was going up instead: opportunity pipeline, "expanded by over 25% since the start of the year."

**Winter 2025-26 — the company buys a new growth engine.** With organic revenue shrinking, Itron spent $325 million on Urbint (closed 3 November 2025) and $515 million on Locusview (closed in the first quarter of 2026), both software businesses serving utilities, and created a fourth reporting segment called Resiliency Solutions. It funded this partly with $805 million of zero-coupon convertible notes priced on 23 February 2026. The narrative expands from "we sell grid-edge intelligence" to "we support the customer across the entire asset life cycle, from planning to build-out to operations to maintenance and protection." This is the single biggest thesis expansion in the window, and it is bought, not built.

**Spring and summer 2026 — recurring revenue becomes the headline number.** By the first quarter of 2026 the lead metric is annual recurring revenue: $400 million, up 28%. By the second quarter it is $417 million, up 21%. Revenue itself fell 7% year over year to $563 million. Management's own summary of the June quarter is candid about the trade: "record gross margin, strong free cash flow and earnings well ahead of our expectations on in-line revenue. That combination is the story of the quarter."

**What was quietly dropped.** The 1:1 book-to-bill commitment, offered as a target in July 2025, is gone. Asked directly on the July 2026 call whether he would give a full-year book-to-bill figure, Deitrich answered "We don't guide to book-to-bill. It's not something that's strictly within our hands." The 2027 revenue goal of $2.6–2.8 billion has been walked down inside its own range: from "no change to the targets" (October 2025) to "probably towards the lower end" (February 2026). And the company has pre-announced that it will replace those targets entirely at an investor day — "we haven't really picked a date yet, but I would suspect sometime in the next year or so."

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Overall: stable in confidence, but with a clear migration from offence to a very well-defended position.**

- *July 2025 (defensive turn begins).* "Despite ongoing macroeconomic and trade policy uncertainties." Asked by Guggenheim's Joseph Osha whether regulator pushback reflects ratepayer anger at high retail rates, Deitrich's entire answer was a single word: "Yes." That is the most unguarded moment in the five calls.
- *October 2025 (lowest point).* "Lower-than-expected Q3 bookings and heightened uncertainty have tempered our year-end booking expectations." Also the first appearance of the pipeline-instead-of-orders substitution.
- *February 2026 (re-offensive, on acquired ground).* "Modern civilization depends on energy and water systems that cannot fail." Confidence returns, but it is attached to the two acquisitions and to the recurring-revenue line rather than to the core order book.
- *April 2026.* "Grid modernization is inevitable." The word "inevitable" enters the vocabulary and stays.
- *July 2026 (highest).* "Itron is a different company today than in prior cycles, and this quarter proved it." "Regulatory calendars shape when programs move forward, rarely whether they move at all."

Guidance has become **more** specific over time, not less, which is a genuine positive: the company now guides a quarterly revenue range and an adjusted earnings range, and it hit or beat the revenue range in each of the last four quarters. The specificity that has been withdrawn is exactly the forward-looking order metric — book-to-bill, and the 12-month backlog number, which Deitrich in July 2026 described as "up quarter-over-quarter" while adding "I don't know that I would focus in on that precise number too much."

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Score: High.**

The record within the window is good, and specific:

- Third-quarter 2025 was guided to $570–585 million in July 2025; it delivered $582 million, "near the top end of the range."
- Fourth-quarter 2025 was guided to $555–565 million in October 2025; it delivered $572 million, "higher than the range we expected."
- First-quarter 2026 delivered $587 million, "above our outlook range."
- Second-quarter 2026 delivered $563 million, "within the range."
- Full-year 2025 was reset in July 2025 to $2.35–2.40 billion; the company landed at $2.367 billion.

There is a mild but consistent sandbagging pattern on revenue: three of four quarters came in at or above the top of the guided band. On earnings the beats are larger and more systematic — the screen records seven earnings beats in seven quarters. Management has also over-delivered on its own multi-year margin plan, and said so plainly in February 2026: "gross margin, EBITDA, free cash flow for the '27 targets, we already achieved them in 2025." That claim checks out against the reported margins.

The one credibility debit is definitional rather than dishonest. The 1:1 book-to-bill commitment made in July 2025 was missed, then reframed as something outside management's control. When a metric moves from "we maintain our outlook for" to "we don't guide to" inside four quarters, the change is worth remembering.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

Three questions recur, and management answers two of them well and one of them not at all.

**The one they will not answer: when do orders inflect?** Seaport's Scott Graham has asked a version of this on every single call. October 2025: "when we can start to see bookings really inflect upward." April 2026: "Is it possible that second-half sales could be down given the TTM book-to-bill being below 0.9?" July 2026: "would you say you're kind of like over the hump on bookings?" The July 2026 answer was a refusal — "No, I really don't think it is. You're trying to draw a pattern where there just isn't one. Pay attention to backlog and not necessarily bookings quarter-to-quarter." The instruction to look at a flat number instead of a declining one is the sharpest deflection in the transcripts.

**The one that already turned into bad news.** In July 2025 Graham asked whether the near-term uncertainty changed the 2027 revenue goal; the chief financial officer, Joan Hooper, answered "No. There's no change to the targets." Seven months later the same question produced "probably towards the lower end" plus a plan to replace the targets at an investor day. Graham was right and the answer he was given was wrong.

**The one management handles credibly.** Graham's question in October 2025 about whether delivery dates inside the backlog were being pushed out drew a genuinely useful, non-evasive answer: "If you had a project that you were planning on doing the deployment over 3 years, maybe you're now profiling it over 4 years... you're really kind of taking the same area under the curve and perhaps spreading it a little bit." That is an honest description of a real problem.

**What analysts are not pressing on and should be.** Nobody on any of these five calls asked about the $830 million of net debt created by funding $840 million of acquisitions and $377 million of buybacks out of a cash pile that has fallen from $1.33 billion to $745 million in nine months. Nobody asked what Itron paid as a multiple of revenue for Locusview. And nobody asked what happens to the record gross margin *when* the Networked deployment volume management is promising for the second half actually arrives — which is the central question in the entire file.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

🔴 **The margin record and the revenue decline are the same event.** Networked Solutions revenue is down 17% year over year and its gross margin is up 4.3 percentage points. The mix that produces a 41.4% company gross margin is the mix in which the low-margin hardware has not shipped. Management guided third-quarter 2026 revenue to $590–600 million, a step up of roughly 5-7% from June — if that arrives as hardware, the margin should give some back.

🔴 **Book-to-bill below 1 for a full year, and the metric retired.** Trailing-twelve-month orders of about $2.14 billion against $2.30 billion of revenue. Backlog flat at $4.4 billion for three quarters. (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 calls.)

🔴 **Earnings are guided down in 2026.** Adjusted earnings of $6.30–$6.50 a share guided against $7.14 delivered in 2025. The drivers are lower interest income — cash has halved — and a normalised tax rate after an unusually low one.

🔴 **The balance sheet has flipped and the screen does not know it.** Roughly $830 million net debt as of 30 June 2026 versus the $598 million of net cash our input file asserts. Tangible book value is negative. Goodwill and intangibles are 49% of assets.

🔴 **Insider selling is one-directional.** Zero open-market purchases against 57 sales totalling 138,005 shares, including through a period when the shares traded in the low $80s.

🔴 **A named input-cost risk, volunteered by management.** "We are proactively managing pockets of tightness, memory pricing in particular" (Q2 2026). Itron was burned by exactly this mechanism in 2022-23 through fixed-price pre-inflation backlog, and a large part of the current margin gain is the roll-off of that damage.

🟢 **The margin improvement is at least half structural and independently datable.** A closed electricity meter factory, a pruned product portfolio, and the confirmed roll-off of pre-inflation contracts. Device Solutions gross margin went from 29.8% to 34.8% and operating margin from 22.6% to 28.3% year over year — that is not deployment-mix noise.

🟢 **Recurring revenue is compounding, and it is measurable.** Annual recurring revenue of $417 million, up 21% (Q2 2026). Distributed-intelligence endpoints deployed rose from 14.4 million (Q1 2025) past 16 million (Q3 2025); licensed applications reached "close to 28 million," up more than 50% year over year (Q2 2026). Outcomes and Resiliency together are now 25% of backlog and growing faster than the whole.

🟢 **Cash generation is exceptional and capital-light.** $383 million of trailing free cash flow on $25 million of capital spending. Free cash flow set a company record in four of the five quarters reviewed.

🟢 **Buybacks were executed into weakness, not strength.** $100 million in the December 2025 quarter, $100 million in the March 2026 quarter and $125 million alongside the February 2026 convertible offering — all with the shares between roughly $85 and $95 — then only $52 million in the June quarter as the price recovered. That is price-sensitive, not mechanical.

🟢 **No refinancing wall.** Both convertible tranches — $805 million due 2030 at 1.375% and $805 million due 2032 at zero coupon — mature well beyond any forecast horizon, and both carry conversion prices ($131.24 and $123.77) above today's $104.01.

🟢 **Named, dated customer wins keep landing.** Exelon multi-year multi-application extension (Q4 2025); Duquesne Light grid-visibility programme (Q1 2026); Los Angeles Department of Water and Power expansion, Sacramento Municipal Utility District Riva expansion, and a mid-market Platform-as-a-Service cohort (Q2 2026); Watercare New Zealand's 100,000-meter upgrade (15 June 2026); Hunter Water Australia (26 May 2026).

## 7. Hidden signals

**The vocabulary migrated from orders to inevitability.** Across five calls the load-bearing noun changes from "bookings" to "pipeline" to "backlog" to "structural" and finally to "inevitable" (used in both April and July 2026). Each substitution moves one step further from a number a shareholder can check.

**"Book and ship" is being reframed from a weakness into a feature.** In July 2026 Deitrich disclosed that Itron used to enter a quarter with "80%, 85% of the shipments that are planned for the quarter in backlog," and that the share arriving as within-quarter turns business is now higher, particularly in Networked Solutions. He presented this as customers buying targeted capability rather than whole-territory upgrades. It is also, mechanically, less forward visibility — and it explains why he now steers analysts away from the 12-month backlog figure he used to volunteer.

**The remaining-performance-obligation question was answered honestly and it was not flattering.** Asked in April 2026 how backlog differs from remaining performance obligations, Hooper explained that the reported gross backlog includes contracts carrying termination-for-convenience clauses, often mandated by regulators, which the accounting definition strips out. Her defence — "nobody ever cancels" — is supported by history but is a behavioural claim, not a contractual one. A meaningful share of the $4.4 billion is cancellable at the customer's option.

**Capital allocation changed character without being flagged as a change.** Itron spent the 2021-24 period fixing its own operations. In the last nine months it has spent $840 million buying two software companies with, in the chief executive's own words, "tens of customers each" — against Itron's 8,000. The stated logic is distribution: push acquired products through an existing sales force. That is a credible plan and also a high-multiple bet, and it is being funded by turning a net-cash balance sheet into a net-debt one.

**Management pre-committed to resetting its own scorecard.** "It's appropriate to reset long-term targets with an Investor Day" (February 2026). Announcing that you will replace your targets before you have met them is an orderly way to retire them.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING** — but strengthening in profitability and business quality while the revenue base is flat to shrinking. This is a genuine operational turnaround (gross margin up 8.6 points in three years, free cash flow up sharply, earnings quality clean) layered on top of a demand line that is not yet cooperating.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing.** The record 41.4% gross margin and the missing revenue are two readings of one fact: the low-margin network hardware has not shipped. Management has guided the third quarter of 2026 to $590–600 million, a step up of 5-7% from June, and has said the second half depends on "an uptick in the rate of Network deployments." You cannot underwrite both the volume recovery *and* the peak margin. Whichever one you are paying for, decide which, because the current share price appears to be paying for both — and it does so on a balance sheet that carries roughly $830 million of net debt, not the $598 million of net cash our screen shows.

**Economic moat: NARROW.** The moat type is switching costs backed by an installed base. A utility that has deployed Itron's Gen5 Riva mesh network across a service territory cannot swap the network without a multi-year, regulator-approved forklift replacement; the 16-million-plus deployed distributed-intelligence endpoints and the roughly 28 million licensed applications running on them are the anchor, and the 2026 win described as helping "a large early-adopter AMI customer" bridge an aging system to next-generation technology is the moat visibly paying out. It is narrow rather than wide because the competitive set is deep-pocketed — Landis+Gyr, Honeywell, Hubbell, Xylem, Badger Meter — and because the buyer is a regulated monopoly under explicit political pressure on affordability, which caps price. Deitrich's own answer to the question of whether public utility commissions are pushing back because retail rates are already very high was, simply, "Yes" (July 2025).

**Moat trend: STABLE.** The evidence cuts both ways and roughly cancels. In favour of widening: gross margin up 8.6 points in three years, segment operating margins at records across all four segments, recurring revenue compounding above 20%, and management's assertion that share is being taken "both where Itron is the incumbent and in head-to-head competition" (July 2026). Against: return on invested capital is only 7.6% and is being diluted by $840 million of goodwill-heavy acquisitions, revenue has declined for six consecutive quarters, and the order book has run below replacement for a year. Rising margins on a shrinking base with falling returns on capital is not a widening moat; it is a better-run business of the same width.

**Secular threat: MANAGEABLE.** The structural forces in this chain point toward Itron, not away from it: electrification, rising and more volatile electricity demand including data-centre load, distributed energy resources, and a fleet of meters and grid equipment aging out. The genuine terminal risks are political rather than technological — sustained regulator refusal to approve rate cases on affordability grounds would defer, not destroy, this demand, and open-standards pressure could over time commoditise the network layer that today carries a 42.8% gross margin. Neither is impairing the terminal value on the current evidence.

**Is the cheapness a mispricing or a trap?** Neither, cleanly. On our reconstructed enterprise value of roughly $5.4 billion — market value of $4.55 billion plus $830 million of net debt — Itron trades near 14 times adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, against Xylem at about 16, Badger Meter at about 20 and Hubbell at about 21. That discount is real but it is *earned* by negative organic growth, orders below replacement, and a balance sheet with negative tangible book value. It is not a fear-print on a stable base, and it is not a melting base either. It is a fairly priced good business whose next twelve months rest on one unproven step-up in deployment volume.
