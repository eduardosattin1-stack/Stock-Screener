# Emerson Electric Co. (EMR) — Forensic Dossier

**Prepared 4 August 2026. Price used throughout: $154.15 (New York Stock Exchange, US dollars), the live quote at midday on 4 August 2026.**

Transcripts reviewed: five consecutive quarterly calls — 7 May 2025 (fiscal Q2 2025), 6 August 2025 (fiscal Q3 2025), 5 November 2025 (fiscal Q4 and full-year 2025), 3 February 2026 (fiscal Q1 2026) and 5 May 2026 (fiscal Q2 2026). Emerson's fiscal year ends 30 September, so "fiscal 2026" covers October 2025 through September 2026. The company reports fiscal Q3 2026 **after the close today**, 4 August 2026, with the call at 4:30 p.m. Eastern (company announcement dated 14 July 2026).

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story management tells has changed shape three times across these five calls, and the direction of travel is consistent: from *"we are finishing a transformation"* to *"we are a compounder with a numbered plan."*

**May 2025 — the transformation is declared finished.** The opening framing is portfolio surgery. Emerson had spent four years selling its heating-and-cooling business (Copeland), buying National Instruments, and buying out the minority shareholders of AspenTech, the industrial-software firm. On this call the chief executive says plainly: "We're also marking the completion of the portfolio transformation we began in 2021." The buy-in of AspenTech closed 12 March 2025. A strategic review of the Safety and Productivity unit ended with a decision to keep it. The dominant risk topic is tariffs — a gross exposure of $245 million for the year, which management says it will fully offset.

**August 2025 — the story pivots to product.** Tariff exposure is cut nearly in half (the annualized figure drops from about $455 million to about $210 million; the in-year figure from $245 million to $130 million), and the airtime shifts to three named launches: an industrial data platform built with TotalEnergies, an artificial-intelligence adviser embedded in the Ovation power-plant control system, and a test-software assistant called Nigel. Ovation orders are up 40% in the quarter. This is the first call where power generation, rather than oil and gas, is the lead vertical.

**November 2025 — the story becomes a numbered framework.** The full-year call previews an investor conference held 20 November 2025. Named customer wins arrive in volume: five Entergy power plants totalling about 3.1 gigawatts, the Doel nuclear station in Belgium, the Woodside Louisiana liquefied-natural-gas export terminal with Bechtel, and three life-sciences plants in Indianapolis. Management discloses that its Ovation system controls roughly 30% of all power generated in the world, and that a complete new nuclear reactor is worth about $40 million of Emerson content plus a similar amount again over a ten-year service annuity.

**February 2026 — the framework hardens into 2028 targets.** The company commits publicly to $21 billion of revenue, a 30% adjusted segment margin, $8.00 of adjusted earnings per share and a 20% free-cash-flow margin by fiscal 2028, plus $10 billion of cumulative cash returned to shareholders. Orders grow 9%, the fastest of the five quarters. Ovation orders are up 74%. A new theme appears — the United States industrial policy is described as aligning with "five specific sectors that just happen to be our growth verticals."

**May 2026 — an external shock enters, and the growth number is cut.** The Middle East conflict and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz cost a full point of underlying sales in the quarter, and management reduces full-year underlying growth guidance from about 4% to about 3%. Notably, the earnings guide is *raised* at the bottom and the midpoint even as the sales guide is cut. Management also pre-empts a question nobody had yet asked loudly, addressing "concerns in the broader software market regarding AI" head-on.

**What was quietly dropped.** The "energy transition" and "sustainability and decarbonization" vertical, prominent in May 2025, is gone by 2026 — first softened in August 2025 ("we've seen some moves in sustainability and decarbonization projects in the funnel"), then absent from the five growth verticals named in May 2026 (power, liquefied natural gas, life sciences, semiconductors, and aerospace and defence). Factory automation, the classic short-cycle barometer, has been on a "watch" list for six straight quarters without ever inflecting.

**Two numbers in the screener file that must not be relied on.** The 27.8% return on equity is a stale artifact of fiscal 2023, when the Copeland sale produced $13.2 billion of net income; the current trailing figure is 12.1%. And the model-derived fair values on the stock page ($36.40 from discounted cash flow, $71.37 from the owner-earnings method) are broken here: Emerson's reported earnings are suppressed by roughly $1.5 billion a year of purchase-accounting amortisation, which those models treat as a real economic cost. Valuing this company by its parts is the only honest instrument.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising, then steady-confident with one honest wobble.**

- *May 2025 — hedged.* "We, like all companies, are operating in a period of unusual volatility and therefore, have factored a variety of outcomes into our thinking." Guidance is given as ranges; tariffs dominate.
- *August 2025 — firmer.* "We are pleased to see the turn in our discrete end markets." The tariff number halves. Specific launches replace general reassurance.
- *November 2025 — celebratory.* A 135-year company history opens the call: "our company completed 2025 stronger than ever. We have transformed."
- *February 2026 — peak confidence.* "I am surrounded by the best management team in industrial tech." Hard multi-year targets are put on the record — the single clearest signal of confidence a management team can give, because it creates a scorecard they can be graded against.
- *May 2026 — confident but candid.* Management leads with a shock it could have buried: the Middle East cost a point of sales, sales guidance is cut, and the detail given is unusually granular (47 customer sites damaged, field engineers at under 50% of normal in March recovering to 80%, customer plants at about 75% capacity).

Specificity has increased steadily, not decreased. That is the opposite of the pattern you see in a deteriorating business, where guidance grows vaguer as visibility fades. There is no drift from offensive to defensive language.

There is mild sandbagging on earnings; there is genuine over-optimism on volume.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**Where management gives a straight answer.** Tariffs (quantified every quarter and revised down honestly), the Middle East (volunteered before being asked), the Ovation power franchise (specific customers, specific megawatts), and nuclear content per reactor (about $40 million plus a similar service annuity).

**Where the answer thins out.**

1. *How much of the backlog is actually liquefied natural gas?* Andrew Buscaglia (BNP Paribas) had to ask twice in November 2025 before getting "about $350 million" of a $7.4 billion backlog. The narrative weight given to that vertical is far heavier than its near-term order weight.
2. *When does artificial intelligence become revenue?* Asked directly in May 2026, the chief operating officer answered: "it's a little early for it to translate into meaningful revenue opportunities... we don't have meaningful impact on revenue as we sit here today." That is honest, and it means the artificial-intelligence framing that has helped the shares is not yet in the numbers.
3. *Free cash flow came in light.* Alexander Virgo (Evercore) pressed on this in May 2026. The answer — interest, tax timing, working-capital build — is plausible but leaves the entire year resting on the back half.
4. *Factory automation in Germany and China.* Flagged as a watch item in every single call for six quarters, never resolved. Management now simply plans both Europe and China flat and says of Europe: "We haven't seen a catalyst to indicate that those will turn."
5. *Order push-outs.* Nigel Coe (Wolfe Research) raised the possibility in August 2025 and was firmly corrected — "I did not talk about order pushouts, Nigel." The correction was fair; subsequent order growth supports management.

**What analysts are not pressing on, and should be.** Nobody on these calls asks why return on invested capital sits near 7.7% after $15 billion of acquisitions, or whether the goodwill carried from National Instruments and AspenTech is supportable if either business stalls. That is the single largest unexamined risk in the file.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

🔴 **Gross margin has stopped expanding.** Down 30–40 basis points year over year in both fiscal-2026 quarters reported so far, on positive revenue. In a supply-constrained niche, widening gross margin on rising revenue is proof of pricing power; a flat-to-narrowing one on rising revenue is volume bought at the market price.

🔴 **The project funnel is flat at about $11.2 billion across five quarters**, and awards run at a steady $350–450 million a quarter. Presented as evidence of an accelerating capital cycle; it is actually a stable run-rate.

🔴 **Returns on capital do not justify the acquisitions yet.** 7.7% return on invested capital, 64% of assets in intangibles, negative tangible book value of $6.8 billion, and net debt of $12.27 billion against $5.14 billion of trailing profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (2.39 times).

🔴 **The balance sheet runs on short-term paper.** Working capital is negative $1.4 billion and the current ratio is 0.87. The AspenTech buy-in was funded with roughly $5.1 billion of short-term borrowing in the March 2025 quarter, which is still being termed out.

🔴 **Insiders are selling only.** Zero open-market purchases against twelve sales totalling 84,275 shares. Not damning on its own, but there is no insider conviction behind the recent share-price move.

🔴 **The Middle East drag is under-provisioned against what has actually happened.** In May 2026 management sized the hit at one point of full-year sales, with customer sites running at about 75% capacity "due to their inability to move product out of the Strait of Hormuz." That waterway has been effectively closed since 28 February 2026 and, as of 3 August 2026, remains closed — day 156 — with traffic at roughly ten ships a day against a normal 88. The entire April-to-June quarter that reports tonight sits inside that closure.

🟢 **Orders have exceeded sales for five straight quarters** (4%, 4%, 6%, 9%, 5% against 2%, 3%, 4%, 2%, 0.5%), and backlog is up 9% year over year. This is the most reliable forward indicator an equipment maker produces, and it is unambiguously positive.

🟢 **The power franchise is real and named.** Ovation controls roughly 30% of global power generation, over 50% in the United States. Orders in that business grew 40%, 18%, 74% and 41% across four quarters. Named wins: five Entergy plants (about 3.1 gigawatts), the Doel nuclear station in Belgium, Oncor's grid modernisation in Texas. These are contracts with counterparties, not memoranda of understanding.

🟢 **Maintenance and repair work is 62–65% of sales**, drawn from a $155 billion installed base. This is the recurring annuity that makes the business defensive, and it held up even through the Middle East disruption.

🟢 **Recurring software revenue is compounding.** Annual contract value reached $1.64 billion, up 9–10% for several years running, with the grid-management suite up 25–31%.

🟢 **Guidance is delivered.** Seven earnings beats in seven quarters, and every cost-synergy target announced has been reported as executed.

🟢 **Tariff exposure was managed down honestly**, from $245 million to $130 million in-year, with the reduction explained rather than claimed.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**The vocabulary migrated from "transformation" to "value creation framework."** Through 2025 the language was about what the company had *become*. From November 2025 it is about what the company will *deliver by 2028*. Committing to $21 billion of revenue and $8.00 of earnings creates a public scorecard — a confident act, and also a trap if the top line keeps under-delivering.

**"Growth verticals" narrowed and hardened.** From a loose list including sustainability and decarbonisation, to a fixed five: power, liquefied natural gas, life sciences, semiconductors, and aerospace and defence. The chief operating officer was explicit in May 2026 that growth is *not* broadening: "We haven't seen tremendous broadening beyond that."

**A political dependency was made explicit.** In February 2026 the chief executive tied the order surge directly to government policy: the administration's industrial policy "is benefiting five specific sectors that just happen to be our growth verticals." That is a candid attribution — and it means a policy reversal is a thesis risk that management has itself identified.

**A speculative narrative appeared and should be discounted.** The February 2026 call spent real airtime on a possible Venezuela rebuild — a $1 billion installed base, channel partners "still intact." Management was appropriately cautious ("we're ways away"), but the fact that it reached the prepared discussion at all suggests the near-term opportunity set is being stretched.

**New voices, same message.** The investor-relations lead changed between November 2025 and February 2026 (Colleen Mettler to Doug Ashby), and a new director (Jennifer Newstead, Apple's general counsel) joins in August 2026. Neither changed the operating narrative. The February 2026 transcript garbles which executive is speaking in several places, which is a transcription artifact rather than a signal.

**The artificial-intelligence framing runs ahead of the artificial-intelligence revenue.** Five product launches were announced across these calls (Ovation Virtual Adviser, Nigel, Guardian, AspenTech subsurface intelligence, DeltaV v16), plus a data-centre platform announced 3 August 2026. Management's own answer in May 2026 is that none of it is material to revenue yet.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.** Orders lead sales, backlog is up 9%, margins are at records, the recurring software base compounds near 10%, guidance is met, and the balance sheet — while levered — is comfortably serviced. This is a franchise operating well.

**The single thing to weigh before sizing.** *The quality is not in question; the entry price is.* The shares have moved from $136.58 on 20 July 2026 to $154.15 today — about 13% in eleven trading days — on a JPMorgan upgrade to Overweight dated 17 July 2026 with a $157 price target, into an earnings report that lands tonight. That leaves the stock roughly 76% of the way up its 52-week range and about 11% above its 200-day average, with a beat already expected (consensus $1.68 of earnings on $4.79 billion of revenue). The read-across is available in real time: Rockwell Automation reported this morning, beat on both lines, raised full-year guidance — and fell 7%. When a good print is already the price, a good print is not enough.

**The economic moat: WIDE, and holding.** The moat type is **switching costs**, reinforced by a **regulatory licence** effect in the most valuable niches. A distributed control system is the nervous system of a refinery, a power plant or a drug factory; ripping one out means re-qualifying the plant and, in life sciences and nuclear, re-validating it with regulators. That is why 62–65% of sales are maintenance work off a $155 billion installed base, why Ovation controls about 30% of world power generation, and why Emerson content sits in roughly 90% of the world's nuclear reactors. It is also why annual contract value grows near 10% with almost no churn discussion on any call.

**Moat trend: STABLE, not widening.** The evidence forbids calling it widening. Return on invested capital of 7.7% is low and has been diluted, not improved, by acquisition. Gross margin — the cleanest test of pricing power — has stopped expanding and is running 30–40 basis points below year-ago levels. Adjusted segment margin still rises, but on cost synergies, which are finite. Return on tangible assets of 16.3% confirms the underlying business is genuinely good; the group return is low because roughly $15 billion was paid for two of the parts.

**Secular threat: MANAGEABLE.** The obvious candidate is artificial-intelligence displacement of industrial software. It does not hold up here. Emerson's software controls physical processes in real time under regulatory traceability requirements — management's own framing in May 2026, that "being right 99.9% of the time is not good enough," is the correct description of why a probabilistic model does not substitute for a deterministic control system. The realistic path is that artificial intelligence becomes a premium tier *on top of* the installed control system, which is exactly what the Ovation and Nigel launches are. The genuine structural risks are different and slower: China's move toward domestic automation suppliers (China has been negative for six quarters), a reversal of United States industrial policy that management itself named as the order driver, and the goodwill risk if National Instruments or AspenTech stall.

**Mispricing or value trap: neither, at this price.** The earnings base is not shrinking, so this is not a value trap. But the shares are not printing fear either — they are within 7% of a 52-week high after a two-week run. The discount to Rockwell Automation (19.2 times profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation versus 28.3 times) is largely *earned* by a 16-point gap in return on invested capital and a 5-point gap in growth. What is on offer here is a fine business at a fair price, not a cheap one.
