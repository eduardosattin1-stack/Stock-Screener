# TE Connectivity plc (TEL) — Forensic Interrogation

**Prepared 2026-08-04.** Transcripts reviewed: fiscal Q3 2025 (2025-07-23), Q4 2025 (2025-10-29), Q1 2026 (2026-01-21), Q2 2026 (2026-04-22), Q3 2026 (2026-07-22). Share price used throughout: **$215.87 (US dollars, NYSE, 2026-08-04)**.

---

## 1. Narrative arc

Five quarters ago TE Connectivity told a story about a portfolio that had been *repositioned*. Today it tells a story about a company sitting in front of a spending wave. That is a real escalation, and — unusually — the numbers escalated with it rather than behind it.

The July-2025 framing was defensive-adjacent: management kept reminding listeners that its wins came from "the strategic positioning of our portfolio and the investments that we've made," and it spent as much time on regional auto softness as on growth. Artificial-intelligence revenue was a proof point, not a pillar: "AI revenues last year was $300 million. We now expect our revenue from artificial intelligence applications to be above $800 million in this fiscal 2025 year."

By October 2025 the pillar had arrived. The company reported over $900 million of AI revenue, "tripling our AI sales versus the prior year," and Industrial Solutions grew 34% in the quarter. By January 2026, after a November Investor Day, the language changed again — management started describing a *model* rather than a set of wins: co-creation engineering with customers, a localized supply chain, and a through-cycle growth target of "6 to 8 points of annual average growth" that they immediately said they would exceed.

The July-2026 call is the widest claim yet: "We are at the intersection of the largest technology and infrastructure investment cycle that's taking place around the world." Note the word *cycle*. In the same paragraph management credits "both secular growth trends as well as positive cyclical market inflections." That is a quiet piece of honesty — they are telling you part of this is a cycle, and cycles turn.

**What was added.** Energy was re-framed from a grid-hardening niche into a second AI business: "Our AI opportunity extends beyond the rack. Data center build-outs are driving significant investment across the power infrastructure." Defense went from a routine line item to a named inflection ("defense is growing faster than commercial air right now, which is a little bit different than you would've thought about TE maybe 10 years ago"). Optical connectivity entered the story in April 2026 via the RAM Photonics acquisition.

**What was quietly de-emphasized.** Electric-vehicle content, which used to be the transportation headline, has been folded into a broader "content growth" story balanced across data connectivity, e-mobility and electronification. That is not evasion — it is a defensible response to slower Western EV adoption — but a reader who bought TE Connectivity in 2023 for electric-vehicle content should notice that the driver has been substituted, not delivered.

**Where the story is richer than the trajectory justifies.** One place only, and it is at the gross-profit line. Gross margin was 37.2% in the December-2025 quarter, 36.8% in March-2026, and 35.6% in June-2026 — down about 1.6 percentage points in two quarters while revenue grew. Management's own explanation from the October-2025 call covers it: "in certain pieces of the business that are passing on a little bit more tariff pricing, that tariff pricing and the revenue that comes from that does not include any margin behind it." The same mechanic now applies to copper, which reached roughly $14,000 per tonne on the London Metal Exchange on 4 August 2026, an all-time high. Pass-through revenue with no profit attached mathematically dilutes the gross margin percentage. The operating margin still expanded, so this is dilution, not deterioration — but anyone reading "expanding margins" as "expanding gross margins" is reading it wrong right now.

**The one number that contradicts a house metric.** Our deterministic screen labels the gross-margin trajectory as *expanding*, with a three-year gain of about 3 percentage points. Against the company's own reported figures that is correct on a multi-year view (roughly 34.0% in the September-2024 quarter to 35.6% in the June-2026 quarter) and correct year over year (35.3% to 35.6%), but it **disagrees with the last two quarters**, which compressed. Both facts are true and both belong in the file.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, steeply, and with more specificity rather than less.**

- **July 2025** — confident but hedged with regional caveats: "The global auto market remains uneven by region." Guidance was given to the quarter only.
- **October 2025** — the first unqualified superlative: "quarterly and full year records for sales, earnings and free cash flow in what continues to be an uneven macro environment." Still paired with the macro hedge.
- **January 2026** — the tone becomes structural. Management commits to a numeric through-cycle target and then immediately exceeds it: "we outlined a long-term through-cycle target of 6 to 8 points of annual average growth. With the momentum that we're seeing, we expect to deliver growth in fiscal 2026 that is ahead of this target."
- **April 2026** — a hard forward number, volunteered: "Last year, we delivered $1.4 billion of growth as a company. And this year, we expect to deliver well over $2 billion of growth."
- **July 2026** — that number was raised again to "more than $2.5 billion of incremental revenue," and the macro hedge is gone entirely from the opening remarks.

The movement is decisively from defensive to offensive. In 2025 the recurring words were *localization*, *resilience*, *uneven*. In 2026 they are *momentum*, *record*, *backlog*, *inflection*. Critically, the confidence is attached to falsifiable numbers — order levels, backlog, a dollar figure for incremental revenue — not to adjectives. That is the kind of confidence that can be checked, and so far it has checked out.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Four for four, on both lines, with a consistent and deliberate sandbag.**

| Guided on | For the quarter | Sales guided | Sales delivered | Adjusted EPS guided | Adjusted EPS delivered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-07-23 | Q4 FY25 | $4.55B | $4.75B | ~$2.27 | $2.44 |
| 2025-10-29 | Q1 FY26 | $4.5B | $4.67B | ~$2.53 | $2.72 |
| 2026-01-21 | Q2 FY26 | $4.7B | $4.74B | ~$2.65 | $2.73 |
| 2026-04-22 | Q3 FY26 | $5.0B | $5.16B | ~$2.83 | $2.94 |

Every quarter beat on both revenue and adjusted earnings per share, and the earnings beat has been remarkably stable at roughly 8 to 19 cents. This is a textbook sandbagging pattern — management guides to a number it is confident of clearing and clears it. That is a virtue for a director's purposes (the guide is a floor, not a coin flip), but it means the fourth-quarter guide of approximately $5.25 billion and $3.05 should be read as a floor of roughly $3.05 to $3.15, and the market almost certainly reads it that way too. Do not underwrite the beat as if it were free upside.

**Reliability: High.** One qualification. Beginning in fiscal 2026 the company changed its non-standard earnings definition to exclude the amortization of acquired intangible assets, which mechanically raises reported adjusted earnings per share. To their credit they recast fiscal 2024 and 2025 for comparability, filed the recast in a regulatory 8-K filing the same morning, and flagged it in the opening remarks — and standard accounting earnings per share in the June-2026 quarter still grew 19% to $2.55. The change is disclosed and does not hide anything, but a definition change made at the start of the best growth year in a decade is worth naming.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

**Incremental margins — pushed every single quarter, and management's answer held.** William Stein (October 2025): "margins are a little bit disappointing from a [modelling] perspective. Is there something dragging on profitability today?" Shreyas Patil returned to it in three separate calls. Mitts never moved off the "30% flow-through" anchor, and by July 2026 the answer was simply arithmetic: 36% in the quarter. This is the strongest credibility marker in the file — an analyst-contested, falsifiable, repeated commitment that was ultimately delivered.

**Book-to-bill of 0.96 — deflected in October 2025, and the deflection was vindicated.** Asiya Merchant calculated it and asked whether investors should focus on it. Curtin redirected to seasonality: "you have factories that shut down around holidays in the western part of the world... it's almost like we have 1 less week of business." Book-to-bill then printed 1.1, 1.12 and 1.1 in the three quarters that followed. The deflection was correct.

**The optical gap — pushed for three consecutive quarters, and here the story got *poorer*, not richer.** In April 2026 Colin Langan asked directly what TE loses if a customer switches from copper to fibre. Curtin's answer was rhetorical: "it's not going to be an or, it's going to be an end between copper and fiber... And that word, while it's a simple word... the end is the most important." Three months later, under pressure from Shreyas Patil, the answer became concrete and considerably less comfortable: "we are not a major player in scale-out. When you think about scale-out, which is almost all optical today, we have a limited position there."

That is the single most important admission in five calls. It is honest, and it should be credited as honesty — but it confirms that the fastest-growing architecture layer in AI networking is one where TE Connectivity is not a leader, and that the RAM Photonics acquisition is a foothold rather than a position. The offsetting disclosure, also new in July 2026, materially softens it: "probably one-third of what we do in AI is power connectivity, whereas two-thirds is data." Power connectivity is indifferent to whether the signal travels on copper or glass.

**Market share against the leader — acknowledged without dispute.** Shreyas Patil (October 2025): "One of your competitors appears to be quite a bit ahead on revenue, maybe 3x the revenues that you're doing... do you see TEL as sort of a firm #2 over time?" Curtin did not contest the three-times figure and answered on technology inflections instead. TE Connectivity is the number-two player in AI interconnect and management has effectively conceded it.

**What analysts are *not* pressing on, and should be.** Nobody on any of these five calls asked what return the company is earning on nearly $4 billion of acquisitions completed or announced in two years, or why it is buying assets at a higher valuation than its own shares carry. That is the gap in the questioning, and section 6 prices it.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

### Concerning

🔴 **Buying growth at a higher price than its own stock.** Richards Manufacturing cost $2.3 billion in fiscal 2025 for roughly $400 million of annual sales — about 5.8 times revenue, or roughly 16 times operating cash earnings on the mid-30% margin management guided to. On 22 July 2026 the company signed for Astrodyne TDI at approximately $1.4 billion for "more than $250 million" of annual sales — about 5.6 times revenue, on the same arithmetic. That is nearly $4 billion deployed in two years at roughly 16 to 17 times operating cash earnings, while TE Connectivity's own shares change hands at about 14 times. Buying other people's assets for more than the market charges for your own is only value-creating if the synergies are real; the burden of proof sits with management, and it has not been quantified on any call.

🔴 **The market voted against the latest deal, hard.** Shares fell 6.9% on 22 July 2026 — the day of a beat on both lines, a raised outlook, record orders and a record backlog. The selling was attributed to the Astrodyne announcement rather than the results. When a stock falls seven percent on unambiguously good news, the disagreement is about capital allocation, not about demand.

🔴 **Copper is a cost, not a tailwind — and it is at an all-time high.** Copper reached roughly $14,000 per tonne on 4 August 2026. For a connector manufacturer this is pure input inflation. Management is candid about feeling it (January 2026: "as the spot market goes up, we feel that... yes, we're feeling it right now") and claims fast pass-through, which the industry appears to be executing — reports point to a further round of connector price increases from Amphenol, Molex and TE effective July 2026. But pass-through revenue carries no profit, which is precisely why the gross margin has compressed 1.6 points in two quarters.

🔴 **A structural gap in optical scale-out, now admitted** (see section 5).

🔴 **Demand concentration in a single customer class.** Curtin, October 2025: "the bigger driver of growth for us is the hyperscalers that have their custom TPUs and GPUs." Digital Data Networks orders are up over 70% year-to-date and the energy business is now explicitly levered to the same data-centre build-out. More of this company depends on one spending decision by a handful of buyers than the two-segment reporting structure suggests.

🔴 **Insider open-market behaviour gives no support.** Zero open-market purchases against 37 sales totalling 115,317 shares. This is ordinary for a large company where executives are paid in stock, and it is not evidence of anything wrong — but it is also not the insider buying you would want to see if the shares were as mispriced as the relative valuation implies.

🔴 **The non-standard earnings definition changed at the start of the best year** (see section 4).

### Positive

🟢 **Four consecutive quarters beating both guided lines**, with a stable and modest beat size — the mark of a management team that controls its own forecast.

🟢 **Orders up 27% year over year to a record $5.7 billion**, book-to-bill of 1.1 both in-quarter and year-to-date, and a record backlog that management explicitly ties to fiscal 2027 visibility. This is the single most durable fact in the file: order intake, unlike revenue, is a leading indicator, and it has risen for five straight quarters across every business and every region.

🟢 **The Industrial margin repair is extraordinary and real.** Adjusted operating margin in Industrial Solutions expanded by roughly 400 basis points (June-2025 quarter), 300 basis points (September-2025), 500 basis points (December-2025), reaching 22.8% by June 2026. This is not mix flattery — it survived through a period of heavy acquisition integration.

🟢 **Transportation delivered 21.0% adjusted operating margin into a flat-to-declining global auto market.** Global auto production is guided at roughly 88 million units, "down slightly," with Western regions declining. Holding above 20% margins in that environment is genuine operating execution and is the least-appreciated fact in this file.

🟢 **The AI ramp beat its own public target early.** From $300 million (fiscal 2024) to over $900 million (fiscal 2025) to a $3 billion annualized run rate by mid-2026, ahead of the Investor Day schedule.

🟢 **Cash returns are disciplined and consistent.** Roughly 100% of free cash flow returned to shareholders in fiscal 2026 year-to-date, a 10% dividend increase approved in the March-2026 quarter, and diluted share count down from 305 million to 293 million over seven quarters.

🟢 **Localization turned a tariff threat into an advantage.** Over 70% of production is localized in the region of sale, which management describes as "a differentiator with our customers" — and the revenue and order data across five quarters support that rather than contradict it.

## 7. Hidden signals

**The word "cycle" appeared, and it was management who added it.** For four quarters everything was a "secular growth trend." In July 2026 the framing became "both secular growth trends as well as positive cyclical market inflections." A management team that inserts the word *cyclical* into its own bull case while orders are at a record is not spinning — it is inoculating. Read it as a real signal about how they see the durability of the current run rate.

**Energy was quietly reclassified as an AI business.** The July-2026 line — "Our AI opportunity extends beyond the rack. Data center build-outs are driving significant investment across the power infrastructure" — widens the addressable market and is fair. It also means the two fastest-growing businesses in the company now share one demand driver. Diversification on the segment chart, concentration in reality.

**The competitive statement got noticeably harder.** On 800-volt data-centre architectures Curtin said something he has not said before: "I don't see new entrants coming in because this is a simultaneous equation of trade-offs and constraints between the data and the power equation when you're at the rack. Our customers really like somebody that understands both the power chain and the signal chain." That is a specific, mechanism-based claim about why the moat holds — far more useful than a generic technology-leadership assertion, and it is testable.

**The chief financial officer's constant.** Mitts has repeated "roughly 30% flow-through" on every call in this file, in almost identical words, under repeated challenge. Executives who keep a single falsifiable number stable across five quarters of changing conditions are usually running the business to it. He has now beaten it.

**The disclosure that most changes the risk picture** is the one-third power / two-thirds data split of AI revenue, volunteered in July 2026. It converts an all-or-nothing copper-versus-optical debate into a question about two-thirds of a business — and even there management argues the copper addressable market keeps growing because power density inside the rack keeps rising.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.**

Every operating measure that matters is moving the right way and has been for five quarters: revenue, orders, backlog, book-to-bill, segment operating margins in both segments, free cash flow, share count. Management guides conservatively and beats consistently. The confidence is expressed in numbers, and the numbers arrive. There is no forensic red flag in the accounting, no working-capital games (the cash conversion cycle is stable near 90 days), no leverage problem (net debt under one times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization), and no evasion under repeated analyst pressure — including on the one question where the honest answer was unflattering.

**The single most important thing the director must weigh before sizing.** Not the business — the price and what it embeds. At $215.87 the shares carry about 14 times enterprise value to trailing operating cash earnings and roughly 19 times this fiscal year's adjusted earnings, a wide discount to Amphenol at about 24 times and Hubbell at about 21 times on the same basis. That discount exists because just over half of revenue has been automotive, which the market values at half the multiple. But the mix crossed over in the June-2026 quarter: Industrial Solutions and Transportation Solutions each printed exactly $2.58 billion of sales for the first time, and Industrial is growing at three times the rate of Transportation. The entire investment case is whether the market re-rates the mix as it shifts — and against that, the buyer is underwriting hyperscaler capital spending, at a record-high copper price, in a stock the peer group has already bid.

### Economic moat

**Type: switching costs reinforced by scale.** Connectors are qualified into a customer's architecture years before volume production; TE Connectivity's own description is a "co-creation engineering model" in which its engineers sit inside the customer's design. Displacing an incumbent connector means requalifying the platform. Layered on top is manufacturing scale — over 70% localized production — which is a genuine cost and service advantage that smaller competitors cannot fund.

**Width: WIDE. Trend: WIDENING.** The evidence is in the returns, not the rhetoric. Adjusted operating margin rose from 20.0% to 22.0% year over year while revenue grew 14%; Industrial Solutions margins expanded 300 to 500 basis points in four consecutive quarters; return on invested capital is about 13.6%, return on equity about 23.2%, and return on tangible assets about 18.2% — all rising, not falling. Expanding profitability on rapidly growing volume in a supply-constrained niche is the definition of pricing power, and the July-2026 industry-wide price increases moving in lockstep across the three largest suppliers is oligopoly behaviour, not commodity behaviour. The one qualification, stated plainly: gross margin has compressed 1.6 points over the last two quarters on zero-margin copper pass-through. That is an input-cost artifact rather than share loss, and the operating line proves it — but if gross margin keeps falling while copper stabilizes, this judgement changes.

**Secular threat: manageable — but name it precisely.** The credible structural threat is the migration of AI interconnect from copper to optical. If scale-up networking inside the rack goes optical the way scale-out already has, TE Connectivity's largest growth engine is displaced by a technology in which management has just admitted "we have a limited position." Three things keep this at manageable rather than material. First, one-third of AI revenue is power connectivity, which is technology-agnostic and growing as rack voltages rise toward 800 volts. Second, management's copper addressable-market argument is mechanically sound — higher power density means more copper inside the rack even as data moves to glass. Third, they have bought a foothold (RAM Photonics, April 2026) rather than ignored the problem. The rest of the portfolio — automotive content, grid hardening, defence — faces no analogous displacement; if anything the energy transition and defence rearmament are structural tailwinds.

**Is the cheapness a mispricing or a trap?** A mispricing, not a trap. The base is growing at 15% with a record backlog and expanding returns; a value trap requires a shrinking base. The discount is a mix discount on a mix that is actively changing. The honest caution is that a mispricing you have to pay 19 times forward earnings for is a modest one, and the copper and hyperscaler-capex exposures are live.
