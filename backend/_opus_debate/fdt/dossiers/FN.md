# Fabrinet (FN) — Forensic Dossier

**Prepared 2026-08-04. Price used: $538.80 (NYSE, US dollars, intraday quote via Financial Modeling Prep). The shares are up about 18% on the day, from a prior close of $455.94, on a Reuters report that the Federal Communications Commission is drafting an import ban on new Chinese optical transceivers. The whole optical complex moved with it: Coherent +16%, Lumentum +10%, Celestica +9%, Applied Optoelectronics +17%.**

Transcripts reviewed: five consecutive quarterly calls, 2025-05-05 (fiscal Q3 2025) through 2026-05-04 (fiscal Q3 2026). Financials cross-checked against the company's filed income statements, cash-flow statements and balance sheet through the quarter ended 2026-03-27.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story Fabrinet told a year ago and the story it tells today are the same story, only louder — and that consistency is itself the most important forensic finding.

In May 2025 the company was managing a problem: datacom revenue (transceivers for inside the data centre) was falling 18% year on year because a large customer was transitioning products, and telecom was covering the hole. Management's framing was patient — "the steepest part of the 1.6T ramp is yet to come" — and the new items on the board were an Amazon Web Services manufacturing agreement and the start of construction on Building 10, a two-million-square-foot factory in Thailand.

By August 2025 the tone shifted from covering a hole to catching a wave: fiscal 2025 closed at $3.4 billion of revenue, up 19%, and management said it had "clear visibility toward reaching $1 billion in quarterly revenue" and was studying whether to pull Building 10 forward.

By November 2025 a genuinely new growth line appeared — high-performance computing, meaning assembly work for accelerated-computing infrastructure — which contributed $15 million in its first quarter. By February 2026 that line was $86 million; by May 2026 it was roughly $111 million by our estimate from the disclosed growth rates. Over three quarters an entirely new business went from nothing to an annualised run rate near $450 million.

The arithmetic of the arc: quarterly revenue went $872M (Mar-2025) → $910M → $978M → $1,133M → $1,214M (Mar-2026). Year-on-year growth went 19% → 21% → 22% → 36% → 39%, with the June-2026 quarter guided to roughly 40%. Growth is not merely high; it is accelerating, which is the rarer and harder thing.

Nothing was quietly dropped. Automotive was flagged as decelerating in May 2025 ("sequential growth may moderate") and it did decelerate, exactly as described, for four straight quarters. Two disclosures were retired in August 2025 — revenue by data rate and silicon-photonics revenue — with a stated reason (the categories had stopped being informative as the mix moved), and both were replaced with *more* granular disclosure, not less: data-centre interconnect revenue and high-performance computing became their own reported lines. Companies that are hiding something reduce disclosure. This one traded two stale buckets for two live ones.

The single genuinely new strategic thread is co-packaged optics. In May 2026 management disclosed a minority investment in Raytec Semiconductor, a Taiwanese wafer-level packaging specialist. Read that as the company buying insurance against the technology that could eventually make its largest product category — the pluggable transceiver — obsolete.

**Where the narrative is richer than the numbers — and this is the central finding of this dossier: the gross margin does not move.**

Fabrinet's adjusted gross margin (revenue less the direct cost of making the product, as a percentage of revenue) was 12.0% in the March-2025 quarter and 12.1% in the March-2026 quarter, having passed through 12.5%, 12.3% and 12.4% in between. Over three fiscal years the change is minus 23 basis points — statistically flat. On reported figures the March-2026 quarter was 11.6%, the lowest of the nine quarters we measured.

That is the lie detector for this kind of business, and it gives a clear answer. Revenue grew 39% and the margin on each dollar of revenue did not improve. All of the profit improvement — operating margin from 10.2% to roughly 10.9% on the adjusted basis — came from holding operating expenses near 1.4% of sales while the top line ran away. That is superb cost control. It is not pricing power.

The consequence for how this company should be valued is significant. Fabrinet is not capturing the scarcity rent in the artificial-intelligence optical chain; it is earning a fixed and unchanging fee on a rapidly growing pass-through. The rent is being captured upstream by the component and design owners. Anyone paying an optical-component multiple for Fabrinet is paying for an economic position the company does not hold.

**Where the numbers are worse than the narrative: cash.**

Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow (operating cash flow less capital spending) is $46 million on $4.235 billion of revenue — a margin of 1.1%. Free cash flow was *negative* in two of the last three quarters: minus $5M in December-2025 and minus $11M in March-2026. Over the first nine months of fiscal 2026 working capital consumed $202M, as receivables and inventory built ahead of collections.

This is not distress — it is the arithmetic of 40% growth in a business that must buy materials before it gets paid, and the $941M of net cash absorbs it comfortably. But it means the enterprise value sits at more than 400 times trailing free cash flow, and any bull case resting on cash generation is resting on a forecast, not on a fact.

**Where the deterministic screen disagrees with the company.** Our scan carries revenue growth of +29.8%, which is the fiscal-year annual figure and materially understates the current run rate: the company reported +39% in the March-2026 quarter and guided to roughly +40% for June-2026. The screen's gross-margin read ("flat", minus 23 basis points over three years) agrees precisely with the company's own reported margins. The screen's net-debt field is wrong and the live correction is right: net cash of $941M.

## 3. TONE AND CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising, steeply, and — importantly — the specificity rose with it.** Over-confident managements get vaguer as they get louder. This one got more concrete.

- May 2025 — hedged and defensive on the datacom hole: "we continue to see near-term softness due to a product transition at a large customer."
- August 2025 — the first forward commitment: "clear visibility toward reaching $1 billion in quarterly revenue."
- November 2025 — "an exceptional start"; capacity pulled forward; a new reporting line created for a business worth $15 million.
- February 2026 — "the fastest year-over-year growth we have achieved since our IPO over fifteen years ago."
- May 2026 — the most striking line of the five calls: "demand during the quarter far exceeded what we were able to ship, meaning our reported revenue does not fully reflect the true momentum of the business."

That last claim is the one to watch. It is unfalsifiable — a company can always say it would have sold more. Management refused to quantify it in August 2025 ("we would be up fairly significantly were it not for that issue… we're not going to quantify it") and refused again in May 2026. It is the single sentence in these transcripts that a promoter would also say.

Two things restrain the concern. First, the constraint is corroborated externally: Lumentum's chief executive publicly warned on 2026-07-28 that indium phosphide — the semiconductor material behind the lasers Fabrinet needs — is the next shortage and will be "bigger than memory." Second, Fabrinet is loss-making on the claim: it costs them revenue to say it. Promoters manufacture demand; they do not manufacture reasons they under-shipped.

The offensive-to-defensive tilt runs entirely the other way over these five quarters. There is no cost-cutting language, no macro hedging beyond tariffs, and no efficiency programme. Every capital sentence is about adding capacity.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY — High

Guidance was beaten in every one of the five quarters reviewed, and the beats were on both revenue and earnings. There is no evidence of the classic sandbag pattern (guide low, beat by a whisker, repeat) because the beats came alongside *rising* guidance: the June-2026 quarter is guided to $1.250–1.290 billion of revenue and $3.72–3.87 of adjusted earnings per share, roughly 40% growth at the midpoint, after the company had already grown 39%.

Two deliberate restraints stand out. Management guides one quarter at a time and has refused, three times when pressed by analysts, to put a number on the full year — most explicitly in November 2025: "I wouldn't want to put a number on what growth would be for the full year because we don't guide for a full year."

And in February 2026, on the new high-performance computing line, the chief executive volunteered the discipline himself: "Looks like a nice straight line, really, we only have two data points, two quarters of revenue, and everyone knows, two data points is not a trend." A management team talking down the extrapolation of its own best number is behaving well.

Reliability: **High.**

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

Four questions recur and are consistently deflected.

**Sizing the component shortage.** Asked in August 2025 and again in May 2026; refused both times. This is the most material non-answer, because the shortage now caps reported revenue and the size of the cap determines how much of the 39% growth is demand and how much is supply.

**Naming or sizing customers.** The Amazon Web Services agreement has never been quantified: "the commercial arrangements between us and Amazon prohibit us from discussing any of those details" (May 2025). The two direct-to-hyperscaler datacom programmes announced in May 2026 name no customer. The high-performance computing customer is described only as one whose "accelerated computing infrastructure" Fabrinet supports. This is normal contract-manufacturing confidentiality — but it means the largest new growth lines cannot be independently verified.

**How durable is the growth.** Analysts asked directly in November 2025 and May 2026 whether 34-39% is a baseline; management declined to extend beyond the next quarter each time.

**The topic analysts are *not* pressing hard enough:** customer concentration. Disclosed for fiscal 2025: NVIDIA 28% of revenue, Cisco 18%, top ten customers 86%. The fastest-growing line — high-performance computing — appears from the description to sit with the largest customer. Concentration is therefore increasing, not diversifying, and that risk received almost no airtime across five calls.

## 6. RED FLAGS AND GREEN FLAGS

**Red flags**

- *Customer concentration is rising into the growth* (fiscal 2025 disclosure, August 2025 call). Nearly half of revenue sits with two customers, and the newest, fastest line concentrates further. A single design-in loss at NVIDIA or a decision to bring assembly in-house is the one event that breaks the thesis outright.
- *Free cash flow near zero while the story is at its loudest* (December-2025 and March-2026 quarters, both negative). Working capital consumed $202M in nine months.
- *The unquantified under-shipping claim* (May 2026). Corroborated externally, but still the only unfalsifiable growth assertion in the file.
- *Insider selling with no offsetting purchases.* Open-market activity shows zero buys against 49 sales totalling 179,012 shares. Executives at a company whose shares ran from $272 to $749 inside twelve months bought none of it in the open market.
- *The estimated downside from an unfilled Building 10 tripled in one quarter*, from "probably 15 basis points" (February 2026) to "about 50 basis points" (May 2026), as the project grew. The direction of that revision matters more than the size.

**Green flags**

- *The buyback stopped as the price rose, and this is the best single piece of evidence in the file.* Fabrinet repurchased $103M in the first half of fiscal 2025 at an average of $214 a share, and $126M across that year. Then: 970 shares at $276 in the September-2025 quarter ($268 thousand), roughly 12,000 shares at $387 in the December-2025 quarter ($5M), and $151 thousand in the March-2026 quarter. With $945M of cash and $169M still authorised, management bought aggressively at $214 and essentially stopped above $280. That is textbook capital discipline — and it is also a valuation signal, because the best-informed buyer in the market declined to buy its own shares at $387. They are $539 today.
- *Capacity is added from cash, never from debt, and with the downside stated out loud.* Building 10 costs $130–132M for two million square feet supporting roughly $3.0 billion of incremental revenue; management says about six months of that factory's operating profit at full rate repays the entire building. The company carried this discipline through the last cycle, continuing to build through the 2022–2024 datacom air pocket without ever levering.
- *Disclosure expanded voluntarily* as the mix changed — data-centre interconnect from August 2025, high-performance computing from November 2025.
- *Tariff exposure is structurally contained*: goods ship on terms under which the customer bears the duty, and much of the output never enters the United States directly. Management has said in three consecutive calls it has seen no material tariff impact, and the filings show no margin break consistent with one.
- *Management talks down its own best number* (February 2026, on high-performance computing).

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**"We are a services company. We will never have our own products, and we will never compete with our customers."** (May 2026.) This is the most economically loaded sentence in the transcripts. It is a moat statement and a ceiling statement at once. Because Fabrinet will never own a design, its customers can hand it their most valuable work without fear — which is exactly why it wins share. And because it will never own a design, it can never earn design margins. That sentence and the flat 12% gross margin are the same fact expressed two ways.

**A title change without a comment.** Seamus Grady appears as "Chief Executive Officer" through August 2025 and as "Chairman and Chief Executive Officer" from November 2025 onward. The combination of chairman and chief executive weakens board independence, and it passed without a word on the call.

**The competitive language sharpened.** In May 2025 the company was politely soliciting outsourcing from Coherent and Lumentum ("it would be premature for me to comment on the progress"). By May 2026 it is "mostly share gain from some competitors" and "we do not mind who we are making transceivers for." The posture moved from supplicant to incumbent.

**Capacity language moved from a plan to a queue.** Building 10 (2.0 million square feet), then a 250,000-square-foot pull-in six to eight months early, then an extra floor by September, then 120,000 square feet converted at Pinehurst, then a newly bought 100,000-square-foot building and eight-acre site at Navanakorn, then land banked for two more million-square-foot factories. This is not a single announced project restated each quarter — each call adds a distinct, physically located increment. That is the pattern of a company chasing demand, not of a company inflating a capacity headline.

**Co-packaged optics is quietly the most important strategic sentence in the file.** Co-packaged optics moves the optical engine onto the switch package and, if it wins, shrinks the pluggable-transceiver market that is Fabrinet's largest category. The Raytec Semiconductor minority investment (May 2026) says management sees it and is buying a seat rather than defending the old format.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT

**Trajectory: strengthening.** Growth is accelerating, guidance is being beaten every quarter, operating margin is expanding, the balance sheet is net cash, capacity is being added out of cash flow with the downside quantified in advance, and the buyback was correctly switched off as the price rose. This is one of the cleanest operating records we have examined in this lane.

**The single thing the director must weigh before sizing: what you are being asked to pay for a fixed fee.** Fabrinet earns roughly twelve cents of gross profit on every dollar it handles, and that number has not moved in three years of the most explosive demand in its history. The company converts artificial-intelligence demand into volume, and volume into operating leverage, brilliantly. It does not convert scarcity into price. At $538.80 the shares carry about 37 times trailing earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation and 4.5 times trailing sales — the most expensive valuation in its contract-manufacturing peer group (Celestica 28.5 and 2.8 times, Flex 26.4 and 1.7, Jabil 18.2 and 1.2, Sanmina 17.6 and 0.9) — after an 18% single-session move driven by a *draft* regulation that the reporting source explicitly says "could still be modified or shelved." The quality is not the question. The price is.

**Economic moat: narrow but genuine, of the switching-cost and scale-cost type.** The switching cost is real and specific: a customer that qualifies a production line at Fabrinet cannot move it without re-qualifying, which costs months of a product cycle it does not have. The scale-cost advantage is the Thai footprint — 3.7 million square feet today, another 2 million arriving, operating expenses at 1.4% of sales, and a tax rate in the mid-single digits. The evidence caps the width honestly, though: gross margin is flat, not widening, which says the moat protects the *volume*, not the *price*. Returns on capital are high and rising because the denominator turns faster, not because the margin expands.

**Moat trend: stable.** Operating margin widened from 10.2% to about 10.9% over the year, entirely through operating-expense leverage, on an unchanged gross margin. That is a stable moat being levered, not a widening one.

**Secular threat: manageable, and named.** Two structural forces could impair terminal value. Co-packaged optics could shrink the pluggable-transceiver format; management has seen it and taken an equity position in a wafer-level packaging partner. Vertical integration by the largest customer — 28% of revenue — is the sharper risk, because the capability Fabrinet sells is precision assembly, which a determined hyperscaler or chip designer can build. Neither is a melting ice cube today; both are reasons the terminal multiple should be a manufacturer's, not an optical designer's. The cheapness question does not arise here in any case: the shares are not cheap, so this is neither a fear-print nor a value trap — it is a high-quality compounder trading at, or slightly through, a fair price.
