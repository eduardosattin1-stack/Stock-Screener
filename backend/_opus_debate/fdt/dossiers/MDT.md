# Medtronic plc (MDT) — Forensic Dossier

*Prepared 4 August 2026. Price used throughout: $85.99 per share on the New York Stock
Exchange, in US dollars, quoted 4 August 2026. Transcripts covered: the fourth quarter of
fiscal 2025 (21 May 2025) through the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (3 June 2026) — five
consecutive calls. Medtronic's fiscal year ends in late April, so "fiscal 2026" is the year
that closed on 24 April 2026.*

---

## 1. Narrative arc

The story management told across these five calls moved in one direction, and it moved
early. In May 2025 the pitch was **durability**: "we've proven to you that our growth is
durable, as we've now delivered mid-single-digit revenue growth for 2.5 years." Mid-single
digits was the ceiling being defended, not a floor being escaped.

By August 2025 the language changed to **anticipation**: "we're on the cusp of an
acceleration in our financial results and our strategy." By November 2025 it became
**confirmation**: "that acceleration is indeed underway." By February 2026: "Q3 marks the
highest revenue growth Medtronic has achieved in 10 quarters." And by June 2026 the claim
was retrospective and specific: full-year revenue of $36.4 billion, up 8.4% as reported and
5.8% organic (organic strips out currency moves and acquisitions), "marking our strongest
top line performance in 10 years."

That is an unusually clean four-step arc — promise, then evidence, then delivery — and it is
the single most credible thing in this file.

**What was introduced.** The company consolidated a sprawling product story into four named
"generational growth drivers," a framing that first appears fully formed in February 2026:
the pulsed-field ablation franchise for atrial fibrillation, Symplicity Spyral for high blood
pressure, Altaviva for urge urinary incontinence, and the Hugo surgical robot. A fifth,
Stealth AXiS (a combined navigation-and-robotics platform for spine surgery), was added in
the same call. Each is claimed to be capable of "well over $1 billion of revenue."

**What was quietly dropped.** Diabetes. In May 2025 management announced it would separate
the business and spent real airtime defending the logic. By June 2026 the segment had been
listed on Nasdaq as MiniMed Group and the chief executive's framing had hardened into a
capital-allocation dismissal: "it's just a structurally lower-profitable segment. And so it's
kind of hard to allocate capital that way." A business that was inside the "ecosystem"
strategy in 2024 was outside it by 2026. That is a genuine strategic reversal, and management
never quite acknowledged it as one.

**What contracted.** Nothing was withdrawn, but the *pace* language on Symplicity slid.
In May 2025: revenue would "meaningfully ramp when it's covered" by Medicare. In November
2025, with coverage in hand: "we have not incorporated much Symplicity revenue into our back
half guidance." By June 2026 the actual number arrived — the procedure is "annualizing at
$100 million." On a $36.4 billion revenue base that is roughly a quarter of one percent. The
opportunity is described in the same breath as covering "18 million people" with uncontrolled
blood pressure. The gap between the addressable-market language and the realized revenue is
the widest in this file.

| Balance-sheet strength | implied | Net debt of $18.74B on the correct definition (total borrowings $27.96B plus $60M of capitalised leases, less $9.22B of cash and short-term investments) against $9.72B of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — **1.9 times** | **Holds, and is better than the screener's own input claimed.** See the note below |

**A data correction the director must carry.** Two of the deterministic inputs feeding this
review are wrong in ways that matter.

First, the leverage figure. The screener's input file carries net-debt-to-earnings of 2.68
times. That number comes from a data-vendor convention that nets borrowings only against
*cash* ($1.95B) and ignores Medtronic's $7.27B of short-term investments. Netting against
both, as the company's own balance sheet invites, gives 1.9 times. Medtronic is meaningfully
less levered than the input implies.

Second, the gross-margin series. The input labels its margin array "recent to old" and lists
65.67% first. The audited income statements say 65.67% is fiscal 2023 and 65.02% is fiscal
2026 — the array is ordered oldest-first, not newest-first. The three-year change of minus
0.65 percentage points is correct; the direction label "flat" understates a slow, real
compression.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, and the rise is documented rather than asserted.**

- *May 2025* — measured, with a defensive tell. Tariffs get their own segment of the call:
  "you can see from the significant amount that we've already been able to offset that we are
  extremely focused on mitigating actions." The chief executive's confidence is personal
  rather than numerical: "the fundamentals of the company are the best I've seen in my nearly
  14 years here."
- *August 2025* — forward-leaning but hedged. Neuroscience growth is "a bit below trend,"
  pelvic health had "some short-term impact." The word "cusp" does a lot of work.
- *November 2025* — the pivot quarter. Both revenue and earnings beat, guidance raised on
  both lines, and the framing turns declarative: "we shifted to a growth mindset."
- *February 2026* — peak confidence. Ten-quarter high in growth, four named billion-dollar
  drivers, and a claim of underappreciation: Stealth AXiS is "meaningfully underappreciated...
  maybe in the investment community."
- *June 2026* — confident and, importantly, more numerate. The soft language of prior quarters
  is replaced by counts: 200 physicians across 300-plus accounts on the Symplicity finder;
  1,400-plus Touch Surgery installations, up more than 30% sequentially; US ablation installed
  base up 40% sequentially; active Altaviva implanters up threefold, patients treated up 2.5
  times.

**Offensive versus defensive.** The shift is clearly toward offence — "we're really shifting
our stance, moving into more of an offensive footing here" (February 2026) — and it is backed
by spending: research and development up high single digits, direct-to-consumer advertising
funded, "hiring a ton of mappers," and re-accelerated acquisitions. The defensive vocabulary
that remains is entirely about tariffs and currency, not about demand.

**Specificity is improving, with one hole.** Management became more specific on almost
everything except robotics. Asked directly in August 2025 for "any sort of metrics on revenue
robots placed," the answer was qualitative: "over 30 countries," "tens of thousands of
procedures," "significant double-digit growth in our current accounts." Ten months later, in
June 2026, the metric offered was still a ratio without a base: "our worldwide procedure
volume growth is 2x to 3x the market." Not one Hugo placement count has been disclosed across
five calls.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**High.** This is the strongest section of the file.

- The fiscal 2026 earnings guide was **raised in August 2025** ("pleased to be able to raise
  our EPS guidance for the full year"), **raised again on both revenue and earnings in
  November 2025**, and **delivered above the raised bar** in June 2026 at $5.53 of adjusted
  earnings per share, described as "ahead of expectations."
- Within-year revenue guidance was equally reliable. In November 2025 the chief financial
  officer laid out the arithmetic in public — "five and a half on the full year, we were at
  5.2 at the end of the first half. We're guiding at 5.5 in the third quarter. You can do the
  math for what fourth quarter looks like" — and the year finished at 5.8% organic, at or
  above that path.
- Product-level forecasts also landed. In May 2025 management forecast that ablation growth
  would "accelerate again next quarter"; it went from roughly 30% to nearly 50% to 71% to 78%
  across the following four quarters. Blood-pressure coverage was promised "on or before
  October 11" and later "on or before October 8"; the final Medicare decision arrived in
  late October 2025, three weeks before the November call.

**Is there sandbagging?** Yes, and it is disclosed rather than hidden. The fiscal 2027 guide
issued on 3 June 2026 — organic revenue growth of 6.75% to 7.25% and adjusted earnings per
share of $5.90 to $6.00 — absorbs a **$250 million tariff cost with no refunds assumed**,
even though the company has applied for them ("we haven't incorporated any potential upside
from reimbursements that could occur, which we have applied for"), and it consolidates a full
year of MiniMed earnings despite an expected separation. Both are conservative by
construction. That is a guide built with room in it.

**The one place the guide is not conservative** is the growth step itself: 6.75% to 7.25%
organic against 5.8% delivered. Management is asking to be believed on roughly a full
percentage point of acceleration, and the load-bearing contributors — ablation, blood-pressure
denervation, incontinence, robotics — are all early-stage ramps rather than installed
franchises.

**Reliability score: High.**

## 5. Analyst pressure points

**Robotics unit economics — deflected five times running.** Every call contained a robotics
question; none produced a placement count, a system price, a utilisation figure, or a revenue
number. June 2026's answer to a different product question states the policy outright: "we
try not to guide at a product level." For a company positioning Hugo as one of four
generational drivers, that is a conspicuous asymmetry — ablation gets share points, growth
rates and installed-base percentages; robotics gets adjectives.

**Blood-pressure denervation revenue — pushed on repeatedly, answered slowly.** Analysts
asked for "actual numbers to date even directionally" in February 2026. The answer was website
traffic: visits rose from about 50,000 to 2.5 million quarter over quarter. Website visits are
a lead-generation metric, not revenue. The revenue figure finally appeared in June 2026 at
roughly $100 million annualised. An analyst who took the November 2025 framing of a
"multi-billion dollar opportunity" at face value waited seven months for a number two orders
of magnitude smaller.

**The separation logic — challenged directly and answered candidly.** In June 2026 JPMorgan's
analyst asked the uncomfortable question: if consensus expects MiniMed to deliver strong
growth and improving margins, "wouldn't you want to keep a business with strong improvements?"
Management did not dodge. The answer conceded that the growth gap had closed — "the gap
between MiniMed and the rest of Medtronic isn't as much as it used to be" — and rested the
case on margin structure instead. Honest, and it reframes the separation as a margin-mix
decision rather than a value-creation one.

**What analysts are not yet pressing on.** Nobody in these five calls asked why adjusted
operating margin fell 130 basis points in a year of record revenue growth, or why gross margin
has drifted down across three years. The investment spending is treated as self-evidently good.
It may well be — but the question that decides whether fiscal 2027 earnings guidance holds is
whether that spending stops growing faster than revenue, and it went unasked.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Red flags**

- *Profitability moved the wrong way while the story improved.* Adjusted operating margin
  fell 130 basis points for fiscal 2026 and 230 basis points in the fourth quarter alone
  (June 2026). Gross margin has eroded about 0.65 percentage points over three years on a 16%
  larger revenue base. A company with genuine pricing power in a supply-constrained niche
  expands gross margin as it grows; Medtronic has grown volumes and absorbed cost.
- *Returns on capital remain low for a franchise of this quality.* Return on invested capital
  ran 6.4% in fiscal 2022, troughed at 4.7% in fiscal 2023, and has recovered only to 6.1% in
  fiscal 2026. Return on equity is 9.7%. Goodwill and intangibles are 56.7% of total assets and
  tangible book value is negative $2.66 billion. This is a serial acquirer whose deals have
  historically not cleared their cost of capital.
- *And acquisitions are re-accelerating.* The chief executive said in May 2025 he wanted to
  "turn up the heat on some tuck-in M&A." He has: CathWorks closed in the fourth quarter of
  fiscal 2026, Scientia Vascular for $550 million on 12 June 2026, SPR Therapeutics on 16 July
  2026, plus two ultrasound-catheter investments announced 3 June 2026. Given the returns
  record, more deals are a risk to underwrite, not a catalyst to applaud.
- *Promotional register in the fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 call.* "Thoroughbreds coming out of
  the gate hot," "Cardiac Rhythm is just crushing it," "which is beautiful to see." The
  substance underneath is real, but the language is running ahead of the disclosure — most
  visibly on robotics, where the adjectives are most abundant and the numbers most absent.
- *Guidance now depends on ramps, not on the installed base.* The step from 5.8% to roughly 7%
  organic growth requires four early-stage launches to compound simultaneously. Any one
  stumbling is absorbable; two is not.

**Green flags**

- *The growth acceleration is real, sequential, and independently corroborated.* Ablation
  growth of roughly 30% → 50% → 71% → 78% across four consecutive quarters, with eight points
  of US market share gained in the fourth quarter, is not a presentation artefact. The
  corroboration is external: the principal US electrophysiology competitor cut its full-year
  organic revenue guidance twice during 2026, citing competitive pressure in exactly this
  market. Medtronic's gain and that competitor's loss are the same event seen from two sides.
- *Guidance was raised twice and then beaten.* See section 4.
- *The installed base is the right leading indicator and it is compounding.* Ablation mapping
  systems doubled in the second quarter of fiscal 2026 and the US base rose 40% sequentially in
  the fourth. In a capital-plus-consumable model, placed systems are contracted future
  consumable revenue in all but name. Management identified this correctly: "our mapping system
  sales are a strong leading indicator of future revenue growth and margin expansion."
- *Portfolio pruning is genuine and structurally smart.* Four exits in four years, and the
  chosen separation mechanism for diabetes — an initial public offering followed by a share
  exchange — permanently retires Medtronic shares rather than delivering a one-off buyback.
  The chief financial officer explained the mechanic plainly in May 2025 and refused to claim
  credit for the resulting earnings step-up: "that onetime EPS bump, we're not looking at
  that. We see it for what it is."
- *Balance sheet and cash generation are sound.* Free cash flow of $5.426 billion in fiscal
  2026, up 4.6%, covers the roughly $3.7 billion dividend with room; net debt is 1.9 times
  earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation.
- *Named institutions, not pilots.* Cleveland Clinic, Duke University Hospital and Atrium
  Health Wake Forest Baptist have installed Hugo in the United States; the last of those sits
  outside the original clinical-study network. Named payers cover roughly 30 million lives for
  the blood-pressure procedure.

## 7. Hidden signals

**The vocabulary of confidence changed shape.** Across the five calls the unit of proof moved
from market size to installed count. May 2025 argued from addressable markets ("nearly half of
US adults have hypertension"). June 2026 argued from ledgers (200 physicians, 300 accounts,
1,400 installations, implanters up threefold). Companies that are winning tend to make that
switch; companies that are stalling tend to make the reverse one.

**A quiet admission about the moat's shape.** In February 2026 the chief executive explained
Stealth AXiS not as a better robot but as a better *workflow*: 70% of US spine procedures are
navigated, robots do not fit that workflow, and the new platform "fits right into that
workflow." Read carefully, that is a statement that Medtronic's durable advantage in spine is
the navigation and imaging installed base — the switching cost — and that robotics is a way to
monetise it, not the moat itself. The same logic runs through the surgical pitch: "Medtronic is
the only company that has approved offerings across open, laparoscopic and robotic-assisted
surgeries." The advantage claimed is breadth of the bag, not superiority of the robot.

**The competitive language on robotics is defensive in structure even when confident in tone.**
Hugo is described as "flexibility, portability, open console," as growing "2x to 3x the
market," and as compelling "when paired with our Touch Surgery digital ecosystem." Every one of
those is a differentiation-by-adjacency argument. No call claims Hugo is a better robot than
the incumbent. That is probably an honest read of reality — the incumbent's newest system took
roughly 85% of US placements in the first quarter of 2026 — but it means the robotics line
should be underwritten as an option, not as a driver.

**A new voice, and what it changed.** Investor relations passed from Ryan Weispfenning to
Ingrid Goldberg between the November 2025 and February 2026 calls. The immediate effect was
housekeeping — tighter scripts, faster question rotation — but the disclosure texture also
changed: the counts-and-ratios style of the last two calls postdates the handover.

**Tariffs moved from headline to footnote, deliberately.** In May 2025 tariffs got dedicated
airtime and a mitigation narrative. By June 2026 the treatment is a single balanced paragraph:
status quo assumed, no refund upside booked, "we took a balanced approach on it." Costs that
management stops narrating are usually costs management has decided it can absorb. The
$250 million assumed in the fiscal 2027 guide is roughly 2.5% of adjusted earnings — real,
but not thesis-determining unless it escalates.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict

**Trajectory: strengthening.** Revenue growth accelerated for four consecutive quarters and
finished fiscal 2026 at a ten-year high; guidance was raised twice and beaten; the portfolio
has been narrowed four times; and the growth is concentrated in a franchise — pulsed-field
ablation — where market-share gains are corroborated by a competitor's guidance cuts. The
weakness is on the profitability line, not the demand line.

**The single thing the director must weigh before sizing.** Medtronic is buying its
acceleration. Adjusted operating margin fell 130 basis points in the year the growth story
came good, and gross margin has drifted down roughly 0.65 percentage points across three
years while revenue grew 16%. The fiscal 2027 guide asks for roughly a point of *additional*
revenue acceleration while still absorbing $250 million of tariffs. If the spending flattens
and the ablation consumables stream converts the placed installed base into high-margin
revenue, the earnings guide is beatable and the shares are cheap. If the spending has to keep
rising to sustain the ramp — which is what the last two years of margin data actually show —
then this is a mid-single-digit grower being paid for as a high-single-digit one, and the
current price already reflects the good version. Size to the margin line, not the revenue line.

**Economic moat: narrow, and stable rather than widening.** The moat type is **switching
costs layered on scale distribution** — surgeon training, implant registries, the navigation
and imaging installed base in spine, the mapping-system base in electrophysiology, and a sales
bag broad enough that hospitals buy the ecosystem rather than the device. The evidence is
consistent with narrow rather than wide: gross margin of 65% is high but has eroded slightly;
return on invested capital of about 6.1%, recovering from a 4.7% trough but still below the
6.4% of fiscal 2022, does not describe a widening moat. Two forces run in opposite directions
and roughly cancel — the ablation and blood-pressure franchises are genuinely widening the
moat in cardiovascular, while surgical stapling, energy devices and parts of neuroscience face
ordinary commoditisation and Chinese volume-based procurement pricing. Net: narrow, stable.

**Secular threat: manageable.** The relevant structural force is reimbursement compression —
payers and health systems squeezing device prices. It is real, and China's volume-based
procurement already cut through the neurovascular business (management flagged it as "probably
the biggest impact we've had on any one business," August 2025). But Medtronic sits on the
volume side of the same trend: an ageing population and the migration of procedures to
outpatient surgery centres expand its unit demand, and the company is creating reimbursed
categories rather than defending shrinking ones — the Medicare national coverage decision for
renal denervation in October 2025 is a new revenue pool, not a defended one. Nothing in this
file points to terminal-value impairment. The valuation — roughly 14.4 times the midpoint of
fiscal 2027 adjusted earnings guidance, against a ten-year average nearer 16 to 17 times —
looks like a sector-wide fear print rather than a structurally shrinking base. That said, the
cheapness is moderate, not extreme, and the shares have already recovered about 17% from the
$73.31 low set in late June 2026.
