# Vertiv Holdings Co (VRT) — a deep dive on management and the setup

Forensic review of six consecutive earnings calls (first quarter 2025 through first quarter 2026) plus
the second-quarter 2026 report published 29 July 2026, cross-checked against the reported financials.
Price used throughout: $273.35 (New York Stock Exchange, US dollars, 4 August 2026).

Vertiv makes the physical guts of a data centre — uninterruptible power supplies, switchgear, power
distribution and, increasingly, liquid cooling. It sells to hyperscalers (the very large cloud
operators), colocation landlords and enterprises. It is an equipment and services company, not a
commodity producer: copper, steel and freight are costs to Vertiv, never revenues.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story has not shifted so much as **thickened**, in three distinct steps.

**Step one — "we are riding a secular wave" (first half of 2025).** The opening frame was market
growth plus share gain. Executive Chairman Dave Cote in the first quarter of 2025: "the digital
revolution is happening. It still has a long way to go." Chief Executive Gio Albertazzi anchored
everything to a five-year model presented at a November 2024 investor day: 15-17% growth for the
cloud and colocation segment, 12-14% for Vertiv. The proof points offered were backlog ($7.9 billion,
first quarter 2025) and a book-to-bill ratio of 1.4 times — book-to-bill compares orders taken to
revenue billed, so anything above 1.0 means the order book is growing faster than sales.

**Step two — "we are the connective tissue" (second half of 2025).** In the second quarter of 2025
management introduced a genuinely new idea: that the "grey space" (power and cooling) and the "white
space" (the racks and servers) are merging as rack densities climb into the hundreds of kilowatts.
This was not marketing filler; it became the strategic basis for the converged, prefabricated products
launched later. Services were elevated from a footnote to what Albertazzi called "one of Vertiv's
superpowers" in the third quarter of 2025.

**Step three — "we sell the whole data centre" (2026).** By the fourth quarter of 2025 the company had
named products behind the idea: OneCore, a complete data-centre building block sold in 12.5-megawatt
increments, and SmartRun, a prefabricated white-space system. Named deployments followed with Hut 8
and Compass Data Centers. By the first quarter of 2026 the language moved to capacity: new plants,
a doubling of chiller capacity in Italy by end-2026, a new Malaysian factory opened 1 July 2026, and
capital spending stepping from the historical 2-3% of sales to 3-4%.

**What was quietly dropped.** Two things. First, the 12-14% Vertiv growth model from November 2024 has
been overtaken and replaced — at the May 2026 investor conference the company reset to a 20-22%
five-year revenue growth rate and a 27% adjusted operating margin ambition for 2030. That is an upgrade,
not a retreat, but it means the original five-year model no longer serves as a check on the story.
Second, and far more consequential, **the orders and backlog disclosure was withdrawn.** Flagged in
the second quarter of 2025, confirmed in the fourth quarter of 2025 ("we believe that currently, the
best approach is to no longer report actual orders, orders forecast or backlog with quarterly
earnings"), and executed from the first quarter of 2026 onward. The second-quarter 2026 call gave no
backlog figure and no book-to-bill ratio.

**Where the story is richer than the trajectory justifies.** Europe. Management has described the
region as improving for three consecutive calls while it deteriorated from −4% to −29%. The promise
of a return to growth in the second half of 2026 is now embedded in guidance and is the single
unverified operating claim in the file.

**Where the story is poorer than the trajectory justifies.** Cash generation. Management understated
it repeatedly — guiding $1.4 billion in free cash flow for 2025 and delivering $1.89 billion; guiding
$6.02 in adjusted earnings per share for 2026 in February, raising to $6.35 in April, then to $6.70
in July. Three raises in six months is not a company straining to hit its numbers.

**The one genuine operating stumble.** Second-quarter 2026 revenue of $3,274 million grew 24% but
landed roughly 3% below what the street expected. Management attributed it to "minor timing shifts...
primarily driven by multi-phase project execution and temporary supply chain dynamics" — a "learning
curve" on large interconnected projects. The stock fell about 25% over four weeks into that print.
This is the first evidence that the converged, prefabricated strategy carries execution and revenue-
timing risk that the older component business did not: milestone-based revenue recognition on
multi-phase projects is lumpier by construction.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising, and increasingly specific.**

- *First quarter 2025* — confident but hedged on tariffs: "rather than speculate, we'll continue to
  focus on what we can't control" (a transcription slip for "can control"). Guidance ranges were
  *widened* to absorb tariff uncertainty.
- *Second quarter 2025* — the most defensive call in the set. Albertazzi conceded "some executional
  challenges in EMEA" and margin down 110 basis points year on year. Language turned to mitigation
  and cost.
- *Third quarter 2025* — the pivot back to offence. Cote: "this is a very strong quarter by any
  measure... I wonder what would have happened if we hadn't blown the doors off of every single
  metric." Note the irritation at the share-price reaction; the stock fell on a beat.
- *Fourth quarter 2025* — peak confidence, with the numbers to match: orders up 252% year on year,
  backlog doubling to $15 billion.
- *First quarter 2026* — Cote: "what we're seeing in customer conversations is different than 6
  months ago. The urgency has increased."
- *Second quarter 2026* — confidence maintained through a revenue miss; guidance raised across every
  line rather than trimmed.

There is no shift from offensive to defensive language. If anything the reverse: the defensive
vocabulary clustered in the middle of 2025, when tariffs and European execution were biting, and has
since been replaced by capacity, investment and content-per-megawatt.

**One tonal caution.** The rhetoric is consistently superlative — "the future has never been
brighter", "I'm more excited today than I've ever been", "insatiable, dare I say, demand". In a sector
where promoters live, this register is a standing flag. What defends Vertiv here is that the numbers
have so far exceeded the rhetoric rather than trailed it.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Score: High.**

The pattern is unambiguous and one-directional: guide conservatively, raise repeatedly, deliver above
the raised number. Fiscal 2025 sales guidance went from 18% growth (April) to 24% (July) and landed at
27.7%. Fiscal 2026 adjusted earnings per share guidance went $6.02 (February) → $6.35 (April) →
$6.65-$6.75 (July). Free cash flow guidance for 2025 went to $1.4 billion and delivered $1.89 billion.
The screener records seven earnings beats out of seven quarters.

This is sandbagging, and it should be read as such — the *level* of guidance carries little
information, the *direction of revision* carries most of it. The single blemish is the tariff-neutrality
date, promised for the end of 2025 and re-promised for the end of the first quarter of 2026. They
missed the first date and hit the second.

The offsetting concern is not accuracy but **verifiability**. With orders and backlog withdrawn from
quarterly disclosure, the leading indicator that would confirm or contradict the guidance a year ahead
is now visible only once a year, in the annual report. An investor is being asked to take the
company's word on the one number that matters most.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**The recurring question is technological obsolescence, and it is asked every single quarter in a new
costume.** Amit Mehrotra of UBS put it plainly in the third quarter of 2025: "every three or four
months, there's some announcement or some innovation that gets everybody to question the entire thesis
around Vertiv's position." The specific threats raised across the calls: Amazon Web Services designing
its own in-row heat exchangers; Microsoft microfluidics cooling chips directly; 800-volt direct current
architectures eliminating power supply units; NVIDIA's Rubin-generation racks running warm enough to
skip chillers; competitors' stainless-steel chillers displacing coolant distribution units.

Management's answers have been substantive rather than evasive, and — importantly — checkable.
Albertazzi argued that microfluidics is still direct-to-chip liquid cooling and "preserves everything,
thermal chain... intact"; that 800-volt direct current plays to decades of Vertiv direct-current
experience; that warmer chips reduce chiller count but not the need for heat rejection, which is why
the company catalogued a "Trim Cooler". The falsifiable claim attached to these answers is content per
megawatt, and it has gone **up**, not down: from a $3.0-3.5 million range to $3.25-3.75 million as of
the third quarter of 2025, and Vertiv is now embedded in NVIDIA's Omniverse DSX reference blueprint
(announced 1 June 2026).

**Genuinely deflected.** Two topics. First, backlog margin — Chris Snyder of Morgan Stanley asked
directly in the second quarter of 2025 whether the margin embedded in backlog supports a fourth-quarter
recovery, and got "we do not go in those level of details." Second, orders guidance — Mehrotra pressed
on trailing-twelve-month orders in the second quarter of 2025 and was told "I would be guiding orders,
and that's not what we do."

**What analysts worried about that turned into bad news.** The margin pressure Snyder probed in the
second quarter of 2025 was real and lasted three quarters. The European weakness analysts pushed on
from the first quarter of 2025 got worse for a full year. Both were flagged by the sell side before
management conceded them.

**What analysts are not pressing hard enough on.** The working-capital shape of the new converged
business. Nigel Coe of Wolfe Research got closest in the first quarter of 2026, asking whether the
surge in customer deposits is a readable proxy for backlog growth; Chief Financial Officer Craig
Chamberlin's answer was "it is a way to look at backlog, but it's not entirely our read through."
Advance payments flatter free cash flow while orders are being taken and reverse when they are
delivered. Second-quarter 2026 free cash flow of $925 million, up 234%, is partly a deposit-timing
figure, not a steady-state one.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

🔴 **Orders and backlog disclosure withdrawn at the exact peak.** Announced in the second quarter of
2025, confirmed in the fourth quarter of 2025 immediately after reporting orders up 252% and a
book-to-bill of 2.9 times, effective from 2026. The stated reason — lumpiness causing "unnecessary
volatility... not beneficial to our investors" — is coherent, and the fact that it was pre-announced
before the blowout quarter is genuinely mitigating. The effect is not. The primary leading indicator
for this company is now dark between annual reports.

🔴 **Europe: three calls of improvement language against a worsening number.** "The coiled spring is
uncoiling" (fourth quarter 2025) was said while the region ran −14%, and it then ran −29% in the first
quarter of 2026.

🔴 **No insider buying on the open market.** Zero open-market purchases against 38 disposals totalling
about 271,000 shares. Recent Form 4 filings (25 June 2026) are dividend-equivalent accruals of a few
shares each; the 4 May 2026 disposals at $330.97 were tax withholding on vesting rather than
discretionary sales. So the disposals are less damning than the raw count suggests — but the absence
of buying at any point in a 130% share-price round trip is still an absence.

🔴 **The 2022 precedent is in this company's own accounts.** Revenue grew 13.8% in fiscal 2022 while
net income collapsed to $77 million and free cash flow went to **negative $264 million**. That was
input-cost and freight inflation running through a fixed-price order book. The mechanism that broke
Vertiv four years ago has not been legislated away; it has been managed better.

🔴 **Execution stumble on the new converged products.** The second-quarter 2026 revenue shortfall was
attributed to a "learning curve" on large interconnected projects — the first time the strategy that
justifies the premium has cost the company a number.

🟢 **Gross margin expanding while revenue accelerates.** From 32.2% in the second quarter of 2025 to
37.7% in the second quarter of 2026, on revenue up 24%. In a supply-constrained niche this is the
cleanest available evidence of pricing power. The opposite pattern — margin falling as revenue grows —
would have signalled that volume was being bought with price.

🟢 **Buybacks at the bottom, none at the top.** The company repurchased $599.9 million of stock in
fiscal 2024, when the shares traded at a fraction of today's price, and has repurchased **nothing** in
2025 or 2026 at three to five times that level. Cash has gone instead to bolt-on acquisitions
($1.18 billion in 2025: Great Lakes, WeiLay; then ThermoKey completed 12 June 2026 and Strategic
Thermal Labs announced 20 July 2026) and a token dividend of $0.0625 a quarter. This is the reverse of
the usual pattern and it is the strongest single piece of evidence on capital discipline.

🟢 **Balance sheet effectively unlevered.** Net debt of $146 million including finance leases at 30
June 2026 against trailing twelve-month adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and
amortisation of about $2.52 billion — roughly 0.06 times. Excluding leases the company is in a small
net cash position, which is how the second-quarter release describes it. Liquidity $5.6 billion.

🟢 **Named, dated, checkable deployments rather than memoranda of understanding.** Hut 8 and Compass
Data Centers (fourth quarter 2025), the Naval Postgraduate School NVIDIA DGX GB300 installation
(23 July 2026), VisionBay.ai/Foxconn in Taiwan and Data4 in Frankfurt (second quarter 2026 call).
Backlog is defined tightly: "a binding purchase order... very often with advanced payment."

🟢 **Capacity is being added against demand, with disclosed sites and dates.** Johor, Malaysia opened
1 July 2026; Tognana, Italy chiller capacity to double by end-2026 with a testing laboratory in early
2027. Management is explicit that it runs 20-25% deliberate slack in factory loading.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**Terminology.** The vocabulary moved from components ("thermal", "power") to systems ("connective
tissue", "converged", "prefabricated", "time to token") and finally to the customer's own unit of
account — dollars of Vertiv content per megawatt of data centre. That last shift matters because it
converts an obsolescence debate into an arithmetic one, and the arithmetic has moved in Vertiv's
favour.

**How competition is discussed.** In the third quarter of 2025 Albertazzi made a striking observation:
comparing liquid cooling today with eighteen months earlier, he saw "a better delineation of the
competitive landscape... more consolidated, more rational players not bad." A market leader welcoming
consolidation among its rivals is describing an industry where price competition is easing — which is
exactly what the gross-margin line then did.

**Capital allocation language hardened.** In the second quarter of 2025 acquisitions were "an important
element". By the first quarter of 2026 Albertazzi said the company has "no reticence in cutting bigger
checks when that's needed". Deal sizes are not being disclosed ("we didn't give any of the sizes, but
if they were materially impactful to us, we would have"), which is defensible for bolt-ons but removes
a check on the return being earned on $1.18 billion of 2025 acquisition spend.

**New voices.** Chief Financial Officer David Fallon retired, announced on the third-quarter 2025 call
with Craig Chamberlin succeeding him from the fourth quarter of 2025. A new Chief Procurement Officer,
Frieda He, joined 5 May 2026 with a mandate on supply-chain resilience and cost. Chamberlin is
noticeably more forthcoming on the mechanics — his answers on incremental margins, customer deposits
and product mix are more concrete than his predecessor's, and he volunteered the constraint that
incremental margins are being guided at the low end of the 30-35% range because of the investment ramp.
That is a chief financial officer describing a cost, not selling a story.

**One arithmetic tell.** The second-quarter 2026 revenue of $3,274 million included five percentage
points of growth from acquisitions and one from currency. Organic growth was 18%, against 34% organic
in the same quarter a year earlier. Headline growth of 24% is being increasingly assisted by
acquisitions. Full-year guidance still calls for 31% organic, which requires a sharp reacceleration in
the second half — the third-quarter guide of 34-36% organic is where that gets tested.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.**

Returns are rising, not falling: return on invested capital of 20.8% and return on equity of 42.1% on
a trailing twelve-month basis, with gross margin up about 550 basis points and adjusted operating
margin up 410 basis points year on year. Growth capital spending is stepping up from 2-3% to 3-4% of
sales into visible, dated capacity additions, funded entirely from a free cash flow line running at
$2.4-2.6 billion for 2026 with essentially no net debt. Buybacks were executed at the low and stopped
at the high.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing.** Not whether the franchise is good — it
demonstrably is — but that **the one number that would tell you when this cycle turns is no longer
published quarterly.** Vertiv's entire forward visibility rests on a backlog last disclosed at $15
billion on 11 February 2026 and on management's unaudited assurance since. The company is priced for
the order book to keep compounding, and the buyer has surrendered the instrument that would give early
warning if it does not. Size for a business whose demand signal you can only inspect once a year.

**Economic moat: NARROW, and WIDENING.** The moat type is switching costs layered over scale and
intangible engineering assets. The switching costs are real and getting deeper: an installed base
served by a global field-service organisation, early involvement in customer project design ("early
involvement in project technology and in project planning further drives our above-market growth",
third quarter 2025), and now inclusion in NVIDIA's reference architecture for both 800-volt direct
current power and the Omniverse DSX design blueprint. Scale shows up as the ability to deliver
gigawatt-class programmes on a schedule, which is the binding constraint for every hyperscaler.

It is narrow rather than wide because the competitive set is formidable and well funded — Eaton,
Schneider Electric, ABB, nVent, Johnson Controls and Carrier all attack parts of this stack, and the
liquid-cooling entrants are numerous. It is widening rather than eroding because the evidence points
that way: returns rising, gross margin expanding on accelerating revenue, content per megawatt guided
up rather than down, and the leader's own read that the field is consolidating toward "more rational
players."

**Secular threat: manageable.** The structural forces that impair terminal value elsewhere do not
point at this business. Artificial intelligence is Vertiv's demand driver, not its displacer; there is
no channel shift, no reimbursement compression, no disintermediation path. The genuine risk here is
**cyclical, not secular** — a hyperscaler capital-spending digestion phase that empties the order book
for a year or two. That distinction is the whole investment question: the terminal value looks intact,
the near-term earnings base does not look safe. Consequently the shares are neither a fear-print
mispricing nor a value trap; at 41.9 times trailing enterprise value to earnings before interest, tax,
depreciation and amortisation they are a high-quality franchise carrying a full price and a genuine
cyclical drawdown risk.
