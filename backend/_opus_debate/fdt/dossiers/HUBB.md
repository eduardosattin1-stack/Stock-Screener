# Hubbell Incorporated (HUBB) — Forensic Dossier

*Prepared 2026-08-04. Six consecutive earnings calls reviewed: first quarter 2025 (2025-05-01)
through second quarter 2026 (2026-07-28). Share price used throughout: $498.62, United States
dollars, New York Stock Exchange listing, live quote taken 2026-08-04 (previous close $476.04,
up about 4.7% on the day).*

Hubbell makes the physical hardware that carries electricity: connectors, transformers,
protective devices and substation components for electric utilities (the Utility Solutions
segment, about 63% of 2025 revenue), plus wiring devices, fittings and enclosures for
commercial and industrial buildings (the Electrical Solutions segment, about 37%). It is an
equipment maker, so metals such as copper, aluminum and steel are input costs, not revenue.

---

## 1. Narrative arc

The story management tells has changed shape three times across these six quarters, and the
direction of travel has been steadily more confident.

**Early 2025 — defence.** The first-quarter 2025 call was about surviving cost inflation. The
opening remarks led with tariffs, raw-material prices and a promise of resilience: Hubbell is
"a U.S.-centric manufacturing company with more than 90% of our sales in the U.S.", cost
inflation cycles "are not new to Hubbell", and the company was "maintaining" — not raising —
its full-year outlook. Two businesses were visibly broken: grid automation (metering and
advanced metering infrastructure, sold under the Aclara brand) was contracting, and utility
distribution products were still working through a customer inventory glut.

**Mid-2025 to early 2026 — repair, then offence.** The second-quarter 2025 call introduced a
mid-year accounting change (moving inventory costing from last-in-first-out to
first-in-first-out) and the first guidance raise. By the fourth quarter of 2025 the tone had
flipped entirely: 12% sales growth, 9% of it organic, and a new framing of Hubbell as "uniquely
positioned at the intersection of grid modernization and electrification". Data centre revenue
grew more than 60% in that quarter.

**2026 — expansion.** The first-quarter 2026 call introduced an entirely new growth pillar:
765-kilovolt high-voltage transmission, sized at "approximately $1.5 billion over the next
10 years". The second-quarter 2026 call added a second: the $3.0 billion cash acquisition of
NSI Industries, closed 2026-06-09, the largest deal in the company's history.

**What was quietly dropped.** The tariff-mitigation narrative that dominated early 2025 has
faded from the opening remarks into a technical footnote about refunds. The "customer inventory
normalization" story, which management called finished in the first quarter of 2025, was still
being explained away as an easy comparison in the second quarter of 2026 — fifteen months later.
Nothing was abandoned dishonestly; the older stories simply stopped being useful.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, steadily and without a stumble.**

- *Q1 2025 — guarded.* "The current environment warrants caution and continued proactive cost
  management." Guidance maintained, not raised. Chief financial officer Bill Sperry described
  a "$10 million drag" and asked for patience.
- *Q2 2025 — turning.* "We are raising our full year outlook today." Grid infrastructure orders
  "up high teens year-over-year in the first half."
- *Q3 2025 — confident but honest about the source.* "We anticipate the impact of lower organic
  growth to be fully offset by stronger margin performance, while a lower full year tax rate
  drives higher adjusted earnings per share." That is an unusually candid admission that the
  raise was not operational.
- *Q4 2025 — offensive.* "As these megatrends accelerate... we are confident in our ability to
  deliver continued strong performance."
- *Q1 2026 — expansionary.* A brand-new addressable market introduced unprompted.
- *Q2 2026 — most confident of the six.* "We continue to believe utility transmission and
  distribution markets are in the early stages of a multiyear investment cycle."

The language moved decisively from efficiency and cost control toward growth and investment.
Capital expenditure guidance rose from $155 million in 2025 to $175–190 million in 2026, and
management explicitly accepted a lower free-cash-flow conversion rate to fund it. That is an
offensive posture, and it is consistent with what they say they see.

Guidance has become *more* specific over time, not less — the second-quarter 2026 call gave
segment-level organic growth ranges, a data-centre growth figure, an interest-expense figure,
a tax rate, and a quarter-by-quarter margin shape. Companies hiding something do the opposite.

## 4. Guidance credibility

| Guidance given | Delivered |
|---|---|

| Q2 2025: 2025 sales growth 4–6%, about 3 points of price | 2025 organic growth landed at 9% in the fourth quarter; full year delivered |
| Q3 2025: "we anticipate these headwinds to fade in the fourth quarter" for grid automation | Grid automation declined 8% in Q4 2025 versus 18% in Q3 — the fade was real |
| Q1 2026: grid automation "to return to slight year-over-year growth in the second quarter" | Delivered exactly. Grid automation returned to growth in Q2 2026 with book-to-bill above 1.0 |
| Q1 2026: full-year adjusted earnings per share $19.30–$19.85 | Raised in Q2 2026 to $20.25–$20.55 |
| Q2 2026 result versus street | $5.52 actual against a $5.31 consensus, a beat of about 4% |

The screener records a beat in 6 of the last 7 quarters, about 86%. That is corroborated by the
transcripts.

**Reliability: High.** There is a mild sandbagging pattern — guidance is set conservatively in
January and raised through the year — but it is the ordinary industrial variety, not
manipulation. The one blemish is composition rather than accuracy: three of the raises drew on
accounting, tax and tariff items rather than operations, and in each case management said so
plainly when asked.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

**The underlying margin question is the one they will not answer.** In the second quarter of
2026, Nigel Coe of Wolfe Research forced the margin bridge and got it. He then asked the
obvious follow-up — stripping out the tariff refund, would Electrical Solutions still show
margin expansion in the third quarter? The reply: "that is hard to reconcile right now, Nigel.
We can take that offline." That is the single clearest non-answer in six calls, and it sits on
exactly the question that matters most.

**Data-centre revenue is never quantified.** Data centre is the largest single growth driver —
up more than 60% in the fourth quarter of 2025 and about 65% in the second quarter of 2026,
guided to roughly 50% for the full year. Across all six calls, Hubbell has never disclosed the
dollar size of that business, never named a customer, and never disclosed concentration. When
Christopher Glynn of Oppenheimer asked in the second quarter of 2026 whether the content gains
came from allocation changes or expanded design wins, the answer was "I would call it more of
the same."

**Grid automation inflection calls were repeatedly early.** Management described a "floor" in
this business in the first quarter of 2025, then watched it decline 15%, 18%, 8% and 7% over
the following four quarters before it actually turned in the second quarter of 2026. Brett
Linzey of Mizuho pressed on this in the first quarter of 2025 and was told the quarter "was
softer than we had anticipated." The eventual turn was called correctly one quarter ahead —
but only after five quarters of premature bottoms.

**Distribution destocking outlasted the all-clear.** In the first quarter of 2025 management
said "the period of destocking is now behind us." In the second quarter of 2026, explaining
double-digit distribution growth, Gerben Bakker conceded "the comps are still somewhat easy to
lap." Analysts have not pushed hard on how much of that growth is genuine end demand.

**Non-residential and heavy industrial.** Julian Mitchell of Barclays noted in the third quarter
of 2025 that Hubbell sounded more cautious than peers. Management agreed. By the second quarter
of 2026 non-residential was described as "gaining momentum" but with an immediate hedge: "we
are a little cautious to say that is going to continue to accelerate."

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Red flags**

- **Leverage roughly doubled in one quarter** (Q2 2026). Net debt at 2026-06-30 is $4.98
  billion against 1.3 times leverage at the end of 2025. The $3.0 billion NSI purchase was
  funded with a $900 million term loan, $1.9 billion of senior notes and commercial paper.
  Deleveraging is promised over "24 to 30 months" — a long time to be exposed.
- **The full-year margin raise contains no core operating improvement** (Q2 2026, confirmed
  by the chief financial officer): 30 basis points from a one-time tariff refund, 40 basis
  points from acquisition mix, with volume gains offset by investment.
- **Percentage margins are compressing right now.** Gross margin down 139 basis points and
  adjusted operating margin down 50 basis points year over year in the second quarter of 2026,
  on 15% revenue growth. Electrical Solutions adjusted operating margin fell 130 basis points.
- **Three price increases in fifteen months to stand still.** Price contribution went from a
  planned 2 points, to 3 points after an April increase, to 3–4 points after a July increase —
  each time to offset inflation "on a dollar-for-dollar basis." Passing cost through in dollars
  mathematically shrinks percentage margin. This is cost recovery, not pricing power.
- **Acquisition multiples paid at the top of the cycle.** In February 2026 Gerben Bakker
  acknowledged that "we were buying companies not too long ago in the single-digit multiples,
  and that is clearly increased." Four months later Hubbell paid $3.0 billion for a business
  guiding to roughly $570 million of 2026 revenue — about 5.3 times sales.
- **Insider behaviour.** Zero open-market purchases against 13 sales totalling 32,977 shares.
- **No named customers and no disclosed backlog value anywhere.** Book-to-bill of about 1.2
  times is the only order-book figure given, and management themselves caution that Hubbell is
  "not typically a backlog driven business."
- **Negative tangible book value** of about $3.62 billion, with intangibles at 63.7% of total
  assets. There is no asset floor beneath this equity.

**Green flags**

- **Restructuring costs are included in adjusted results** and management said so pointedly in
  the third quarter of 2025: "a lot of our competitors would exclude that number from their
  margin and say that it is a discretionary item. And we just feel it is going to be part of
  our year in and year out modus operandi." Segments are also fully burdened with corporate
  costs. Both are genuinely conservative choices.
- **The accounting change was disclosed with a complete bridge** (Q2 2025): $29 million of
  cost-of-goods reduction in the quarter, $20 million in the first half, about $0.30 of
  earnings per share, all handed to analysts before anyone asked.
- **A dated operational prediction that landed.** Grid automation was called to return to
  growth in the second quarter of 2026, on the first-quarter 2026 call. It did.
- **The new growth story was honestly sized.** The 765-kilovolt opportunity was framed as "about
  a point of additional growth" on a $1.5 billion ten-year addressable market — not a headline
  number detached from company revenue. First 765-kilovolt shipments are dated to 2027 and
  550-kilovolt shipments to the second half of 2026. Promoters do not deflate their own numbers.
- **Buybacks executed below today's price.** $168 million repurchased in the first quarter of
  2026 at an average cost below $500 per share, plus $225 million in the first half of 2025.
- **Order book extending into 2027** in transmission and substation, with project quoting
  volume roughly double what it was two years ago.
- **Capacity investment is explicit and quantified**: capital expenditure rising to 2.5–3% of
  sales from under 2%, adding roughly $25 million of new capacity at a time, with the resulting
  drag on cash conversion openly acknowledged rather than hidden.

## 7. Hidden signals

**The chief financial officer changed, and so did the balance sheet.** Bill Sperry retired at
the end of 2025 after eighteen years, during which — by management's own accounting on the
third-quarter 2025 call — Hubbell "more than doubled sales, improved operating margins from low
teens to over 20%" while running leverage at 1.3 times. His successor Joe Capozzoli, promoted
internally from the Electrical Solutions segment, closed a $3.0 billion acquisition within two
quarters and took leverage to nearly 3 times. This is the most consequential unremarked change
in the six calls. It is not evidence of a mistake — NSI looks like a sensible strategic fit —
but the capital-allocation posture is measurably different, and the director should size the
position against the new posture, not the old track record.

**Book-to-bill became a talking point precisely when it turned favourable.** In the first
quarter of 2026 Gerben Bakker said "we generally do not talk about book and bill a lot because
it is about order rates, because we are a more short-cycle business." One quarter later,
book-to-bill of 1.2 times led the utility segment discussion. Management did hedge it honestly,
but the selective emphasis is worth noting.

**Free-cash-flow conversion was quietly reset.** The historical standard was 100%-plus of net
income. It is now "pacing around 90% for the next couple of years" because capital intensity
rose structurally. Honest, disclosed, and permanent — this is a higher-capital-intensity
company than it was three years ago.

**Market capitalisation appeared as a management scorecard.** The third-quarter 2025 tribute to
the departing chief financial officer cited increasing "our market cap from less than $3 billion
to $23 billion." Harmless in context, but a small tell that share price is watched internally
as a performance measure.

**Modularity is the real competitive story, and it is under-told.** Across the second-quarter
2026 call Bakker described a shift toward factory-built assemblies — power skids for data
centres, control houses for substations, crimped connectors replacing field welding — driven by
skilled-labour scarcity. That is a genuine, durable content-per-project expansion, and it
receives far less airtime than the megatrend language.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.** Revenue growth accelerated from roughly 4% in early 2025 to 15%
in the second quarter of 2026, organic growth from negative-to-flat to 10%, and every segment
that was broken in early 2025 — grid automation, utility distribution, telecom — has now turned.
The order book supports the second half and is beginning to fill 2027. Guidance has been raised
four consecutive quarters and delivered each time.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing:** *the balance sheet changed
character in a single quarter, and the current-quarter margin story is being carried by items
that do not repeat.* Hubbell went from 1.3 times leverage to roughly 2.9 times pro-forma to buy
an asset at about 5.3 times sales, at a point in the cycle management itself described as one of
elevated acquisition multiples. Simultaneously, the entire full-year margin-guidance raise came
from a one-time tariff refund and acquisition mix rather than operations, while gross margin
compressed 139 basis points year over year. The operating business is genuinely excellent; the
financial structure supporting it is thinner than it was, and the fourth quarter of 2026 — which
laps a very strong comparison without the tariff refund — is where the underlying margin claim
gets tested with nothing to hide behind.

**Economic moat: WIDE.** The moat is a combination of switching costs and intangible brand,
reinforced by scale. Electric utilities write specific manufacturer brands into their
construction standards, and requalifying a substitute connector or transformer bushing is a slow,
committee-driven process with high cost of failure and low cost of ownership — exactly the
dynamic Bakker described for the DMC crimped-connector product. Hubbell's leading installed base
in transmission and substation infrastructure, its testing laboratory, and its ability to supply
"a full package of critical components" from a single relationship are genuine and hard to
replicate. The evidence supports width: gross margin expanded roughly 572 basis points over
three years, adjusted operating margin moved from the low teens to above 23% over the past
decade, and return on equity is about 23.7%.

**Moat trend: STABLE.** Not widening, despite the historical record. Gross margin has compressed
in each of the last two quarters, pricing is now recovering cost dollar-for-dollar rather than
running ahead of it, and return on invested capital measured on the full capital base has been
diluted by a decade of goodwill-heavy acquisitions to roughly 9.6%. The moat is holding, not
deepening.

**Secular threat: none.** Grid modernisation, electrification and data-centre load growth are
structural tailwinds for this asset base, not threats to it. The risks here are cyclical —
utility capital-expenditure digestion, a data-centre building pause, a copper or aluminum spike
— not terminal. The valuation is therefore not a fear-print and not a value trap; it is a
high-quality franchise trading at a full price with the cycle running hot.
