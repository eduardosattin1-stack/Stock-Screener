# FormFactor (FORM) — a deep dive on management and the setup

*Forensic dossier, prepared 4 August 2026. Live price used throughout: $121.25 (Nasdaq, US dollars), up 11.4% on the day in a market-wide relief rally.*

FormFactor makes probe cards — the custom, single-use-per-design test fixtures that touch a silicon wafer and check every chip on it before it is cut and packaged — plus a smaller line of engineering test systems, including cryogenic probe stations used to characterise quantum processors. Five consecutive earnings calls (April 2025 through April 2026) are in the transcript file; the July 2026 second-quarter call and press release were verified separately against company filings and the earnings release. What follows tests management's story against what the numbers actually did.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

**The story has completed a full arc from apology to victory lap, and it happened faster than management themselves promised.**

In April 2025 the emphasis was defensive. The company was managing tariff damage ("we manufacture approximately 80% of our revenue in the United States and therefore face a direct cost impact from tariffs"), absorbing tightened export controls that cut China revenue, and reminding investors why it had divested its China operations in 2023. Growth was framed as coming from someone else's spending — hyperscaler investment in generative artificial intelligence pulling through high-bandwidth-memory probe cards.

By July 2025 the tone turned to open self-criticism, which is rare and worth crediting. Chief executive Mike Slessor said plainly that "our recent financial results and especially gross margins have not reflected our unique market leadership position," and named three causes: a mix shift toward lower-margin memory, operational cost increases, and tariffs. The company had been carrying a public target of 47% gross margin on $850 million of annual revenue for years and repeatedly failing to reach it.

October 2025 introduced the repair programme with specific numbers attached — a 250 basis point sequential margin gain already banked, another 100 basis points promised for the fourth quarter. February 2026 delivered 290 basis points instead. April 2026 announced the target model had been cleared on a quarterly run-rate basis and pre-announced an investor day (11 May 2026) to publish a new one. By the July 2026 second-quarter call the new model was in place: roughly $1.6 billion of revenue, 55% gross margin and more than $5 of adjusted earnings per share by 2030.

Three growth drivers were introduced and each has since produced revenue rather than being quietly dropped:

- **High-bandwidth memory.** From "a growing contribution from a second customer" (April 2025) to shipping in volume to all three of the world's memory makers (July 2025) to a stated 50%-plus increase in high-bandwidth-memory probe card revenue comparing the first half of 2026 with the first half of 2025 (April 2026).
- **Co-packaged optics** — putting the optical transceiver inside the chip package to cut data-centre power. From "multiple systems to a single customer to support pilot production" (April 2025) to a stated 2026 revenue range of $10–20 million (April 2026, at "the high end") to management confirming in July 2026 that full-year revenue will exceed $20 million.
- **Graphics-processor probe cards** — the single largest new share opportunity, sized by management at roughly a $50 million annual spend that today goes mostly to their competitor. Tracked from "met all technical requirements… now in the pilot production stage" (October 2025) to "nearing completion" (April 2026) to qualification complete with production units shipping in the third quarter of 2026 (July 2026).

What was *not* dropped but has quietly shrunk in prominence is quantum computing. It featured in the April and July 2025 calls (the IQ2000 and IQ3000 cryogenic probers, "Google's progress in error correction with their Willow platform") and produced one product launch in the first quarter of 2026, the Flat Iron benchtop dilution refrigerator. It did not feature at all in the July 2026 call. Management's own framing — "high-volume production remains a few years out" (July 2025) — is the honest one.

**The single most important divergence:** the deterministic screener metric says gross margin is flat at about 39% with a three-year change of minus 0.4 percentage points. The company reported 50.7%. The proxy is not wrong about history; it is simply blind to a fourteen-percentage-point inflection that occurred entirely inside the last four quarters. Any model built on the proxy is modelling a different company.

**The divergence that cuts the other way:** free cash flow. Trailing twelve-month free cash flow is $136 million on $902 million of revenue — a 15% margin, respectable, but far below what a 50%-margin business should throw off, because capital spending is ramping. Management has committed **$140–170 million of capital expenditure in calendar 2026** for the Farmers Branch, Texas plant (partly offset by a $24.2 million Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant) plus $25–30 million of pre-production operating cost. Free cash flow is going to stay suppressed through 2027, which is why the screener's price-to-free-cash-flow of 726 times looks absurd — it is a build-year artefact, not a valuation.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising, and the rise is earned rather than asserted.**

- *April 2025 — cautious.* "We're taking a wait-and-see approach as we evaluate various tariff scenarios." Visibility described as poor and expected to stay poor: "that forecasting is not very good… I would expect it to stay elusive."
- *July 2025 — defensive, confessional.* "We acknowledge that our recent performance has not demonstrated a clear path to [the target]." Revenue beat the guidance range but margin missed it, on "unforecasted ramp-up costs for a second HBM DRAM customer." Admitting an unforecasted cost is the opposite of promotion.
- *October 2025 — turning offensive.* "This disconnect is driving urgency across FormFactor." Specific, dated, numeric improvement commitments replace aspiration.
- *February 2026 — confident.* "We're making progress faster than expected."
- *April 2026 — assertive.* "These outstanding results exceed our target model on a quarterly run rate basis."
- *July 2026 — confident but self-limiting.* Record everything, and yet management volunteered that the headline margin is inflated by one-off items and that the new Texas plant will create ramp inefficiencies through 2027 before it helps in 2028.

The language shifted from cost-and-macro defence to share-gain offence over four quarters, and — this is the part that matters — guidance became *more* specific as confidence rose, not less. Promoters do the reverse.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**High, with one documented miss.**

The one clear miss is the June 2025 quarter: revenue exceeded the high end of the outlook range but "non-GAAP gross margin and overall profitability fell short of our outlook." Management named the causes rather than blaming the environment.

Everything after that was delivered or beaten. The October 2025 promise of a further 100 basis points became 290. The June 2026 quarter guided to $235–245 million of revenue and printed $258.2 million, with adjusted earnings of $0.82 per share against a $0.61 consensus. The screener's beat rate of five in seven quarters (71%) understates the recent record, because the misses are concentrated in the older, pre-turnaround quarters.

There is no sandbagging pattern in the classic sense — the company did not lowball for years and then beat. It genuinely underperformed for two years, said so, fixed it, and is now beating. The forward-looking caution is worth noting: management has repeatedly warned that quarter-to-quarter memory demand is volatile because "our customers' output is concentrated in a relatively small number of designs that are ramping up on short lead times" (July 2025).

**Reliability score: High** for the current management team's near-term guidance; **Medium** for multi-year targets, given the previous target model was carried publicly for years before it was met.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

Three questions recur and management has never fully answered any of them.

**Market share in high-bandwidth memory.** Asked directly in February 2026 what combined share is across the three memory makers, the chief executive answered: "we haven't quantified that for people… it's tough to parse through all the different HBM applications." He conceded "we have low enough incumbent share that there's a real opportunity" at the two non-lead customers. This is honest but it means the largest growth claim in the story cannot be independently sized.

**Backlog and visibility.** Analysts have probed this since April 2025 and there is no disclosure at all — no backlog figure, no book-to-bill ratio, no order-book commentary in any of the six calls reviewed. That is not evasion; it is structural. Probe cards are a device-specific consumable with short lead times, so the company genuinely has little forward visibility. The strategic implication is real and negative: **there is no order book to cushion a downturn.** Management demonstrated exactly this risk itself in October 2025, explaining that a personal-computer demand recovery produced no probe card revenue because customers "employ their existing probe card fleet" on legacy designs.

**Custom application-specific chips for the hyperscalers.** In October 2025 management volunteered an unusually candid answer: "we've got a smaller competitor who's doing a pretty good job serving that business… that's a hole in both our and our primary competitor's revenue that we're both working to fix." A year later this is still described as a growth area built on one multi-million-dollar design win. It is the one place where a smaller rival is beating the incumbents.

**What analysts are not pressing on and should be:** the durability of the 51% baseline margin once the Texas plant carries fixed cost, and the fact that two customers now account for 35% of revenue.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

🔴 **Insider selling is entirely one-sided.** Zero open-market purchases against 57 sales totalling 177,460 shares. At current prices that is roughly $21 million of stock sold by people who see the order flow, with nobody buying. Automatic plans explain some of it; the complete absence of a single purchase through a 14-percentage-point margin turnaround is still the loudest negative datapoint in this file.

🔴 **The headline margin is partly borrowed from one-off items.** Roughly a third of the June 2026 sequential gain and about 300 basis points of the September 2026 guide come from tariff refunds and scrap metal recovery. The company disclosed this itself, which is to its credit, but the difference between a 54% margin and a 51% margin compounds into a very different valuation.

🔴 **No backlog, by construction.** The Lane-A durability test — backlog against annual revenue, book-to-bill direction, firm orders versus framework agreements — cannot be applied to this company because it discloses none of it and structurally has none. Every quarter is sold from a standing start.

🔴 **Customer concentration is rising, not falling, despite the diversification narrative.** Two customers were 35% of revenue in the June 2026 quarter. The diversification story is real at the memory-maker level (three customers instead of one) but the top-line concentration went the other way.

🔴 **The Keystone Microtech partnership (28 July 2026) is an announcement without contractual substance.** The release names no dollar amount, no ownership stake, no capacity figure, and no dated milestone. It states each party keeps its own intellectual property. In this sector that is a press release, not a contract, and it should carry no valuation weight until numbers appear.

🔴 **A roughly $28 million non-operating charge in the March 2026 quarter** drove the gap between $47 million of operating income and $21 million of pre-tax income. The cause is not disclosed in the reviewed materials.

🟢 **The margin repair is genuine at the baseline level.** Strip every one-off item and the sustainable gross margin is about 51%, against 37.2% reported in the June 2025 quarter. That is a structural fourteen-point gain built on cost reduction, volume absorption and mix — management attributed only a minority of the change to volume when asked directly (October 2025, chief financial officer Aric McKinnis: "the volume, for example, is the minority of the change").

🟢 **Every named milestone has landed.** The graphics-processor qualification tracked from pilot (October 2025) to nearing completion (April 2026) to complete with production shipments in the September 2026 quarter. Co-packaged optics revenue was guided to $10–20 million in April 2026 and raised above $20 million three months later. That is a management team whose dated statements convert.

🟢 **The balance sheet is genuinely clean.** Net cash of $326 million (cash and investments of $345.6 million against $19 million of debt, including capitalised leases). Piotroski score 7 of 9, Altman solvency score 23.2. Share count grew 2.6% year over year — normal equity compensation dilution, not serial issuance. This is not a company that will need to finance the Texas build with paper.

🟢 **Independent third-party validation.** Ranked number one global supplier in both test subsystems and focused chip-making equipment in the TechInsights 2025 customer satisfaction survey (cited July 2025), and awarded Intel's 2026 Epic Supplier Award (cited April 2026).

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**A chief financial officer change mid-story.** Shai Shahar was replaced by Aric McKinnis effective 12 August 2025 — precisely at the trough quarter. The new voice speaks differently: where the previous finance chief discussed tariffs as something to wait out, McKinnis immediately reframed margin as a controllable ("our main path for gross margin improvement is not pricing, but rather more long-term controllable items on the cost front"). The turnaround begins the quarter he arrives. That is either good timing or good hiring, and either way the operational language became measurably more precise.

**Management explicitly declines to lean on price.** Asked in February 2026 whether pricing could drive margin, the chief executive gave a nuanced yes — "it is an interesting time in the industry where the elements to share in the increased value the industry is creating are more than they have been in the past" — and the finance chief immediately walked it back to cost. A promoter would have taken the pricing-power question and run with it. They did not.

**The customer mix quietly inverted.** In February 2026 management disclosed that its historically largest customer, a large microprocessor manufacturer, "was not a 10% customer in either the fourth quarter or for 2025 overall" — while total revenue set records. The business rebuilt itself around memory and data-centre networking without the old anchor customer. By April 2026 a high-performance-compute leader had become a 10% customer for the first time.

**Terminology drift toward the picks-and-shovels framing.** "Test intensity" and "test complexity" became the two load-bearing phrases from July 2025 onward, backed by a specific and checkable claim: high-bandwidth memory customers spend nearly 1% of that product's revenue on probe cards, roughly double the industry-wide rate, and each generation adds another 20–25% of test intensity on a like-for-like basis. This is the honest core of the story — the company sells more consumable per wafer as chips get harder to test, regardless of who wins the chip race.

**Quantum computing has receded from the narrative.** Present in both 2025 calls, one product launch in early 2026, absent from the July 2026 call. The chain assignment for this name is quantum computing, but on the evidence quantum is a small slice of a Systems segment that was itself 19% of fiscal 2025 revenue. The real driver is memory and advanced packaging for artificial-intelligence chips.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.**

Capital allocation over the reviewed period has been disciplined and coherent. Three deployments, all defensible: a minority equity stake in FICT (the Japanese maker of the multilayer organic substrates that go inside advanced probe cards, spun out of Fujitsu), taken alongside MBK Partners rather than as an outright acquisition — management's stated logic was that this was "more capital-efficient, lower-risk, and faster" than buying or building, and that is correct for a supply-security investment. Second, the opportunistic purchase of a brownfield clean-room facility in Farmers Branch, Texas, bought when few such assets existed, with state incentives attached. Third, the shutdown of the Baldwin Park site. There was no buyback at the top and no dividend initiated into a cyclical peak. The one criticism is that the Texas commitment of $140–170 million was sized against an eventual doubling of the business, which is a 2030 assumption underwriting a 2026 cash outflow.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing:** *the durable gross margin is 51%, not the 53.3% reported or the 54% guided.* Everything about the valuation — the multiple this business deserves, the earnings power in 2027, whether the 2030 model of $5 per share is reachable — turns on which number you underwrite. Management told you which one is real; the share price is being set by the other one.

**Economic moat: NARROW, and WIDENING.** The moat type is switching costs reinforced by scale in a duopoly. Each probe card is qualified to a single customer chip design through a months-long reliability process — the graphics-processor qualification described in these calls ran from pilot in October 2025 to completion in July 2026, roughly nine months of hundreds of wafers and multiple touchdowns before a single volume order could be competed for. Once qualified, the incumbent holds the design. Above a certain performance threshold, management states "there's really only two suppliers worldwide that can do that," and the SmartMatrix architecture is described as the only production-proven design combining high parallelism with 10-gigabit-plus test speed. The evidence says widening rather than eroding: return on invested capital rose to 11.2% on a trailing basis from the mid-single digits, gross margin expanded fourteen points on 32% revenue growth, and share was gained at all three memory makers simultaneously. It is narrow rather than wide because the product is a consumable resold every design cycle, a credible smaller rival is winning the custom-chip niche outright, and Technoprobe remains a genuine competitor at the leading edge.

**Secular threat: manageable.** No structural force impairs the terminal value here. The company's product is the physical act of touching a wafer to test it, which every new packaging architecture makes *more* necessary, not less — stacked memory with sixteen layers must have every layer tested or the whole stack is scrap. The real risk is cyclical rather than secular: this is a short-cycle consumable with no order book, levered to memory capital spending, and the cheapness or dearness of the stock at any moment is a cycle call, not a terminal-value call.
