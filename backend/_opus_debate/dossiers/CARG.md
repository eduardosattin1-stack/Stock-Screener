# CarGurus (CARG) - forensic dossier (debate run 2026-11-09)

Quarters read: Q3-2025 (2025-11-07), Q4-2025 (2026-02-19), Q1-2026 (2026-05-07), Q2-2026 (2026-08-06), Q3-2026 (2026-11-05). Price used: $34.55 (Nasdaq, USD, FMP quote 2026-11-09).

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC
In late 2025 the story was "a two-sided marketplace with scale and data", with Marketplace revenue up about 14% in Q3-2025 and the CarOffer wholesale arm being wound down. Through 2026 the emphasis has moved from listings to software and data sold to dealers: PriceVantage (pricing, launched October 2025), VINMAX (merchandising boosts), Shopper Signals (lead scoring) and, in the week of the Q3-2026 call, StockVantage (which cars to buy at auction). Management frames these as roughly doubling its addressable market, from about $3.5B dealers spend on marketplaces to a further ~$4B spent on software and data (Q3-2025 call). The consumer side moved from "most visited site" to AI search (CG Discover, the "Guru" assistant) and an app inside the large chatbot platforms. The tone toward the macro shifted from "sheltered harbor" (Q3-2025) to an open admission in Q3-2026 that "dealer enthusiasm has not returned to 2025 levels" because of dealer margin pressure and stretched consumer affordability.

## 2. CLAIMS vs FINANCIALS (forensic)
| Claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Double-digit growth continues | Q3-2026 revenue $258M, +12% year over year, top of guidance; full-year 2025 continuing revenue +14% | Matches |
| Profitable growth | Q3-2026 adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization, excluding stock pay) $89.7M, a 35% margin; GAAP net income from continuing operations $52.8M, up only 2% | Partly - GAAP profit grew much slower than revenue as investment rose |
| Strong cash generation | Operating cash flow Q3-2026 $80.8M; trailing free cash flow about $325M (FMP) | Matches, but flattered: cash taxes paid were only about $9M over the last four quarters versus deferred tax charges of about $25M, and stock pay runs about $52M a year |
| Shareholder returns | Buybacks of $174M in Q1-2026, $28M in Q2, $23M in Q3; new $250M authorization on 2026-11-05 | Matches; share count falling |
| The reported revenue decline is not the core decaying | The 3-year revenue CAGR of -17% is the CarOffer wind-down (2022 revenue $1.66B included wholesale); continuing Marketplace grew every year | Matches |

The headline "11x free cash flow" overstates the true owner yield. After normal cash taxes and stock pay, owner earnings are roughly $200M a year, about $2.25 a share - nearer 15x the price.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY
Stable, slightly more defensive. Q3-2025: "we don't face a lot of pressure". Q4-2025: "2025 was a pivotal year". Q3-2026: dealer activity "moved in the right direction" but affordability "remains stretched" and "rising borrowing costs have added to that pressure". Language has moved from offense-only to offense plus "disciplined with our investments". A new CFO, Matthew Mandel, joined in late October 2026.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY
High. The scan shows earnings beats in 7 of 7 quarters; Q3-2026 revenue landed at the high end of guidance and adjusted EBITDA near the high end; EPS $0.70 against a $0.66 consensus (Zacks, 2026-11-05). The Q3-2026 result included a $1.3M one-off real-estate tax true-up benefit, small. No sandbagging pattern beyond the usual conservative ranges.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS
- AI disintermediation (UBS, 2026-11-05): if a shopper finds a CarGurus listing through a chatbot and goes straight to the dealer, CarGurus gets no credit. Management's answer - car buying is a multi-week research process and shoppers still come back to the site - is plausible but unproven.
- Growth versus margin in 2026: asked repeatedly since Q3-2025; management prioritizes product investment and gives no margin floor.
- Dealer health: inventory up about 10% and dealer margins pressured; analysts keep probing churn risk.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS
Red flag: GAAP net income grew only 2% in Q3-2026 while revenue grew 12% (Q3-2026).
Red flag: open-market insider activity is one buy against 33 sells over the scan window.
Red flag: cash taxes are running far below the book tax rate; free cash flow will compress when deferred taxes reverse.
Red flag: AI search can route shoppers around listing sites; management concedes attribution is "pretty indirect" in that case (Q3-2026).
Green flag: US net dealer additions of 251 in Q3-2026, the strongest quarter of 2026.
Green flag: US paying dealers buying at least one product beyond listings up about 16% year over year (Q3-2026).
Green flag: no borrowings; $165M cash against $182M of capitalized leases (2026-09-30).
Green flag: consistent buybacks shrinking the share count.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS
"Marketplace" as a word is giving way to "platform", "workflow" and "intelligence" - a deliberate re-labeling toward software multiples. The CFO change is new; first-call commentary was limited to a factual answer on the tax true-up. Management now tracks "AI referral traffic and AI visibility" as a metric, which tells you the board is watching the chatbot channel closely.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)
Trajectory: stable. The core marketplace keeps growing low double digits with a 35% adjusted margin and cash returned through buybacks. The single most important point: on normalized taxes and after stock pay the business earns about $2.25 a share, so at $34.55 the stock already trades around 15x owner earnings - fair, not cheap.

Moat: a network moat (the largest US shopper audience attracts the most dealers and vice versa), narrow rather than wide - returns on invested capital about 11%, and US competitors (Cars.com, Autotrader, Facebook Marketplace) keep dealer pricing power in check. Trend stable: share gains in traffic and dealers, gross margin 89%. Secular threat: material, not terminal - AI assistants could commoditize the discovery step, but dealers still pay for qualified leads and CarGurus is building into the dealer workflow. The cheapness relative to Auto Trader is mostly a fair reflection of a narrower moat and this AI risk, not a clear mispricing.
