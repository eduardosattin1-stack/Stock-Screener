# Comcast (CMCSA) — a deep dive on management and the setup
*Debate run 2026-11-09. Transcripts: Q3 2025 (2025-10-30) through Q3 2026 (2026-10-22). Live price $24.51 (Nasdaq, USD, 2026-11-09).*

**The short version:** Comcast is a cash machine whose core business, home broadband, is slowly shrinking under attack from fixed wireless (home internet sold by mobile carriers) and fiber overbuilders. Management has been unusually candid about that, and it is spending heavily — price cuts, a five-year price guarantee, free mobile lines — to stop the bleeding. The content side (parks, studios, Peacock, Sky) is in better shape and is being split off into a separate public company by the middle of 2027. The numbers corroborate the candor but not yet the turnaround: free cash flow is real (about $17.1B over the trailing twelve months to 2026-09-30), but connectivity profit is still falling every quarter.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

- **Q3 2025 (2025-10-30):** the story was "a deliberate investment phase." Mike Cavanagh said broadband would end up as a market of "2 multi-gig symmetrical providers," conceded "most of our footprint will eventually be overbuilt," and guided that the decline in Connectivity & Platforms EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization — a cash-profit proxy) would "build slightly over the next several quarters." Leadership change: Steve Croney named head of connectivity.
- **Q4 2025 (2026-01-29):** the theme became execution of "the most significant go-to-market shift in our company's history" — four national speed tiers, a five-year price guarantee, a 12-month free mobile line. Management promised that "a meaningful portion" of free lines would convert to paid in the second half of 2026 and that connectivity profit would improve in the back half. The Versant cable-network spin was completed.
- **Q1 2026 (2026-04-23):** the high-water mark of the story — broadband net losses "improved by more than 100,000 year-over-year, the first year-over-year improvement since the fourth quarter of 2020," record wireless adds, record Olympic/Super Bowl advertising.
- **Q2 2026 (2026-07-23):** the narrative pivoted to structure. Three weeks earlier the company had announced the separation of NBCUniversal and Sky into a standalone company, targeted in "approximately 1 year," and the return of Michael Angelakis as the future Comcast chief executive. Peacock reached profitability.
- **Q3 2026 (2026-10-22):** tone turned defensive on connectivity. Management described fixed wireless as "aggressive," fiber pricing as having "pockets of irrational pricing," flagged an unusually large wave of two-year promotional roll-offs, and announced "the most significant operating and cost transformation in our company's history" with a roughly $1B fourth-quarter restructuring charge (reported 2026-10-22). Connectivity revenue fell about 2.6% and connectivity EBITDA about 4.4% year over year. Parks: Orlando softness guided to persist "at least next quarter and in the first half of next year."

The arc runs from "invest, then grow" to "split the company and cut costs." The growth promise for connectivity has been pushed out; the value-creation promise has shifted toward the separation.

## 2. CLAIMS vs FINANCIALS (forensic)

| Management claim | What the numbers say | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Connectivity decline will "build slightly," then improve in the second half of 2026 (Q3 2025, Q4 2025 calls) | Connectivity EBITDA fell ~3.7% in Q3 2025, ~5.8% in Q2 2026, ~4.4% in Q3 2026 — it did build, and the Q3 2026 narrowing is the first sign of the promised improvement, but it is still falling | Partly delivered |
| Broadband losses turning (Q1 2026: first year-over-year improvement since Q4 2020) | The Q1 improvement did not become a trend; Q3 2026 was hurt by promotional roll-offs and aggressive competitor pricing | Not yet corroborated |
| Free lines will convert to paid in the second half of 2026 (Q4 2025) | Q2 2026: "positive early traction"; no conversion percentage has ever been disclosed | Unverified — a recurring non-number |
| Peacock will approach profitability (Q1 2026) | Peacock reached profitability in Q2 2026 and stayed profitable in Q3 2026 | Delivered |
| Cash generation is durable | Revenue flat for three years (3-year CAGR +0.6%; 2025 revenue $123.7B vs $123.7B in 2024); free cash flow $17.1B trailing twelve months, up ~25% in fiscal 2025 | Corroborated |
| Earnings growth | Fiscal 2025 EPS $5.39 (+30%) is flattered by one-offs and by the post-Versant comparison; trailing twelve-month diluted EPS is $3.16, and consensus forward EPS growth is only ~1% | The headline EPS growth overstates the earning power |

Net debt is about $79.8B (borrowings $87.4B less cash $7.6B at 2026-09-30, excluding leases), roughly 2.3-2.5 times EBITDA on our estimate — manageable, but it limits buybacks, which press coverage on 2026-11-08 described as on hold.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Declining on connectivity, rising on content.** Q3 2025: "intensely competitive environment, which we do not expect to change anytime soon" — candid but confident. Q1 2026: "early signs that the strategic pivot ... is underway" — the most offensive language of the run. Q3 2026: "near-term financial pressure," "pockets of irrational pricing," and a shift to cost and efficiency language ("structurally improve our cost base"). Guidance specificity has fallen: in Q4 2025 the chief financial officer said "I will stop short of giving a full EBITDA guide," and no connectivity framework has been offered since. On content, the tone is steadily more confident (Peacock profitable, The Odyssey, NBA ratings).

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

Management gives directional rather than numerical guidance, which makes it hard to catch them out — and that is itself the point. Directional calls on the trough (the decline would "build") were accurate; calls on the recovery (broadband turning, back-half improvement) have been only partly delivered. Peacock guidance was met. The screener's 7-of-7 quarterly earnings-per-share beats reflects low consensus bars and cost control more than operating momentum. **Reliability: Medium.**

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- The free-line conversion rate: asked repeatedly (Q1 2026, Q2 2026), never quantified.
- Post-separation leverage, dividend policy and exchange ratio: asked in Q2 2026; answer was "nothing to add." The dividend split between the two companies is the largest unanswered question for the yield-holder base.
- Orlando parks softness and Epic Universe capacity: management now guides softness into the first half of 2027.
- Promotional roll-offs in the legacy base (Q3 2026): management admits it is "a pain point."

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

**Red flags**
- Q3 2026: a new round of the "most significant" transformation, with a ~$1B charge, one year after the last "most significant" go-to-market shift — the fixes keep getting bigger.
- Q3 2026: competitive language escalated ("irrational pricing"); peer Charter reported wider internet losses on 2026-11-06, so the pressure is industry-wide, not easing.
- Q2/Q3 2026: no capital-return framework for the two companies; buybacks reported paused.
- Insiders: 3 open-market sales (about 70,700 shares) and zero open-market buys.

**Green flags**
- Q2-Q3 2026: Peacock profitable two quarters running.
- Q3 2026: connectivity EBITDA decline narrowed to ~4.4% from ~5.8% in Q2.
- Business services generates "nearly $6 billion annually in EBITDA" with enterprise growth.
- Wireless: record line additions through 2026; over 10 million lines.
- The separation creates two cleaner stories and a potential sum-of-the-parts (valuing each business line separately) unlock.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- "Satellite is not yet a material factor" (Q3 2026) is the first time management has named satellite as a future competitor — a widening of the threat set.
- The language moved from "return to growth" to "position the business for return to growth over the long term" — a quiet extension of the timeline.
- Leadership churn: Croney (connectivity, 2026), Cavanagh co-CEO (2026), Angelakis returning as Comcast CEO (2027), a new NBCUniversal chief financial officer (Chris Halpin). The new voices emphasise cost and agility rather than growth.
- The proposed ITV acquisition for Sky signals the media company will be acquisitive, adding execution risk on the content side.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: PIVOTING.** The company is turning from a conglomerate defending cable into two separate companies, with an aggressive cost program on the connectivity side.

**The single most important thing to weigh:** the cash is real (about $4.80 of free cash flow per share against a $24.51 price), but the cash comes from a connectivity business whose profit is still falling about 4% a year. The investment works if that decline flattens; it is a slow value trap if it does not. The fourth-quarter 2026 results (late January 2027) are the next clean read.

**Moat:** scale-cost and network (a sunk, capital-efficient hybrid fiber-coax network) plus switching costs from bundling broadband with mobile. It is real but narrow, and the evidence says it is eroding: return on invested capital is only 6.8%, connectivity revenue and profit are falling, and management itself expects most of its footprint to be overbuilt. **Secular threat:** fixed-wireless and fiber overbuild (cord-cutting/broadband overbuild) is a material, not terminal, threat — the business still earns large cash margins and can defend share with price, and the content half is not exposed in the same way. The cheapness is partly a fear-print and partly a fair price for a shrinking core; at roughly 5 times free cash flow, the market is pricing more decline than the latest numbers show, but the burden of proof sits with management.
