# SoftwareOne Holding (SWON.SW) - a deep dive on management and the setup

Prepared 2026-11-16. Sources: the full-year 2025 results call (2026-03-31), the first-half 2026 results call (2026-08-26), the third-quarter 2026 trading update (released 2026-11-11), the screener's financial block and a live SIX quote of CHF 8.78 (2026-11-16). All figures are in Swiss francs (CHF) on the Zurich listing.

SoftwareOne resells software licences and cloud capacity - mostly Microsoft, increasingly Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google and others - to about 70,000 corporate and public-sector customers, and wraps advice and managed services around those sales. It merged with Crayon, a Norwegian competitor, on 1 July 2025, which raised the share count from roughly 152 million to roughly 215 million.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

- **March 2026 (full-year 2025): "we returned to growth".** The story was repair. Like-for-like growth (the merged company compared with what the two firms did separately a year earlier) was only 1.4% for 2025, but Q4 2025 reached 11%. Management leaned on three promises: North America (down 12.6% in 2025) would return to growth in 2026, Microsoft's enterprise-agreement incentive cuts were behind them ("we do not see any headwinds effect"), and synergies would reach CHF 80-100 million. Google Cloud global distribution (won February 2026) was introduced as the new growth lever for the channel arm (sales through 12,000 resellers).
- **August 2026 (first half 2026): "integration is now substantially completed".** The story moved from repair to delivery: 11.6% like-for-like growth, a 24.9% adjusted EBITDA margin (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, before one-off costs), CHF 100 million of run-rate synergies six months early, North America up 8.6%. A 2030 target of a 28%-plus margin appeared, with capital spending stepping up to about 8% of revenue in 2027-28 for AI and automation, "funded from the release of net working capital".
- **November 2026 (Q3 trading update): "solid", not "exceptional".** Growth slowed to 7.4% at constant currency, as flagged in August (about 4 points of first-half growth came from customers renewing early ahead of Microsoft's July price rise). The margin guide was raised from above 23% to above 24%. The language is noticeably calmer, and the channel arm, the star of the first half (+35.6%), grew only 7.3% in the quarter. A new theme appeared: Microsoft enterprise-agreement-to-cloud-subscription conversion volumes in 2027-28 "could be 2-3 times higher than in 2026".

The arc is consistent: each call narrows the claims to things that can be checked, and the next one checks them.

Where the screener's numbers diverge: the scan shows a net margin of 0.1%, return on equity of 1.8%, an Altman Z-score (a bankruptcy-risk score) of 0.5 and trailing free cash flow of CHF 585m. Most of this is noise. The 2025 net income of CHF 0.9m was struck after merger costs and purchase-price amortisation; H1 2026 alone earned CHF 54.3m. The Z-score is distorted by CHF 4.5bn of pass-through receivables against CHF 4.5bn of pass-through payables. The CHF 585m free cash flow is wrong: it captures year-end supplier-payment timing; the company figure is about CHF 200m. The live balance-sheet correction (net debt CHF 483m gross of other financial assets, CHF 408m as the company reports it, 30 June 2026) is right, and the scan's "net cash" never existed.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY - Rising, then steadying

- March 2026: cautiously confident, defensive on North America ("2025 was a disappointing year in North America").
- August 2026: the most assertive call - "I'm proud of our performance", "exceptional" growth in channel, "absolutely no material impact" from Microsoft.
- November 2026: "solid third quarter". The guidance raise is margin only; revenue growth guidance stays mid-to-high single digit. Confidence is now expressed through numbers rather than adjectives, which is healthier.

Specificity has risen across the three releases: synergy counts, integration-cost ranges and a cash-conversion target are all checkable.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY - High

Every dated operational commitment made in March 2026 was met or beaten by November. The screen's earnings-beat rate of 2 of 7 quarters reflects statutory earnings that were swamped by merger accounting, not missed operating guidance. One caution: a firm that guided a 23-25% range, then "above 23%", then "above 24%" may be managing expectations low (sandbagging), which helps credibility but means some of each beat was banked in advance.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- **Sustainability of first-half growth** (Kepler, 2026-08-26): management conceded about 4 points came from early renewals. Q3's 7.4% confirmed the slowdown.
- **Microsoft incentives** (UBS, both calls): asked twice; answered firmly. This is the single largest dependency and the one analysts keep returning to.
- **Capital spending** (Berenberg/Deutsche Bank): the 2027-28 step-up to about 8% of revenue is to be "cash neutral" through working-capital release. That is unproven and in a pass-through business is partly a choice about supplier timing.
- **Tax** (Zürcher Kantonalbank): a 42% statutory rate in H1 against a 20% cash rate; 30% guided for 2026.
- Not asked, and not answered: what drove the channel slowdown in Q3, and how much of the lower Q3 margin (21.1%) is seasonal mix.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- 🟢 Synergies over-delivered and early (2026-08-26), and integration costs guided down.
- 🟢 Adjusted-versus-reported gap narrowed as promised (CHF 33m to CHF 18.4m, H1 2026).
- 🟢 Margin guidance raised after the Microsoft margin cut took effect (2026-11-11).
- 🟢 North America and the direct business returned to growth as promised (H1 and Q3 2026).
- 🔴 Channel growth fell from 35.6% (H1) to 7.3% (Q3 2026) - the part of the business the bull case values at a premium.
- 🔴 Net debt rose from CHF 369m (December 2025) to CHF 408m (June 2026) despite the cash generation; the dividend per share was roughly halved after the merger.
- 🔴 Capital spending roughly doubles in 2027-28; free cash flow will fall unless working capital is released.
- 🔴 The H1 transcript contains a garbled sentence on adjustments ("CHF 60.9 million of the total of CHF 18.4 million"); we rely on the CHF 18.4m total, which reconciles with the reported-to-adjusted gap.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- The leadership shrank from co-chief operating officers (Raphael Erb and Melissa Mulholland, March 2026) to a single chief executive (Erb, August 2026); Mulholland has left. The new executive board adds regional heads and a channel/vendor leader. The voice is now more operational and less evangelical.
- "Conversion volumes in 2027 and 2028 could be 2-3 times higher than in 2026" (2026-11-11) quietly re-frames Microsoft's push of large customers from enterprise agreements to its cloud-subscription programme as a multi-year tailwind rather than a one-off - a claim worth testing in March 2027.
- AI vendor diversification is being built: OpenAI select partner (October 2026), Anthropic select partner tier (2026-11-09). Small today, but it reduces single-vendor dependence.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: strengthening.** The merger has been integrated on time and under budget, and the margin has risen about 4 points in a year.

The single most important thing to weigh: **how much of the adjusted profit reaches shareholders as cash once capital spending doubles in 2027-28.** At CHF 8.78 the company is valued at about CHF 1.89bn of equity plus CHF 0.4-0.5bn of net debt, roughly 6 times 2026 adjusted EBITDA of about CHF 400m and under 10 times trailing free cash flow of about CHF 200m. If cash conversion holds near 60% through the investment phase, that is cheap; if conversion falls toward 35-40%, it is roughly fair.

**Moat:** narrow - switching costs and scale in licence management (70,000 customers, Gartner and IDC leader in software asset management), and vendor authorisations (first global Google Cloud distributor). Returns are rising off a low base (ROIC about 11%) and margins are widening, so the trend is stable-to-improving, not eroding. **Secular threat: material, not terminal.** Two forces: Microsoft can and does re-cut partner economics at will, and AI-driven productivity deflates the price of the labour in the services arm (about half of revenue, 8.5% margin). Neither shrinks the base today: services grew 11.2% in Q3. The cheapness reads as a post-merger, leverage-and-noise discount rather than a structurally shrinking base.
