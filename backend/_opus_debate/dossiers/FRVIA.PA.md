# Forvia (FRVIA.PA) — a deep dive on management and the setup

*Prepared 2026-11-10 (refresh of the 2026-10-12 deep dive). Price used: EUR 8.78 (Euronext Paris, FMP live quote 2026-11-10; 52-week range EUR 8.01-15.03). Transcripts read: full-year 2023 (2024-02-19), Q1 2024 sales (2024-04-18), H1 2024 (2024-07-28), H1 2025 (2025-07-28), H1 2026 (2026-07-31).*

**Bottom line:** Forvia is a large French auto-parts supplier (seats, interiors, electronics, lighting, exhaust systems) that has spent three years paying down the debt it took on to buy the German supplier HELLA in 2022. The new management team (CEO Martin Fischer since March 2025) is credible on cost and cash, and the numbers back it: operating margin and cash flow have improved for three straight half-years. But the shareholders own a thin slice on top of a large debt pile — roughly EUR 5.7 billion of net debt against a market value of about EUR 1.7 billion — so small changes in what the business is worth swing the share price violently. The one division that is clearly going the wrong way is HELLA Lighting.

## Update since the last look (2026-10-12 to 2026-11-10)

- **Q3 2026 sales (published 2026-11-02):** about EUR 5.08 billion, down 0.6% organically against global car production down about 1.6% - a modest outperformance. Europe grew 4.1% organically; China fell 17.2%. Seating, the largest division, fell 4.4%; Electronics rose 7.0%. Nine-month sales were down 1.5% organically. Full-year 2026 guidance (sales EUR 20-21 billion at constant currency) was confirmed, and management reiterated that the Interiors sale to Apollo funds will close in Q4 2026 with all regulatory approvals obtained (Ideal Investisseur, Teleborsa, Finimize, 2026-11-02). UBS kept a neutral rating, calling the quarter in line.
- **What it means:** the quarter confirms the plan rather than changing it. The China decline (-17%) is the new soft spot to watch alongside Lighting, which a sales-only release does not break out at margin level. No net-debt or margin update was given; those arrive with full-year results in February 2027.
- **Price:** EUR 8.78, essentially where it was a month ago (EUR 8.64), after a dip to a 52-week low of about EUR 8.01 in between.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

- **2024 (Koller era, "Power25"):** the story was growth plus deleveraging. February 2024 led with "organic growth of 14%", a 5.3% operating margin, EUR 31 billion of orders and a return to a dividend. Ambitions were broad: hydrogen ("speeding up hydrogen activities", the Symbio fuel-cell gigafactory), sustainable materials (MATERI'ACT, "EUR 2 billion of sales by 2030"), and the EU-FORWARD European restructuring (EUR 500 million savings by 2028).
- **2025 (Fischer takes over):** the tone narrowed to "performance, transformation, culture" with a single overriding priority: "deleveraging the group" (H1 2025). Hydrogen was quietly demoted after Stellantis exited Symbio — "we are slowing down our own hydrogen investments" — with a provision taken. Project SIMPLIFY (EUR 110 million of overhead savings by 2028) was added, and "sizable disposals" became the main lever.
- **2026 (IGNITE plan):** the portfolio was cut. Interiors is being sold to Apollo funds (signed April 2026 at an enterprise value of about EUR 1.82 billion), Exteriors is already gone, and results are now reported excluding both. New adjacencies appeared — defense drones ("an interceptor drone", target 1,000 a month by H1 2027). The growth date has been pushed out: management says what it wins now "is going to show up on the streets with SOPs in 2028".

The arc is a classic shrink-to-fix: from a growth-and-ESG conglomerate story to a smaller, cash-focused company whose growth is promised for 2028 and beyond.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Stable, more disciplined.** Koller (2024) was expansive and promotional ("it works. And we are probably not at the end of the story"). Fischer (2025-2026) is drier and more operational — "we hold the organization really tight and accountable" (H1 2025); "firmly on track to deliver our full year guidance" (H1 2026). Language has moved from offense (growth, hydrogen, ESG) to defense (cost, cash, disposals). On margin guidance in H1 2026 he hedged: "a band that we can fill" (6.0-6.5%, with H1 at the bottom of it).

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Medium.** Cash and leverage targets have been met or beaten (2023 targets met; H1 2025 cash "better than expected"; H1 2026 net debt down EUR 0.5 billion, the largest organic half-year reduction since HELLA). Against that, the older ambitions slipped: the "below 1.5x by end-2026" leverage target now relies on the Interiors proceeds, hydrogen was written down, Lighting has missed for four half-years, and the screener records only 2 of 6 recent quarters beating expectations (33%). Management delivers on what it controls (costs, capex, working capital) and misses on what the market controls (volumes, Lighting demand).

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- **Gross debt and cash balance** (credit investors, H1 2025): management answered with targets (investment-grade eligibility around 1.2x; cash down to EUR 3.5 billion), not a date for refinancing the big bond stack.
- **Free-cash-flow guidance** (Roce Capital, 2024 and 2025): analysts repeatedly asked why guidance was not raised; management stayed "prudent".
- **Order intake vs the Capital Markets Day sales path** (H1 2026): orders of EUR 13.4 billion in H1 imply far more than the EUR 21-22 billion annual target, but revenue only follows from 2028 — investors are being asked to wait.
- **Lighting** and **China competition** recur; answers are about process ("intimacy", "proximity") rather than numbers.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- Red: HELLA Lighting margin collapsed to 0.3% in H1 2026, with organic sales down 4.7%.
- Red: net loss of EUR 2.09 billion in 2025; book value per share has shrunk about 19% a year over five years.
- Red: Altman-Z (a bankruptcy-risk score) of 1.6, in the distress zone; equity is a small slice of a large capital structure.
- Red: Interiors sold at about 3.1x EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) — a low price that crystallises a loss.
- Red: growth is deferred to 2028; defense drones are a small, unproven adjacency.
- Green: three consecutive half-years of better margin and cash; customer claims down 33% (H1 2026); North American plant problems fixed.
- Green: order intake +15% in H1 2026, with 30% from Asian, Indian and commercial-vehicle customers; China order book-to-bill of 3x.
- Green: Interiors deal has US, EU and French antitrust clearance and works-council consultation completed (H1 2026 call); all approvals obtained and Q4 2026 closing reiterated with Q3 sales (2026-11-02).
- Green: Q3 2026 organic sales -0.6% vs global production about -1.6% (2026-11-02); 2026 guidance confirmed.
- Red: China organic sales -17.2% in Q3 2026 (2026-11-02), despite the strong Chinese order book; the 2028 growth ramp is not yet visible in revenue.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- The vocabulary changed from "Power25" and "outperformance" to "IGNITE", "SIMPLIFY" and "accountability" — a reset, not a continuation.
- The CFO's mid-term cash target (EUR 3.5 billion) implies using cash to repay gross debt — a creditor-first capital allocation; no talk of buybacks or a meaningful dividend.
- "Value cluster" vs "growth cluster" framing at the 2026 Capital Markets Day suggests further disposals of the value cluster (Clean Mobility, possibly Lighting assets) are possible.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: turning (pivoting).** The company is mid-way through a shrink-and-repair plan run by credible operators. The single most important thing to weigh: the equity is a leveraged stub. With about EUR 5.7 billion of net debt, a HELLA minority stake held by outside shareholders, and pensions sitting ahead of a EUR 1.7 billion market value, half a turn of EBITDA multiple moves fair value per share by roughly EUR 6 — more than half today's price. The upside is real if deleveraging continues, but so is the downside if European auto volumes or Lighting disappoint.

**Moat:** narrow — scale and switching costs on multi-year vehicle programmes (a supplier is designed in for a car's whole life), but returns on capital of about 3.5% are below the cost of capital, so the moat is not earning a premium. **Secular threat:** material, not terminal — the move to electric vehicles shrinks the exhaust business, and Chinese suppliers and carmakers squeeze pricing in Lighting and electronics. The cheapness is part fear and part a genuinely leveraged, low-return base.
