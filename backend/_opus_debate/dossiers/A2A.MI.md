# A2A S.p.A. (A2A.MI) - a deep dive on management and the setup
*Prepared 2026-11-16. Calls reviewed: H1 2024 (2024-07-30), FY 2024 (2025-03-20), Q1 2025 (2025-05-13), H1 2025 (2025-08-05) and nine months 2026 (2026-11-10). Price on the Milan listing: EUR 2.265 (FMP quote, 2026-11-16).*

**The takeaway.** A2A is a well-run Lombardy multi-utility (a company that runs power generation, electricity and gas networks, waste and recycling plants, district heating and an energy retail business under one roof). Management has delivered or confirmed its guidance at every print we can check, and the 2026 targets were confirmed again on 2026-11-10. But that latest confirmation came with a clear tilt to the low end of the range, net debt is rising faster than profit, and a higher regional business tax (IRAP) and much higher hydro concession fees are leaking value to the Italian state. The business is solid; it is not getting cheaper to own.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

- **2024 (H1 2024 call, 2024-07-30):** a "happy holiday" story. Rain filled the reservoirs, hydro plants ran above 99% availability, and hedged power prices were high. The CEO, Renato Mazzoncini, framed the year as proof of the plan, with a "positive hydro contribution" carrying the numbers.
- **2025 (FY 2024 and H1 2025 calls):** the story moved to regulated networks, meaning businesses whose returns are set by the energy regulator. A2A bought Enel's grid in Milan and Brescia provinces (Duereti, live from January 2025), sold part of its gas network to Ascopiave for EUR 430 million (cashed "exactly in the 3rd of June" 2025), and signed a 17-year liquefied-gas supply deal with BP. Management called this "business risk profile optimization": less weather and commodity risk, more regulated earnings.
- **2026 (nine-month call, 2026-11-10):** the tone turns defensive. The headline is resilience against "a challenging backdrop": the second-worst hydro year in a decade, "extreme weather conditions", price volatility and "higher hydroelectric concession fees". The pitch is now that the diversified model limits the damage, not that it compounds.
- **Dropped or deferred:** the data-centre opportunity (two sites with land secured) still has no dated monetisation step. Asset rotation is now "opportunistic" with "no specific plan" (CFO Luca Moroni, 2026-11-10).

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Declining, from confident to defensive.** In 2024 the CEO spoke of "the reason for being happy". In 2025 the tone was execution ("with a very clear punctuality"). On 2026-11-10 the opening word was resilience: the results show "how our diversified business model has helped us navigate a challenging environment" (investor relations, 2026-11-10). Management also spent a large part of the Q&A rebutting a politician's claim that hydro is sold at EUR 350 per megawatt hour and explaining a possible windfall levy on fixed-price contracts (the CEO estimated "EUR 30 million", stressing "it's difficult really to say a number"). That is a company playing defence on policy.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

- 2026 guidance (adjusted EBITDA EUR 2.21-2.25bn, adjusted net profit EUR 630-660m) was set in November 2025 and confirmed on 2026-03-17, 2026-05-14, 2026-07-30 and again on 2026-11-10 (Il Sole 24 Ore and Teleborsa, 2026-11-10). On 2026-11-10 management pointed to the low end of the range because of weak hydro output.
- In 2025 they delivered roughly in line with plan; the H1 2025 call called the EUR 1.2bn first half "quite in line with our target".
- The screener's "beat 0 of 1 quarter" reading rests on a single quarter and is not informative.
- **Reliability: High** on headline delivery; the steering to the low end is honest disclosure rather than a miss. There is no sandbagging pattern (setting easy targets to beat): guidance has tended to be met, not beaten.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- **Hydro concessions after 2029:** analysts keep asking how renewals will work. The CEO argued the economics (about EUR 103 per megawatt hour sold for 2027, about EUR 35 of concession fee, about EUR 50 of operating cost) leave little room for the state to take more. This is the single largest policy exposure.
- **Windfall levy and IRAP:** the extra IRAP charge is excluded from the "adjusted" profit, so reported profit will be lower than the adjusted figure for the next years.
- **Leverage and refinancing:** the CFO said 2027 refinancing will come early in the year, with duration managed to avoid "deteriorating the average cost of debt" - an implicit admission that the cost of new debt is higher.
- **Hedging:** about 70-72% of 2027 output is hedged, normal for A2A, so 2027 earnings carry roughly 30% open exposure to power prices and water.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- 🔴 Net debt to EBITDA up from 2.4x to 2.8x in nine months (2026-11-10), with earnings flat to down.
- 🔴 Low end of 2026 guidance flagged (2026-11-10); adjusted nine-month profit down 14%.
- 🔴 State take rising: higher hydro concession fees (EUR 47m drag in nine months) and a new IRAP surcharge.
- 🔴 Gross margin fell from 27.6% (2019) to 10.7% (2022) in the last inflation spike; the business is classified as inflation-exposed.
- 🟢 Guidance met or confirmed at every print since November 2025.
- 🟢 Treatment-plant problems from the first half stabilised in Q3 2026; retail customer churn back near 2025 levels.
- 🟢 Grid capital spending up 5% year on year; regulated asset base growing.
- 🟢 Piotroski score 7 of 9 (a test of improving accounting quality) in the scan.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- The vocabulary moved from "growth" and "achievements" (2025) to "resilience", "balance" and "visibility" (2026). Visibility is what a management team sells when growth is not available this year.
- Asset rotation changed from a stated pillar to "no specific plan... opportunistic approach" (2026-11-10). One lever for de-leveraging is now off the table as a promise.
- The CFO's interest-rate swaps "when the interest rate were very high and peaked very high in the last week and month" show they feel the rate backdrop. Same management team throughout; no new voices.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: Stable.** The business is executing its plan, but earnings are flat in 2026 and debt is climbing while capital spending runs ahead of profit. The single most important thing to weigh: at about EUR 2.27 the shares trade at roughly 11x the low end of the 2026 profit guide with leverage rising - fairly priced for a utility, not a mispricing. The value case depends on Monfalcone and grid growth showing up in 2027-28 earnings, not on anything this year.

**Moat:** regulatory-licence and scale. Regulated networks and waste-to-energy permits are hard to replicate, and the hydro concessions are valuable. Width is narrow: return on invested capital of about 5% is roughly the allowed return, not an excess return, and is stable rather than rising or falling. **Secular threat:** manageable. The energy transition is a tailwind for grids and a mixed bag for gas plants; the real long-run risk is political (concession renewals in 2029, windfall levies), which caps upside but does not shrink the earnings base structurally. The cheapness is neither a fear-print nor a value trap; it is a fair price for a levered, policy-exposed utility.
