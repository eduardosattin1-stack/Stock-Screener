# MMT.PA - Groupe M6 (Metropole Television) - forensic dossier

As of 2026-11-09. Price used: 11.28 euros (Euronext Paris, MMT.PA, FMP live quote 2026-11-09). Sources: full-year 2025 call (2026-02-17), first-half 2026 call (2026-07-28), third-quarter 2026 revenue release (2026-11-03, as reported by abcbourse and Zonebourse/Bourse Direct), prior committee record dated 2026-10-12.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story has been stable for a year: M6 is the share-winner in a shrinking French television advertising market, funding a streaming transition (M6+) and cutting costs (an 80-million-euro savings plan to 2030, announced 2026-02-17) while paying a 1.25-euro dividend. In February 2026 the emphasis was transformation and cost; in July 2026 it was the World Cup (sole French broadcaster of 54 matches, 11 June to 19 July 2026) and outperformance against a market down about 9%. The third-quarter release on 2026-11-03 extended the same arc: revenue up 9.4% to 294.5 million euros, video advertising up 6.2% to 199.2 million against a market Zonebourse puts at about minus 9%, streaming up 26.7% to 30.9 million. The narrative has not shifted toward a market recovery: management "remains prudent" on political, geopolitical and economic uncertainty and gave no fourth-quarter guidance.

## 2. CLAIMS vs FINANCIALS (forensic)

- Share gains: corroborated. 2025 video advertising minus 3.1% against a market about minus 8%; first half 2026 group advertising up about 1% against minus 9% excluding M6; third quarter 2026 video advertising up 6.2%. Part of the third quarter is the July World Cup tail, which management said would be roughly neutral to profit (28 July 2026).
- Profit: the claim of resilience is weaker than the revenue line. Nine-month operating profit before amortisation of acquired intangibles (EBITA) was 93.4 million euros, a 10.0% margin, with video programme costs up to 447.0 million from 362.5 million. First-half profit before depreciation fell 34.7%. The third quarter alone grew EBITA only 4.2% on 9.4% revenue growth.
- Non-advertising revenue jumped 34.7% to 65.3 million in the third quarter on cinema and World Cup items - non-recurring.
- Balance sheet: net cash of 130.1 million euros at 2026-09-30 versus 139.9 million at 2025-12-31, after repaying a 75-million-euro Schuldschein (German private-placement loan) in July. The third-quarter cash inflow of 166.7 million (versus 16.1 million a year earlier) is largely the collection of World Cup and seasonal receivables, so it should not be annualised. The 2026-06-30 position (about 65 million of net debt including leases, the prior committee's figure) was the post-dividend seasonal trough.
- Five-year record: revenue 1.39 billion (2021) to 1.26 billion (2025); earnings per share 2.22 to 0.98 euros. The screener's own fair value of 5.51 euros and the minus 51% margin of safety reflect that path.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

Confident and promotional on audiences and the World Cup; candid and uncertain on the market. On 28 July 2026 the chief executive could not separate cyclical from structural decline ("difficult to answer") and called the drop "not justified" without predicting it would stop. The 2026-11-03 release keeps that prudence. The tone on the dividend is non-committal ("too early to say", 28 July 2026). Finance-chief change: Jerome Lefebure leaves end-November 2026; Emmanuelle Marti started 2026-10-05.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

M6 does not give numeric profit guidance, which limits the test. What it did say has held: World Cup July effect near zero, streaming growth around a quarter a year (21.8% for nine months), the savings plan ramping progressively. Streaming target of over 200 million euros by 2028 from 126 million in 2025 needs about 17% a year - on track. No credibility breach found.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

Analysts (Kepler Cheuvreux, Bernstein, Deutsche Bank on 28 July 2026) pressed on three points: whether fourth-quarter budgets were pulled into the World Cup (management conceded "some part"), the dividend after a margin drop (no answer), and whether the television advertising market has stepped down structurally (no answer). None was resolved by the third-quarter release, which carried no outlook.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

Red: earnings per share compounding at about minus 18.5% over five years; gross margin contracting; a 1.25-euro dividend (about 157 million euros) above 2026 earnings and not reaffirmed; programme costs up 23% for nine months; audio revenue falling (31.0 million in the third quarter, down 3.3 million); diversification (real-estate franchises) in decline; two Supervisory Board resignations replaced provisionally by RTL Group/Bertelsmann executives (2026-11-03) - governance is in the controlling holder's hands.
Green: real and repeated share gains; streaming growing about 22-27%; net cash of 130 million at 2026-09-30; Piotroski 8 of 9; the cleanest balance sheet among European free-to-air broadcasters; third-quarter EBITA up 4.2%.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- The third-quarter beat came with non-advertising revenue up 34.7% and other advertising down 9.8%: the core is still a video-advertising business taking share in a falling pool.
- The share price moved from 9.94 euros (2026-10-12) to 11.28 euros (2026-11-09), up about 13.5%; TF1 moved from 5.78 to 6.48 euros, up about 12%, over the same window. The re-rating is mostly sector-wide, not a verdict on M6's own quarter.
- Bertelsmann/RTL Group placing its own executives on the board is consistent with the holder managing the asset ahead of the May 2028 end of the legal bar on a sale - speculative, no dated step.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

A well-run, low-debt share-winner in a structurally shrinking market. Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly in form (high payout) but the payout exceeds current earnings and is undecided until full-year results on 2027-02-23. The latest dated numbers (2026-11-03) confirm the operating claims and do not change the earnings arithmetic. At 11.28 euros the price sits above any fair value this committee can build from declining earnings power.
