# SSR Mining (SSRM) - a deep dive on management and the setup
*Five calls read: 2025-11-04, 2026-02-18, 2026-05-05, 2026-08-04, 2026-11-03. Financials: screener scan plus the FMP balance sheet dated 2026-09-30. Prices: SSRM $26.83 and gold $3,870 an ounce on 2026-11-16.*

**Takeaway:** management did what it said on the big strategic moves. The Turkey exit closed, the cash arrived, the debt is gone and the buybacks were real. Where it has not delivered is costs. All-in sustaining cost (AISC, the full cost of producing an ounce including sustaining capital) slid from "high end" in late 2025, to "upper end" in August 2026, to "about 10% above the top end" on 2026-11-03. Treat the strategy as credible and the cost guidance as optimistic. The stock is a gold-price instrument with a strong balance sheet. It is not a franchise with pricing power.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC
- **2025-11-04 (Q3-25):** the story is a Turkey-heavy company coming back from the 2024 tailings failure at Copler. The themes were the Cripple Creek & Victor (CC&V) technical report, spending of "$60 million to $100 million" on Hod Maden in Turkey, and a pending "construction decision". AISC was "trending towards the high end".
- **2026-02-18 (Q4-25):** the tone turns to capital returns. Cash was $535M at year-end, a $300M buyback was announced, and the CC&V report showed an $824M NPV (net present value, today's worth of future mine cash flows) and a 12-year mine life. Hod Maden was still being promoted as one of the better undeveloped copper-gold projects.
- **2026-05-05 (Q1-26):** the pivot. Copler was sold for $1.5B in cash, the convertible notes were settled, debt reached zero, and the $300M buyback was finished in April. The company now calls itself a "focused Americas-based gold and silver producer".
- **2026-08-04 (Q2-26):** the exit is complete. Both Copler and Hod Maden are gone (Hod Maden swapped for a 4% royalty), cash is "nearly $1.8 billion", and growth capital spending goes up. The AISC message darkens to "upper end".
- **2026-11-03 (Q3-26):** consolidation and bolt-on growth. The new Marigold plan shows 4.6Moz of reserves and a 20-year life, with an after-tax NPV of $3.3B at a $3,948 gold deck (per the 2026-10-13 release). The company is buying Roxmore and its Converse project for about $464M in cash (enterprise value about $392M). About 17M shares were bought back in nine months for more than $530M. AISC will land about 10% above the top of guidance.

Hod Maden went from flagship to sold in about six months. The swap was rational, since it removed both the Turkey risk and the capital bill, but it shows the story bends to what the market rewards.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY
Confidence rose through 2026 on strategy ("paradigm shift for Marigold", "peer-leading capital returns") and got more defensive on costs. In Q3-26 management blamed "industry-wide cost pressures" and diesel, and hoped "we're going to get some relief". Overall: stable. The offensive language is about capital allocation; the defensive language is about operations.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY
- **Strategic guidance:** delivered. Copler was guided to close "before the end of Q3". The cash arrived before the end of Q2.
- **Production guidance:** generally met. FY-25 came in above the midpoint, and FY-26 is on track if Q4 delivers.
- **Cost guidance:** missed three quarters in a row, each time a step worse. About half of the 2026 overrun is a deliberate choice at Puna to defer low-cost stockpile ounces, which is partly a timing shift and not lost value.
- **Earnings beats:** the 6 of 7 quarter beat rate in the financials reflects gold prices more than cost discipline.

Reliability: **medium**.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS
- Q4 Marigold math. Analysts pushed to confirm the low end of Marigold's 170koz guidance.
- 2027 diesel hedges. There are none in place.
- Seabee grade normalization.
- Converse permitting, milestones and capital. Management deferred these "until the transaction closes".
- Mergers and acquisitions appetite. Management said a large-scale development "doesn't feel like the right next step". That is useful discipline language, but it was given while buying a development project.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS
- Red: AISC overrun of ~10% above guidance at the top (Q3-26). This is the third straight negative cost revision.
- Red: no 2027 diesel hedges (Q3-26).
- Red: the 2026 buybacks averaged roughly $31 a share, above today's price (Q3-26 figures).
- Red: Roxmore puts ~$464M of cash into ounces that produce nothing until about 2033 (announced 2026-10-13).
- Red: the free cash flow base depends on gold. Gold was $4,114 on 2026-11-09, $3,870 on 2026-11-16, and $5,627 at the 52-week high.
- Green: the Turkey exit is complete, with $1.5B of cash received in Q2-26 and Hod Maden turned into a royalty (Q2-26).
- Green: zero debt and ~$1.62B of cash at 2026-09-30.
- Green: Marigold has a 20-year life on 4.6Moz of reserves (2026-10-13), and US reserves now exceed 7Moz.
- Green: about 37M shares retired since 2021 (Q3-26).

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS
- The vocabulary moved from "construction decision" (Hod Maden, Q3-25) to "free cash flow focused" and "capital return leader" (Q2-26). The company is pitching itself to value and income investors now.
- The Executive Chairman (Rod Antal) still fronts every call. The CFO voice (Michael Sparks) carries the cost bad news.
- The CC&V NPV of $824M was set at an older, lower consensus price deck. Marigold's $3.3B uses $3,948 gold, close to today's spot price. These per-mine values are the right anchor for a sum-of-the-parts (valuing each mine separately).

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)
**Trajectory: stable.** The strategic repositioning is finished. What remains is execution on cost and staying disciplined with cash. The most important thing to weigh before sizing: **this is a leveraged bet on the gold price with a cash cushion.** With AISC near $2,500 and gold at $3,870, the margin per ounce is about $1,350. A further 10% fall in gold takes about 30% off that margin.

**Moat:** none in the economic sense. A gold miner takes the market price for what it sells. Long-life assets in Nevada and Colorado are a jurisdiction advantage, not a cost-curve advantage: AISC above $2,500 sits in the upper half of the industry cost curve, and ROIC is 2.2%. The moat trend is stable. No secular force threatens the end value. The cheapness is a factor print (gold fell about 6% in a week, and the miners fell more), not a shrinking business.
