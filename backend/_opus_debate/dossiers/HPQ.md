# HP Inc. (HPQ) - a deep dive on management and the setup (2026-11-02)

Five calls reviewed: fiscal Q3 2025 (2025-08-27), Q4 2025 (2025-11-25), Q1 2026 (2026-02-24), Q2 2026 (2026-05-27) and Q3 2026 (2026-08-26). Live price used: $28.43 (NYSE, US dollars, 2026-11-02 quote).

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story moved from "AI PCs and a Windows 10 refresh lift a mature hardware franchise" (2025) to "we can manage a memory-cost shock" (late 2025 into 2026), and finally to "HP as an edge-AI platform under an interim chief executive" (Q3 2026). In the 2025-08-27 and 2025-11-25 calls management framed the memory price rise as "material" but "temporary" and said the portfolio was "less sensitive to the commodities market than it was during the last memory cycle". By 2026-02-24 the tone was about mitigation: price increases ("we're taking additional prices to offset the rising impact of memory"), specification changes, supplier agreements, and a fiscal 2026 earnings-per-share (EPS) range of $2.90 to $3.20, with analysts pressing on whether the low end was the realistic outcome. The Q3 2026 call (2026-08-26) introduced a new voice - interim chief executive Bruce Broussard - and a broader agenda: "greater strategic clarity around where we choose to play and allocate capital", integrated solutions and recurring revenue. A $1 billion gross run-rate cost-savings programme (by fiscal 2028, $300 million by end of fiscal 2026) is the self-help lever that has stayed constant throughout.

Net debt is $6.17B including $1.18B of capitalised leases (about $4.99B excluding leases). Book equity is negative (return on equity -350%) because buybacks exceeded retained earnings; return on invested capital of 29% reflects a capital-light model, not a widening moat.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

Stable to slightly declining. 2025-08-27 was offensive (AI PC, refresh). 2025-11-25 and 2026-02-24 turned defensive (memory "headwinds", pricing, cost savings). 2026-05-27 and 2026-08-26 regained some confidence on delivery but became less specific about the future: on fiscal 2027, chief financial officer Karen Parkhill said "it's premature to give you specifics on our outlook", and unit volumes "continue to move around". Leadership is in transition, with no timeline for a permanent chief executive.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

Medium-High. The scan shows earnings beats in 5 of 7 quarters (71%); management called the fiscal 2026 range at $2.90 to $3.20 and has delivered quarterly against it, helped in Q3 by the tariff refund. The pattern is conservative quarterly guidance with a declining multi-year trend: they hit what they promise, but what they promise keeps shrinking.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- Memory inventory cover and whether long-term supply agreements protect fiscal 2027 margins - answered qualitatively, not quantitatively.
- Whether the tariff refund is in the Print margin guide (it is excluded from the 16-19% range - confirmed 2026-08-26).
- Fiscal 2027 PC unit volumes - deflected as "premature".
- The chief executive search - "unable to share details or a time line".

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- Red: four-year earnings decline (EPS $5.36 in 2021 to $2.65 in 2025), fiscal-year free cash flow down 11%.
- Red: 11 open-market insider sales and zero purchases in the latest window.
- Red: Intel's 2026-10-29 warning of a deepening PC slump and memory shortages - HP fell about 9% on 2026-10-30.
- Red: interim leadership ahead of a difficult fiscal 2027 guide (due with Q4 results around 2026-11-24).
- Green: free cash flow of about $3.9B TTM, Piotroski score 7 of 9, share gains in premium PCs, workstations and big-tank printers, Industrial Print's 12th straight growth quarter (2026-08-26).
- Green: margins held through the 2021-22 inflation (gross margin 19.0% to 19.5%).

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

The vocabulary shifted from "Future Ready" cost plans to "where we choose to play and allocate capital" (2026-08-26) - language that often precedes portfolio pruning or a strategy reset by a new chief executive, which can come with a kitchen-sink guide. Management increasingly cites "attached businesses" (peripherals, services) as a third of Personal Systems gross profit, a sign it wants investors to see the PC business as less commoditised than unit data suggests.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

Trajectory: STABLE. HP is a disciplined cash machine with a slowly shrinking earnings base; the business is not breaking, but the market has just been reminded (Intel, 2026-10-29) that fiscal 2027 PC demand and memory costs are a real risk. The single most important thing to weigh: at $28.43 the stock still trades above our roughly $26 base-case value, so even after the 17% drop from $34.13 the price, not the business, is the obstacle.

Moat: a narrow scale-cost and installed-base moat (printer supplies annuity, PC supply-chain scale, enterprise relationships). Returns are high because the capital base is tiny, and gross margin is stable around 20%, so the moat is narrow and stable rather than widening or eroding. Secular threat: manageable - home and office printing is in slow structural decline, but HP is shifting to big-tank, industrial and subscription; the PC business is cyclical rather than terminally threatened. The low multiple is mostly a fair price for a no-growth, cyclical business, not a fear-driven mispricing.
