# Peabody Energy (BTU) - a deep dive on management and the setup

*Prepared 2026-11-16. Calls reviewed: Q2 2025 (2025-07-31), Q3 2025 (2025-10-31), Q4 2025 (2026-02-05), Q1 2026 (2026-05-05) and Q3 2026 (2026-11-04). The Q2 2026 call (early August 2026) was not in the transcript bundle; its figures are taken from the filed quarterly statements. Live price used: $17.40 (NYSE, US dollars, FMP quote 2026-11-16).*

**Bottom line:** Peabody's story for 18 months has been one mine. Centurion, a new premium hard coking coal (the high-grade coal used to make steel) longwall mine in Queensland, was meant to lift the company from a thermal coal (power-station coal) producer with thin margins into a lower-cost steelmaking-coal franchise. Three straight quarters of operating trouble at that mine have broken management's delivery record, and the share price has fallen from about $39.50 in March to $17.40 today. The balance sheet is still clean (about $60M of net cash on 2026-09-30) and seaborne coal prices have turned up sharply, so the question for the director is whether Centurion is late or whether it is worse than the company says.

## 1. Narrative arc

- **Mid-2025: "controlling the controllables."** In the Q2 2025 call the chief executive, Jim Grech, raised full-year guidance on cost control and announced that Centurion's longwall start had been *accelerated* to February 2026. The US story was policy-led: executive orders and the federal royalty cut from 12.5% to 7% (worth an estimated $15-20M in the second half of 2025).
- **Late 2025: "an interesting inflection point."** In Q3 2025 management said Centurion shipments would rise sevenfold to 3.5 million tonnes in 2026, that Centurion would be the company's lowest-cost steelmaking-coal mine for 25-plus years, and that capital spending was tapering just as cash flow would expand "in both directions". The Anglo American steelmaking-coal acquisition had been terminated in August 2025 and was being wound down.
- **February 2026: peak confidence.** On the Q4 2025 call the last longwall shield was being installed "well ahead of its original schedule", with 3.5Mt guided for 2026 and 4.7Mt a year by 2028.
- **May 2026: the first crack.** The Q1 2026 call opened with "the notable exception of Centurion": mechanical and electrical commissioning problems slowed cutting, which let roof conditions deteriorate. The thermal story took over the narrative: the Iran conflict in late February lifted Newcastle thermal coal prices by more than $20 a tonne in March.
- **August 2026 (from the filings):** the second quarter was the worst of the year - a GAAP gross loss of $72M and a net loss of $90.6M. A securities class action covering 2024-10-14 to 2026-05-04 was filed (lead-plaintiff deadline 2026-08-24).
- **November 2026: "more complex than our mine plan assumed."** Centurion met its Q3 sales target, but faulting across the tailgate, face and headgate cut October output, and Q4 sales guidance for the mine is now only 0.3-0.5Mt. The next longwall move is now expected in Q3 2027. New side stories appeared - germanium, solar and storage on reclaimed land with RWE, and mine-mouth power for data centres - which is what management teams often add when the core story stalls.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory - Declining

- Q2 2025: offensive - "a great first half", guidance raised, start-up accelerated.
- Q3 2025: expansive - "an interesting inflection point", "the intersection of some of the most significant themes going on in America".
- Q4 2025: proud - "well ahead of its original schedule".
- Q1 2026: defensive - "a longer-than-anticipated commissioning period", the emphasis moves to safety and "durable solutions".
- Q3 2026: explanatory and conditional - "more complex than our mine plan assumed", "give us another quarter here, and we will provide full guidance".

Specificity on Centurion fell call by call: from tonnage targets per year to a narrow quarterly range and a promise of guidance next time.

## 4. Guidance credibility - Low

Guidance on the legacy thermal mines and on costs has generally been met or beaten (Q2 2025 costs came in below plan; Q4 2026 seaborne thermal cost guide is $46-51/t versus $56.58/t in Q3). But on the single item that carries the thesis, every forward statement since mid-2025 has been missed: the accelerated start, the 3.5Mt year, the 80% steelmaking-coal price realisation. The screener records one earnings beat in seven quarters (14%), and Q3 2026 missed the consensus loss estimate (-$0.27 against -$0.08). US thermal volumes (the Powder River Basin, PRB) also came in below guidance in Q3 2026 on weather, cheap gas and a customer outage. Reliability score: Low on Centurion, Medium on the rest of the portfolio.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

- **Will Centurion be a higher-cost mine than planned?** (Texas Capital, 2026-11-04). The answer - the fixes are "very modest capital" and long-run costs are unchanged - was confident, but it was followed by "we have got a lot to learn".
- **The next longwall move** (Nathan Martin, 2026-11-04): timing deferred to February guidance.
- **2027 contract coverage for US thermal**: not given; deferred to guidance.
- **Diversification side projects** (B. Riley): germanium deal "near", mine-mouth power "very early days". Analysts are probing them, which shows how little else there is to ask about.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

- Red: Centurion slippage in three consecutive reports (Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026) after it was called "ahead of schedule" in February 2026.
- Red: GAAP losses in every 2026 quarter; Q3 2026 free cash flow negative despite the best coking coal price since early 2024.
- Red: insiders sold about 53,500 shares in five open-market sales and bought none.
- Red: securities class action covering October 2024 to May 2026.
- Red: newest analyst target (2026-11-16) is $22, the low of the range; the bundle shows target velocity down 3% versus a year ago.
- Green: about $60M net cash and $887M of liquidity; no debt wall. The remaining $39.9M of 2028 convertible notes is being redeemed in cash in November 2026.
- Green: seaborne prices strong - premium coking coal $248/t and Newcastle above $145/t at end of Q3 2026, with China removing an estimated 70Mt of domestic steelmaking-coal supply this year.
- Green: every segment had positive adjusted EBITDA in Q3 2026 (total $97.5M); Q4 cost guides are lower than Q3 actuals.

## 7. Hidden signals

- The language moved from "accelerated" and "ahead of schedule" to "faulting", "ground pressure" and "elements of the current longwall design were less effective". That is a design admission, not just bad luck with the rock.
- The October 2026 output figure is unclear in the sources: the call transcript reads "just over 450 thousand tons" while the results release, as summarised, puts it nearer 45,000 tonnes; the Q4 sales guide of 0.3-0.5Mt fits the lower reading better.
- Investor-relations leadership changed in late 2025 (Vic Svec to Kala Finklang); the commercial lead's title changed to chief commercial officer.
- New growth vocabulary (germanium, data-centre power, reclamation-land solar) arrived just as the coal growth story stalled.
- Capital returns quietly shifted from buybacks to retiring convertible notes, which is sensible but slower.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict for the director

**Trajectory: Deteriorating** on execution, with a better price backdrop that partly offsets it.

The single most important thing to weigh: **this is a bet on Centurion reaching most of its 4.7Mt nameplate at roughly $125/t costs in 2027.** If it does, steelmaking-coal earnings alone could add several hundred million dollars of EBITDA a year and today's price looks cheap. If it keeps faulting, Peabody is a thermal coal producer with thin margins whose profits depend on an oil-and-gas price shock (the Strait of Hormuz disruption) that could reverse on a diplomatic headline. The proof arrives with Q4 2026 results and 2027 guidance in early February 2027.

**Moat:** none in the durable sense. Peabody is a price-taker in a commodity. Its cost position in the Powder River Basin and the quality of Centurion's coal give it a cost advantage, not pricing power, and returns have fallen with prices (earnings per share from $8.31 in 2022 to losses in 2026). **Secular threat:** material, not terminal - about 72% of 2025 revenue was thermal coal, which faces long-run decline as power systems move away from coal (US thermal especially), while Asian seaborne demand and steelmaking coal remain needed for years. The cheapness is part fear-print (an execution problem on one mine during a sector sell-off) and part structural (a shrinking thermal base), so the discount should not be treated as pure mispricing.
