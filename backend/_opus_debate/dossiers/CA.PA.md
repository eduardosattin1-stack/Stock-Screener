# Carrefour SA (CA.PA) - a deep dive on management and the setup

*Prepared 2026-10-26. Sources: five calls in the transcript bundle (Q3 2024 sales call 2024-10-23, full-year 2024 results 2025-02-19, Q1 2025 sales 2025-04-24, half-year 2025 results 2025-08-13, full-year 2025 results 2026-02-17), the screener's financial block (fiscal years 2021-2025 plus trailing twelve months, or TTM, to 2026-06-30), and dated public releases since the last call: the half-year 2026 results (late July 2026) and the Q3 2026 sales release of 2026-10-20. Live price used: EUR 13.775 on Euronext Paris (FMP quote, 2026-10-26).*

**Takeaway.** Carrefour is a turnaround story that keeps delivering the cost and cash pieces it controls while the earnings line keeps disappointing. Management's words about France, cost savings and cash have mostly been borne out; its words about Brazil and the group's earnings trajectory have not. On 2026-10-20, eight months into a new plan to 2030, it cut its 2026 targets for margin, earnings per share and cash. The stock is cheap on cash flow for a reason: returns on capital are thin and the earnings record is poor.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

- **Late 2024 (Q3 2024 call, full-year 2024 call):** the story was "invest in price to win share." France had "our strongest investment in prices" and its "best price positioning since 2020" (2025-02-19). Brazil was the growth engine: "set to enjoy an attractive growth trajectory in 2025 and beyond." Argentina posted a record EUR 115 million profit.
- **Mid-2025 (H1 2025 call, 2025-08-13):** the story turned to portfolio surgery. The February 2025 strategic review produced, "in less than a quarter," the buy-out of Carrefour Brazil minorities, a 7% sale of the Carmila property company, and exclusive talks to sell Italy. The chief executive promised to be "radical" and that "we would continue to move and to take bold decisions." The Concordis European buying alliance was launched (2025-07-07).
- **Early 2026 (full-year 2025 call, 2026-02-17):** the tone became "steady delivery." Italy was sold, Romania signed. Brazil had gone from growth engine to a market "marked by record high interest rates and negative volumes," with the claim that "the cycle trough is now behind us." France core became the hero: operating margin at "the 3% milestone," market share "22%, its highest point since 2015." The 2030 plan followed the next day (operating margin 3.2% in 2028 and 3.5% in 2030, EUR 5 billion cumulative net free cash flow in 2026-2028, high single-digit adjusted earnings-per-share growth each year, payout 50-60% of adjusted earnings).
- **Since then (dated releases):** the half-year 2026 results confirmed all 2026 targets, Romania closed and a EUR 0.21 special dividend was paid on 2026-07-30. Then on 2026-10-20 the group cut its 2026 targets, blaming "the social and security situation in France" (lost footfall, curfew-shortened opening hours, higher security and logistics costs) and energy costs squeezing shoppers. The 2028 and 2030 targets were kept.

What was quietly dropped: Brazil as the growth driver; share buybacks (none since the French buyback tax, per the CFO on 2026-02-17); and the 2026 promise of earnings-per-share growth.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Trajectory: Declining, then defensive.**
- 2024-10-23: upbeat - "a positive inflection in most markets," consumer confidence "at their highest level since the beginning of the inflation crisis."
- 2025-02-19: confident on France and Brazil, frank that 2024 was "a challenging year."
- 2025-08-13: offensive on strategy ("radicality," "bold decisions") but evasive on numbers: "we're not going to guide" on second-half margin; on Argentina, "I'm not going to give you an outlook."
- 2026-02-17: measured. Brazil shifted to defence ("our strict cost discipline helped protect margins"). France Christmas was called "a surprising trend."
- 2026-10-20 (release): the cut itself, attributed to outside forces (French social unrest, energy costs) rather than execution.

The shift is from offence (price investment, growth, portfolio action) to defence (cost discipline, external blame).

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

- 2025 targets ("slight growth in EBITDA, recurring operating income and net free cash flow") were met only loosely: EBITDA "stable," recurring operating income up 2.2% excluding Cora & Match, net free cash flow EUR 1.565 billion versus EUR 1.45 billion. Met on cash, flat to missed on the rest.
- Cora & Match integration cost EUR 145 million versus EUR 150 million guided (2026-02-17). Delivered.
- 2026 targets (margin +25 basis points or more, high single-digit adjusted earnings-per-share growth, cash up) were confirmed in late July 2026 and cut on 2026-10-20 to a margin of +0 to 15 basis points, earnings per share "stable to slight growth," net free cash flow "slight decline," capital spending cut to EUR 1.6 billion from EUR 1.7 billion.
- The screener shows an earnings beat rate of 5 of 8 quarters (62%) and three-year earnings-per-share decline of 36% a year.

There is no sandbagging pattern; the pattern is optimism that gets trimmed. **Reliability: Medium-Low** - high on cost and integration targets, low on group earnings.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- **Cash flow quality** (Rob Joyce, BNP Paribas, 2026-02-17): pushed on the EUR 1.7 billion target and on EUR 1.4 billion of factored receivables. Answered at length, and the cash target was cut eight months later.
- **Capital allocation** (Geoffroy Michalet, ODDO BHF, 2026-02-17): why no buyback, and what happens to the Romanian proceeds? Answer: the French buyback tax, a special dividend of about 30% of proceeds, the rest kept "for flexibility and a number of opportunities." Analysts worry the cash funds more deals rather than shareholders.
- **French franchisee dispute** (same call): management said "the door is always open" - no resolution offered.
- **Brazil consumer** (Frederick Wild, Jefferies, 2025-08-13): "well oriented" was challenged; Brazil volumes then went negative for the rest of 2025 and the market was still negative in Q3 2026.
- **Argentina** (2025-08-13): profit halved and management declined to give an outlook.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- Red: 2026 guidance cut on 2026-10-20, only three months after it was confirmed and eight months into the new plan.
- Red: Earnings per share down from 2.31 (2023) to 0.47 (2025); return on invested capital 4.6%; net margin 0.4%.
- Red: Brazil, the former growth engine, has had negative market volumes since mid-2025 under record interest rates (2026-02-17, 2026-10-20).
- Red: EUR 1.4 billion of receivables factoring (disclosed more fully from 2026-02-17) flatters cash flow.
- Red: Unresolved dispute with a group of French franchisees (2026-02-17), in a model that is moving more stores to franchise.
- Green: France core margin reached 3% with recurring operating income +11.3% in 2025; French market share at 22%, the highest since 2015 (2026-02-17).
- Green: Portfolio pruning executed on schedule - Italy sold (2025), Romania sold and a EUR 0.21 special dividend paid 2026-07-30; 35-store sale-and-leaseback in France and Spain closed early October 2026.
- Green: Funding secure - EUR 500 million bond (September 2026), credit facility extended to 2031; ex-lease net debt cut by EUR 1.1 billion year on year at 2026-06-30.
- Green: Ordinary dividend raised 5% to EUR 0.97 (proposed 2026-02-17), about a 7% yield at today's price.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- **"Excluding" creep.** Almost every positive number in 2025-2026 is stated "excluding Cora & Match," "excluding Italy," or "at constant currency." The group-level, reported trend is flat to down.
- **Capital spending as the swing factor.** The October cut trims France investment for the rest of 2026. That props up cash flow now but contradicts the plan's push to transform stores.
- **Leadership continuity.** The chief executive's mandate was renewed (proposed 2025-08-13). No new voices on the calls; the same team owns both the delivery and the misses.
- **Competition language.** France is framed as "narrowing the price gap with the market" - an admission that Carrefour is still more expensive than the discounters and Leclerc, and must keep spending gross margin to hold share.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: turning (portfolio reshaping toward France, Spain and Brazil).** Execution on disposals and costs is real; earnings conversion is not.

**The single most important thing to weigh:** whether the roughly EUR 1.5 billion of annual net free cash flow (about 15% of today's EUR 9.7 billion market value) is durable shareholder cash, or keeps being consumed by price investment, deals, factoring reversals and outside shocks. The 2026-10-20 cut is the first test of the new plan, and it was failed on cash.

**Moat.** Scale purchasing (Concordis, Eureka) and a dense French convenience-franchise network give a narrow, real moat. It is not widening: gross margin is flat around 19%, return on invested capital is 4.6% and the earnings base has shrunk for two years, though France core margin is improving. Call it narrow and stable.

**Secular threat.** The large-hypermarket format faces a slow shift of spending to discounters, convenience and online. Carrefour is answering with convenience stores (a record 456 opened in 2025) and e-commerce growth (+29% in Q2 2025). That is a manageable threat, not a terminal one. The cheapness is mostly a low-return, low-trust discount rather than a melting base - a value case only if cash keeps reaching shareholders.
