# Alnylam Pharmaceuticals (ALNY): a deep dive on management and the setup

*Prepared 2026-11-02. Transcripts reviewed: Q3 2025 (2025-10-30), Q4 2025 (2026-02-12), Q1 2026 (2026-04-30), Q2 2026 (2026-07-30), Q3 2026 (2026-10-29). The live price used is $158.00 on Nasdaq, in US dollars (FMP quote, 2026-11-02 close).*

**The short version:** Alnylam is still one of the best-run companies in biotech. It has six medicines on the market, revenue growing about 50% a year and a balance sheet with $2.3B more cash than debt. But the story management told for a year was that its heart drug AMVUTTRA would keep speeding up, and the last two calls walked that back. In July the 2026 forecast for its TTR franchise (TTR, or transthyretin, is the protein behind the disease its biggest medicines treat) was cut by $200M at the midpoint. On 2026-10-29 management said it was "not yet satisfied with the underlying demand growth" and steered to the low end of the lowered range. The stock fell 25% that day. The business is still growing; what has broken is the belief that growth would keep speeding up.

## 1. Narrative arc

- **Q3 2025 (2025-10-30): the victory lap.** The ATTR-CM launch (ATTR-CM is transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, a fatal build-up of misfolded protein in the heart) roughly doubled cardiomyopathy revenue quarter on quarter. Management raised 2025 product-revenue guidance by 10% at the midpoint to $2.95-3.05B and said "category growth in ATTR-CM is accelerating."
- **Q4 2025 (2026-02-12): the big plan.** GAAP profitability for full-year 2025 (GAAP means standard accounting rules), plus the "Alnylam 2030" goals: more than 25% revenue CAGR (compound annual growth rate) through 2030, a 30% non-GAAP operating margin, two or more new blockbusters, and nucresiran launching in 2028 (PN, the nerve form of the disease) and 2030 (CM). Net price was guided down by a mid-single-digit percentage each year.
- **Q1 2026 (2026-04-30): first quarter above $1B.** Product revenue of $1.036B. Guidance was reiterated, but quarter-on-quarter growth slowed to 4%, which management put down to gross-to-net deductions (the gap between list price and what the company actually collects) and seasonality.
- **Q2 2026 (2026-07-30): the first walk-back.** 2026 TTR guidance was cut to $4.2-4.5B. The reason given was that "early second-line demand growth in 2025 benefited significantly from pent-up demand ... that has since normalized." Second-line patients are those switching from, or adding to, an older stabilizer drug. Management announced a bigger sales force. The stock fell about 28%.
- **Q3 2026 (2026-10-29): the second walk-back.** TTR revenue was $1.15B (+59% year on year, +12% on the quarter). But US demand grew only about $81M on the quarter, and $17M of inventory build plus about $10M of one-time pricing adjustments flattered the reported number. The expanded sales force has "increased the reach and volume" of doctor contacts, but these "have not yet translated into increased demand growth." Management guided to the low end of the range, deferred 2027 guidance to February, and enlarged the TRITON-CM trial of the next-generation drug to about 2,250 patients. New payer worries were raised on the call: a proposed CMS 340B rule (340B is a federal drug-discount program for safety-net hospitals) and a UnitedHealth Medicare Advantage first-line restriction that took effect 2026-10-01.

The story has moved from "an accelerating launch" to "a big, still-growing franchise that now has to be pushed." The pipeline has quietly become a larger part of the pitch, led by encouraging early Huntington's disease data for brezosiran on 2026-10-23.

The financials match the claims on scale and profitability. They diverge on momentum: reported 52% growth now includes inventory and one-off pricing help, while underlying patient demand is growing more slowly than management modelled.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory: declining

- Q3 2025: "exceptional progress," category growth "accelerating."
- Q4 2025: "transformational year," set big five-year targets.
- Q1 2026: "remain confident in our 2026 TTR revenue guidance."
- Q2 2026: hindsight framing, with second-line demand that "has since normalized."
- Q3 2026: "we're not yet satisfied," "we have more work to do," "accelerating that translation is a clear priority."

The tone has moved from offence to defence. The 25% CAGR target was defended only conditionally: "if we can capture a third of the market ... we'll achieve our ambitions."

## 4. Guidance credibility: medium, falling

Management over-delivered through 2025 and then missed twice in a row. The 71% beat rate (5 of 7 quarters) refers to consensus EPS and is inflated by the tax event, so it is not a guide to revenue reliability. There was no sign of sandbagging (setting easy targets on purpose). The opposite happened: 2025 pent-up demand was extrapolated into the 2026 guide.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

- **Can the 25% CAGR to 2030 survive** the second-line slowdown, the 340B proposal and the UnitedHealth restriction? (RBC, 2026-10-29.) Management neither withdrew nor re-affirmed it numerically.
- **How 340B affects the model:** management "can't predict" the volume or price effect and pushed the answer to the February call.
- **Why TRITON-CM was enlarged:** management cited event rates falling across the whole field (earlier diagnosis, SGLT2 heart drugs) and called its assumptions "conservative." Enlarging a trial that is already running is a mild yellow flag about event-rate risk, even though launch timing (2030) is unchanged.
- **First-line versus second-line mix** has been a recurring question since 2025-10-30. The 2026 cuts show this was the weak point.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

- Red flag (2026-07-30): guidance cut on second-line pent-up demand normalizing.
- Red flag (2026-10-29): demand growth not lifted by the sales-force expansion, guided to the low end, 2027 outlook deferred.
- Red flag (2026-10-01 and 2026-10-29): payer headwinds. UnitedHealth Medicare Advantage first-line restriction and the CMS 340B proposal.
- Red flag (ongoing): 72 open-market insider sales against no purchases. Shareholder law-firm "investigations" after the July drop (routine, but noise).
- Green flag (2026-10-29): AMVUTTRA is at 24% of total TTR-category prescriptions 18 months into the launch, already at the one-third target on new-to-brand prescriptions, and ATTR-CM category prescriptions are up nearly 40% year on year. About 80% of prevalent patients remain untreated.
- Green flag (2026-10-23): brezosiran Phase 1b showed up to 83% huntingtin lowering, with a pivotal study planned for 2027.
- Green flag (2026-09-30): $2.33B net cash, GAAP profitability, and a $2.0B deferred tax asset that shelters future cash taxes.

## 7. Hidden signals

- The vocabulary has shifted from "launch" to "adoption at scale" and "prescriber base." Management now reports total prescriptions instead of new patient starts, which smooths away weak start growth.
- In the Q3 2026 call, investor relations was fronted by a new VP of IR (Josh Brodsky) rather than the communications chief. This is minor.
- "Laser-like focus and urgency" on commercial execution is language that usually comes after a miss, not before an acceleration.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict

**Trajectory: deteriorating (commercial momentum), while profitability strengthens.** The most important thing for the director is that the stock is now priced for a franchise whose growth is slowing (about 3.5 times 2026 sales net of cash). That leaves upside if demand re-accelerates. But two guidance disappointments in three months mean the February 2027 guide is the real test. A third disappointment, a 2027 TTR guide implying growth in the single digits, would say the launch has plateaued early.

**Moat:** intangible and regulatory, through patents and the RNAi (RNA interference) platform and its manufacturing know-how. It is narrow but real. The class has competitors in the stabilizers (tafamidis, acoramidis) and other silencers, and pricing power is being tested by payers. Margins and returns are rising, not falling, so the moat is not eroding. Its width is limited by competition and payer leverage.

**Secular threat:** reimbursement compression (340B, Medicare Advantage utilization management, international price referencing under MFN, or "most favored nation," pricing). This is material but manageable. It is a pricing headwind on a growing volume base, not a terminal threat. The cheapness is mostly a fear-print about launch momentum, not a structurally shrinking base.
