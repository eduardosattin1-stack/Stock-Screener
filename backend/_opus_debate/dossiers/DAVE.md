# Dave Inc. (DAVE) - Deep dive on management and the setup
*As of 2026-11-09. Sources: five earnings calls (Q3 2025 on 2025-11-04 through Q3 2026 on 2026-11-04), the Q3 2026 press release (2026-11-04), the screener's financial trajectory, and FMP prices. Live price $282.65 (Nasdaq, USD, 2026-11-09).*

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

Dave is a US consumer neobank whose economic engine is ExtraCash, a small-dollar, single-pay cash advance (average size about $218-225) underwritten from the member's linked bank account, plus a monthly subscription and, from 2026, the Dave Flex card.

- **Q3 2025 to Q4 2025 (2025-11-04, 2026-03-03):** the story was "underwriting is our moat". CashAI v5 drove credit improvements, guidance was raised every quarter, and management announced the move of ExtraCash receivables to Coastal Community Bank in an off-balance-sheet structure (the bank holds the receivables, Dave keeps the full economic exposure) to free up liquidity and lower funding costs. Management noted 2025 opened with $415-435M revenue guidance and closed about 30% above that midpoint.
- **Q1 2026 (2026-05-06):** Dave Flex is introduced as the next growth leg, explicitly kept out of guidance. The pricing story begins: the $15 fee cap is removed for new members.
- **Q2 2026 (2026-08-05):** the fee cap is removed for most grandfathered members and raised to $20 for the rest from late August; Coastal funding begins. The stock gapped down on an earnings miss the next day (from $430 to about $386).
- **Q3 2026 (2026-11-04):** revenue growth accelerated to 31%, a record 1.1M new members, and the third guidance raise of the year. But the tone now carries more explanation: a higher-than-planned provision for credit losses, a sequential gross-margin step-down, and an admission that collections improvements "pulled forward" recoveries rather than lowering ultimate losses.

The arc is expansion, then monetization (pricing), now scale (marketing up about 50%). The story has not shrunk; what has changed is that the earnings leverage is now being spent on growth and on credit, so adjusted EPS flattened year on year.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Stable, with a defensive edge emerging.** Through Q1 2026 the language was offensive ("eighth consecutive quarter of increasing guidance on all metrics"). By Q3 2026 management spent prepared remarks explaining provision and gross-margin drivers, and the CFO conceded on collections: "in hindsight, we did not really change much around the decisioning process... to really expect meaningful loss rate impact". The CEO steered investors to September's sub-2% delinquency as "what I would be using to assess credit performance". That is a reasonable point but also a choice of the most flattering metric.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

High for the company's own guidance: 2025 finished about 30% above the original revenue midpoint and near double the original EBITDA guidance; 2026 guidance has been raised every quarter, most recently to revenue $746-750M (from $725-735M), adjusted EBITDA $327-330M (from $315-325M) and adjusted EPS $17.45-17.65 (from $17.00-17.50). Management describes a deliberately conservative philosophy ("numbers that we have very high confidence in delivering upon"). The weak spot is versus the street: Q2 2026 and Q3 2026 both missed consensus EPS, so the street is now running ahead of management on quarterly earnings timing. Reliability: High on the company's guide, Medium against consensus.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- **Why not push credit harder?** Analysts on 2026-11-04 asked why the improving delinquency is not used to lend more; management pointed to testing limits above $600 and up to $1,000.
- **Collections pull-forward:** the loss benefit analysts expected did not carry through; management called it "a learning".
- **Coastal economics:** whether Dave will balance-sheet more of the book; answer: Coastal will fund "the vast majority".
- **DOJ matter:** each call repeats "no updates and continue to vigorously defend our position" since the case entered discovery. No analyst pushed on settlement scenarios in the transcripts, which is itself a gap.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- Red: provision rose to 18.5% of revenue in Q3 2026 (+1.7 points sequentially) and the collections gain proved to be timing, not lower losses (2026-11-04).
- Red: two straight adjusted-EPS misses against consensus (2026-08-05, 2026-11-04), with adjusted EPS down 1% y/y in Q3 2026 despite 31% revenue growth.
- Red: the Justice Department lawsuit (originally an FTC matter over fees and disclosures) is still in discovery; meanwhile the company removed the fee cap through 2026, i.e. raised the effective price of the very product under scrutiny.
- Red: securities-law-firm "investigations" announced 2026-11-05/06 after the 18% one-day drop; these are routine after a sharp fall and carry little information on their own.
- Green: revenue re-accelerated to 31% with a record 1.1M new members, and customer acquisition cost rose only 14% while marketing spend rose about 50% (2026-11-04).
- Green: net monetization rate (revenue per dollar advanced after credit losses) 5.2%, the highest in five years; September 28-day delinquency below 2% after CashAI v6.
- Green: balance sheet de-risked; the Victory Park facility repaid in October 2026, cash up to $315M.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- The member count was redefined in Q3 2026 (low-engagement savings-only users excluded, prior periods recast). Harmless if recast as stated, but definition changes deserve watching.
- "Invest to maximize gross profit dollars rather than minimize CAC" signals management will keep trading near-term EPS for growth while returns hold above the cost of capital.
- Pricing language moved from "fee cap" to "all customers roughly on the same fee structure" via a 5% additional-draw fee: a quiet price harmonization upward.
- Dave Flex is consistently framed as a 2027 second-half contributor, not 2026: this keeps the 2027 estimate dependent on ExtraCash.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: stable.** The business is still growing fast and profitably, guidance keeps rising, and the balance sheet is cleaner than a year ago. What changed on 2026-11-04 is the cost of that growth: credit provisions and funding costs are absorbing the operating leverage, so earnings per share stalled this year.

The single most important thing to weigh: the credit-loss rate. If provisions settle back toward 17% of revenue as CashAI v6 seasons (September's sub-2% delinquency is the early sign), the 2027 earnings path near $20 per share is intact; if they keep rising with larger advance sizes, the business is a fast-growing lender whose returns are being competed or regulated away.

**Moat:** narrow. The edge is underwriting data (CashAI on linked-account cash flows) and scale in small-dollar advances, visible in record net monetization and stable delinquency while advance sizes grow. Gross margin has held near 70% non-GAAP; the screener's reported ROIC (-2.8%) is distorted by lender accounting and the warrant history, so it is not evidence of erosion. Trend: stable.

**Secular threat:** manageable. Earned-wage-access and cash-advance competition (Chime, Cash App, employer-paid programs) and regulation of small-dollar credit fees are the structural forces; none points to a shrinking base today, and the DOJ suit is a fine-and-conduct risk rather than a ban on the product. The cheapness reads as a fear print on credit and litigation, not a value trap.
