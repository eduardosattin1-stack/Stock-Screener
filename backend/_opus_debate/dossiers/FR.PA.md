# Valeo SE (FR.PA) - a deep dive on management and the setup

As of 2026-10-26. Price used: EUR 11.75 (Euronext Paris close 2026-10-26, FMP quote), down from EUR 13.51 when this committee last looked on 2026-10-12. Transcripts available: full-year 2023 results (2024-03-01) and first-half 2026 results (2026-07-22); the third-quarter 2026 sales release (2026-10-22) is checked from press coverage.

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story has narrowed from growth to cash. In March 2024 the pitch was the "Move Up" plan: record order intake of almost EUR 35 billion, "double-digit growth" (+11% like-for-like, meaning stripped of currency and acquisitions), and a promise that higher-margin orders would lift profitability "beyond 2025". By July 2026 the frame is "Elevate '28" with three engines in a deliberate order: "profitability and cash, are now confirmed ... And the third engine is ready, the return to growth from '27." Growth has been pushed out by roughly three years; what is being sold today is margin and free cash flow (cash left after capital spending).

New threads have appeared as the core auto story slowed: "beyond automotive" opportunities (battery storage, defence drones, data-centre cooling proofs of concept, humanoid robot components), explicitly "not factored into Elevate '28". China has quietly been re-pitched from a recovery market to a Chinese-OEM market (more than 80% of China orders now from local carmakers) because the international carmakers Valeo served there are losing share. Electrification, the 2023 centrepiece (EUR 6 billion of high-voltage orders), is now talked about mainly as a cost turnaround inside the POWER division.

Returns remain poor: return on invested capital 3.6%, return on equity 5.5%, Altman Z (a solvency score; below 1.8 is the distress zone) 1.3, Piotroski 7/9 (improving fundamentals).

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

Stable-to-defensive. March 2024 was offensive ("we could secure a lot of orders", "bring back Valeo where it needs to be"). July 2026 is confident about what management controls (cost, cash, pricing with customers: "outstanding progress" on passing memory-chip inflation through) and openly agnostic about what it does not: on China, "I have no idea of what the global OEMs will do". The H2 2026 wording shows the drift: in July the group expected the second-half margin and cash "at least in line with H1"; at the 2026-10-22 third-quarter release this became "in line" with H1, removing the implied upside. The full-year guidance (sales EUR 20-21B, operating margin 4.7-5.3%, FCF above EUR 400M) was confirmed.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

Medium-High on margin and cash, Low on growth. 2023 delivered "100% of our 2023 guidance" and the H1 2026 margin sat 0.3 points above the bottom of the 2026 range. But the 2023 order-book promise of continued growth did not convert (sales fell in 2024 and 2025), and the screener shows only 2 of 7 quarterly earnings beats (29%). The pattern is a company that hits ranges it sets conservatively on margin and cash, while top-line ambitions slide.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

- China: repeated questions on whether the H2 China decline means the 2027 outperformance is at risk; answered with Chinese-OEM order statistics rather than a number.
- Capex sustainability: how capex can fall while orders rise; answered with "buy better, reuse better, standardize better", guidance unchanged.
- Tax rate of 48% from dividend repatriation, expected to stay high through 2026-27.
- Customer compensation for cancelled EV programmes is booked as a commitment, not cash (one customer paying over four years, about EUR 300M working-capital drag in 2025).
- After Q3 2026, UBS (Neutral, EUR 13 target) called the implied fourth-quarter sales trend of about -1.3% "somewhat optimistic" against production forecasts and flagged no update on non-auto businesses; BofA had cut to Sell with a EUR 13 target on 2026-10-07.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

- Red: Sales shrinking three years running and -2.6% LFL in Q3 2026 with POWER -10% LFL.
- Red: Net debt of EUR 3.8B including leases against a EUR 2.8B equity value; Altman Z 1.3.
- Red: H2 2026 guidance language softened on 2026-10-22 from "at least in line" to "in line".
- Red: Effective tax rate around 48% and restructuring charges keep net income near EUR 200M despite EUR 1B of operating margin.
- Green: H1 2026 FCF more than doubled and net debt fell in a first half for the first time in ten years.
- Green: BRAIN (driving assistance and software-defined vehicle) back to growth, +8.2% LFL in Q3 2026; GM central compute award with a new Texas plant.
- Green: Order intake 1.4x OEM sales in H1 2026 at a gross margin above 20%.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

The vocabulary shifted from "technologies" to "margins" ("We don't like technologies, we like margins", 2024) and now to "cash" as the top priority, which is the language of a deleveraging story rather than a growth story. The beyond-auto list grows each call while carrying no numbers, which is narrative padding rather than value. The CFO's admission that the high tax rate will persist "for the years '26 and '27" quietly pushes the earnings recovery for shareholders out by two years.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

Trajectory: stable. Management is executing a credible self-help programme on cost and cash in a shrinking auto market, and the 2026 targets look safe after Q3. The single most important thing to weigh: the equity is a thin slice on top of EUR 3.8B of net debt, so small changes in margin or the market's multiple swing the share price heavily in both directions; the cheap free-cash-flow multiple (roughly 6x 2026 guided cash) is cheap for a reason until sales grow again in 2027.

Moat: switching costs on multi-year vehicle platforms and scale in lighting and driving-assistance sensors give a narrow but real moat; returns are low (ROIC 3.6%) but margins are rising, so it is stable, not eroding. Secular threat: manageable. The shift to electric vehicles hurt POWER and Chinese carmakers take share from Valeo's international customers, but Valeo is positioned in the growth content (sensors, compute, thermal for batteries) and is winning Chinese-OEM orders. The cheapness is mostly a leverage-and-cyclicality discount, not a structurally melting base.
