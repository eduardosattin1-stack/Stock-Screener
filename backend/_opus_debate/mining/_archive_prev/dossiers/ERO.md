# Forensic dossier — Ero Copper Corp. (NYSE/TSX: ERO)

Prepared 2026-08-04. Price used throughout: **$28.76 per share on the New York listing, in U.S. dollars** (last trade 2026-08-04; the company also trades in Canadian dollars in Toronto — every figure here is the U.S.-dollar line). Market value about $3.0 billion on roughly 105.0 million fully diluted shares. Fifty-two-week range $13.37 to $39.80.

Source material: five consecutive quarterly earnings calls (March 2025 through March 2026), the screener's trailing-twelve-month financial block through 2026-03-31, and dated company releases verified on the web (see section 2).

---

## 1. Narrative arc

The story management has told across these five calls is unusually consistent in structure and unusually honest about its own stumbles. In March 2025, incoming chief executive Makko DeFilippo laid out a four-step plan and has repeated it, in the same order, on every call since: get the new Tucumã mine to commercial production, pay down debt, advance the Furnas growth project, then start returning cash to shareholders. That discipline is rare and it makes the company easy to grade.

The arc runs in three acts.

**Act one — apology and repair (March 2025 to May 2025).** The first two calls are about things that went wrong at Tucumã: a multi-week power outage, "conventional teething pains," a damaged third tailings filter. Management was candid that the dollar cost was trivial ("on the order of $2 million") but the time cost was not. Commercial production was promised "over the coming weeks" in May 2025.

**Act two — the reset (August 2025).** This is the pivotal call. Commercial production at Tucumã was finally declared, but full-year 2025 guidance was cut, and the chief executive said the quiet part out loud: "the high end of our guidance across the board is still at the low end of our prior guidance." He reframed the year as "2 halves" and coined the line "late to the station, but full steam ahead." A management team that cuts guidance and names the size of the cut in one sentence is behaving well; the cut is still a cut.

**Act three — delivery and a new growth leg (November 2025 to March 2026).** The November call reported all-time monthly production records at all three mines in October, plus a genuinely creative piece of value engineering: a decade of high-grade gold concentrate sitting in a stockpile was sampled, converted into a maiden inferred resource of 24,000 tonnes at roughly 37 grams per tonne (about 29,000 ounces of gold), and started being sold at an operating cost of only $300 to $500 an ounce. By March 2026 the emphasis had shifted decisively to growth: the maiden economic study on the Furnas copper-gold project, published 2026-02-23, and a claim that Ero is "exiting a major investment cycle" while peers are entering theirs.

What was quietly dropped: step four. "Initiate returns to shareholders" has been on the slide since March 2025 and, five quarters later, there is still no dividend and no buyback. Each call defers it one more quarter ("let's get through steps one and two"). That is the one place where the narrative has run ahead of the delivery.

## 2. Claims versus financials (forensic)

Every company figure below was verified against the dated release named in the right-hand column.

| Management claim | The evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Commercial production at Tucumã" — promised "over the coming weeks" (May 2025 call) | Declared during the second quarter of 2025, roughly two quarters after the original schedule | Delivered late |
| 2025 full-year guidance reaffirmed (March and May 2025) | Cut on the 2025-08-01 call, then the low end of the revised range achieved | Missed, then reset honestly |
| "Significant deleveraging" (March 2025) | Net debt was $490.7 million at 2026-03-31, down only about $11 million from year-end 2025; leverage 1.0 times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, versus the 1.5 times target set in March 2025 | Target met, pace slower than the word "significant" implies |
| 2026 cash-cost guidance of $2.15 to $2.35 per pound of copper (2026-05-04 release) | First-quarter 2026 actual was **$2.39 per pound**, above the range; Caraíba $2.79, Tucumã $1.97 | Running hot; the year needs a strong second half |
| "Increasing cash generation capacity, declining consolidated capital requirements" (March 2026) | 2026 capital budget is **$275 to $320 million**, versus $267 million actually spent in 2025 — the midpoint is higher, not lower | Contradicted by the company's own guidance |
| Revenue growth of about 89% year on year | Confirmed: trailing-twelve-month revenue $925.2 million to 2026-03-31 | Verified |
| Record profitability | Trailing earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation of $541.8 million, a 58.6% margin; first-quarter 2026 net income $108.8 million, or $1.04 a diluted share | Verified |

**The critical divergence — margin direction.** Gross margin (revenue less the direct cost of production, as a share of revenue) was **39.8% in the first quarter of 2026 against 44.4% in the same quarter of 2025** — down about 4.6 percentage points while revenue more than doubled, from $125.1 million to $259.5 million. Volume and price did all the work; unit costs moved the wrong way. The fourth quarter of 2025 printed a flattering 50.9%, but that was lifted by roughly 15,000 ounces of one-off gold-concentrate stockpile sales carrying a $300-to-$500-an-ounce cost. Strip that out and the underlying trend is: revenue up sharply, margin per unit down.

**The proxy versus the company's own numbers.** Our deterministic screen reads a 58.6% trailing margin, the 62nd percentile of an eight-name cost cohort. The company's own reported cash cost of $2.39 a pound says something less flattering: this is a **second-half-of-the-cost-curve producer**, not a low-cost one. Both readings point the same direction — "respectable, not privileged" — but the margin proxy flatters, because it is struck on a copper price at an all-time high. The unit-cost figure is the honest one, and it is the one that will still be true when copper is not at a record.

**Prices, dated.** Copper on the London Metal Exchange was about $13,799 a tonne (roughly $6.26 a pound) on 2026-07-30, at record levels; the New York contract was $6.65 a pound on 2026-08-04. Ero sells concentrate out of Brazil, so the London price is the relevant one. Ero's own realised price in the first quarter of 2026 was **$5.57 a pound**. For context, the company's Furnas study uses a long-term copper price of **$4.60 a pound**. Today's earnings are being struck roughly 36% above the price the company itself considers normal.

**The gold side has turned, and nobody has said so yet.** On the March 2026 call management named "a big tailwind from byproduct gold prices." Gold futures are $4,157.50 an ounce today against a fifty-two-week high of $5,626.80 — down about 26%. That tailwind reversed after the call and hits three places at once: the gold credit that lowers Caraíba's copper cash cost, the Xavantina mine, and the remaining concentrate stockpile. First-quarter 2026 all-in sustaining cost at Xavantina was **$4,441 an ounce** against a gold price of about $4,157 — on an all-in basis, the gold mine did not make money in the quarter.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, from a low base — and specific throughout.**

- *March 2025:* defensive and granular. Failures described in mechanical detail ("valve dimensioning, small component and pipe weld failures"). Confidence low, candour high.
- *May 2025:* hedged. "We are taking a measured approach here" on declaring commercial production; the finance chief conceded the designation "is a very subjective measure."
- *August 2025:* the honest trough. "This fundamental groundwork was necessary and nonlinear." Guidance cut, and quantified in one sentence.
- *November 2025:* confidence returns with evidence attached, not adjectives — all-time record monthly tonnages at all three mines, a debottlenecking done "at effectively 0 cost," mechanisation delivering "about a 30% reduction in mining unit cost."
- *March 2026:* the most forward-leaning call of the five, and the first with promotional edges. "Three operating mines with the right mix of metals at exactly the right time in the commodity price cycle" and "many companies, including most of our peers, are jumping into major project builds within the next year. We like this dynamic." That is a market call dressed as an operating statement, and it arrives with copper at a record.

The language moved from defensive (repair, maintenance, groundwork) to offensive (growth, cycle positioning, capital markets day) exactly as the copper price moved to a record. That is normal, and it is also the point in a cycle where an analyst should press hardest.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Medium, improving.** The record is: one clear miss, honestly disclosed and repriced; then two quarters of delivery against the revised numbers.

- 2025 production and cost guidance was reaffirmed twice and then cut in August 2025. The low end of the revised range was achieved.
- Commercial production at Tucumã slipped roughly two quarters against the original schedule.
- The Furnas economic study was promised for the first half of 2026 on four consecutive calls and **published 2026-02-23** — early. That is a real credibility deposit.
- 2026 guidance was set deliberately at "steady state," explicitly excluding the additional tailings-filtration equipment that could add throughput in the fourth quarter. That is sandbagging in the good sense: excluding an upside the company expects to deliver.
- But 2026 cash costs are already running above the range in the first quarter, and management's own second-quarter framing was that Caraíba would be "broadly similar to Q1." The second-half recovery is doing all the work.

There is no evidence of the sector's classic sins here. Share count went from 103.1 million to 105.0 million diluted over the eight quarters to March 2026 — growth of about 1.8%, essentially option issuance. **This is not a serial diluter.** Growth capital came from debt, a single $104 million share issue in 2023, and a gold stream — a real cost, but not shareholder shredding.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

Four things analysts return to, and how management handles them:

1. **When do shareholder returns start?** Asked in March 2025, answered "a bit too early to talk about the playoffs." Asked again in August 2025, answered "the first focus is obviously to get the balance sheet back to where we want it to be." Five quarters of deferral on a stated priority.
2. **Are costs structurally rising?** Roald Ross of Clarksons pressed in November 2025 on an 8% rise in mining cost and a 28% rise in processing cost at Caraíba. The answer was "normalise that for volume." The first quarter of 2026 then printed above cost guidance. The analyst was asking the right question.
3. **How much of Furnas is actually drilled?** Anita Soni of CIBC extracted the key admission in March 2026: "about 60% of the material that we have, including the PEA is inferred" — inferred meaning the lowest-confidence resource category, not yet good enough to put in a mine plan. Management answered straight, but this is the number that should govern how much credit the project gets.
4. **Concentrate sales terms.** Nobody asked, and management volunteered nothing. When pressed in March 2026 on smelter charges the answer was that these are "long-term contracts that are commercially sensitive." **No offtake counterparty is named in any of the five calls, and I could not find one in the public releases.** For a business selling 100% of its output as concentrate, that is a genuine disclosure gap — noted, not alleged.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Red flags**

- *Cost guidance already breached (first quarter 2026).* $2.39 a pound against a $2.15-to-$2.35 range, with the second quarter guided "broadly similar."
- *Capital spending is rising into a record copper price (March 2026 guidance).* $275 to $320 million budgeted against $267 million spent in 2025, while the narrative says capital requirements are "declining." Peak-cycle spending is the sector's besetting sin, and the free-cash-flow record shows the pattern already ran once: capital expenditure of $182 million in 2021, $296 million in 2022, $461 million in 2023, $338 million in 2024 — producing free cash flow of **minus $152 million, minus $298 million and minus $192 million** in 2022, 2023 and 2024 respectively.
- *Promotional cycle-timing language (March 2026).* "Exactly the right time in the commodity price cycle," and an explicit favourable comparison to peers' capital plans.
- *Step four still undelivered after five quarters.*
- *The gold tailwind named in March 2026 has since reversed about 26%*, and Xavantina's first-quarter all-in cost of $4,441 an ounce sat above the gold price.
- *Currency protection is rolling off.* The Brazilian real collars, $240 million notional with a floor of 5.53 and ceiling of 6.52 reais to the dollar, ran only through June 2026. Whether they were replaced is not established in these materials.
- *Upside was capped once before.* Zero-cost copper collars over 3,000 tonnes a month from April to September 2025, floor $4.00 and average ceiling $4.68 a pound, gave away the upside in a rising market. Whether similar collars exist for 2026 is not disclosed in these materials — a material unknown with copper at $6.26.

**Green flags**

- *Tucumã works, and it is the low-cost asset.* $1.97 a pound in the first quarter of 2026 against Caraíba's $2.79 — the capital spent in 2022 to 2024 bought a genuinely better cost position, which is the good version of heavy capital spending.
- *The gold-concentrate stockpile is real value engineering.* Roughly 29,000 ounces created from waste at a $300-to-$500-an-ounce cost, delivering about 15,000 ounces in the fourth quarter of 2025.
- *Mechanisation at Xavantina delivered.* Completed ahead of the June 2026 plan, fully mechanised by September 2025, with a roughly 30% cut in mining unit cost in local currency.
- *The Furnas study landed early and reads well.* Published 2026-02-23: an after-tax net present value of about $2.0 billion at an 8% discount rate, a 27% internal rate of return on $1.3 billion of initial capital, a 24-year life, and first-quartile cash costs of about $0.24 a pound. Ero earns 60% from Vale Base Metals on completing the work programme; Vale receives up to an 11% free carry on construction capital.
- *No equity destruction.* No buybacks executed at the top, no dilution of consequence, no dividend cut to reverse.
- *Named, credible partners.* Vale Base Metals at Furnas; Royal Gold on the Xavantina gold stream, extended in March 2025 for $50 million, bringing total stream proceeds to $160 million.

## 7. Hidden signals

- **The vocabulary flipped from process to positioning.** Through 2025 the words were "groundwork," "back-to-basics," "predictive maintenance," "asset integrity." By March 2026 they are "cornerstone asset," "financeable and buildable," "we like this dynamic." Operators talk about maintenance; promoters talk about cycles. This team has earned the benefit of the doubt, but the register changed at the same time the copper price did.
- **Investor relations was professionalised in mid-2025** — a dedicated head of investor relations joined in July 2025, and a capital markets day in São Paulo was announced for mid-September 2026. Companies staff up for the story when they intend to tell it more loudly.
- **"Late to the station, but full steam ahead" is a well-constructed excuse.** It concedes the delay while asserting the destination. It worked, because the fourth quarter of 2025 and the annualised second half validated it. It should not work twice.
- **The Furnas capital number deserves a second look.** A $1.3 billion initial build sits against a company with a $3.0 billion market value and about $491 million of net debt. Ero's 60% share, with Vale carried on up to 11% of construction capital, still implies a funding requirement that this balance sheet cannot carry from cash flow at a normal copper price. That is a future financing event the current share price is not obviously discounting.
- **Reserve lives are adequate but not long, and the mine plan is pulling grade forward.** Caraíba was extended to a 20-year life at its last major reserve update. Tucumã's published reserve of about 43.1 million tonnes at 0.83% copper — roughly 356,600 tonnes contained — was framed as a 12-year life at an average of just over 27,000 tonnes a year; the 2026 run rate is closer to 36,000 tonnes, which implies nearer ten years and confirms management's own repeated statement that early grades are running "2.5 times to 3 times above the life of mine average feed." The high-grade years are now.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict (for the director)

**Trajectory: strengthening.** The operational turnaround is real and is evidenced by numbers, not adjectives: three mines at record output, a new low-cost mine at commercial production, unit costs at Xavantina cut roughly 30%, net debt down and leverage at 1.0 times, a growth study delivered ahead of schedule, and no meaningful shareholder dilution. Management is specific, numerate and has priced its own bad news.

**The single thing to weigh before sizing.** Every one of those improvements is being measured against a copper price at an all-time high and roughly 36% above the $4.60 a pound the company itself uses as its long-term planning price — while unit cash costs are running above guidance and capital spending is guided *up*, not down. The trailing 58.6% margin is a price artefact as much as an operating achievement. The question is not whether this is a better company than it was eighteen months ago; it plainly is. The question is what it is worth if copper spends 2027 nearer $4.50 than $6.25, with $275 to $320 million a year of capital commitments and $491 million of net debt still on the balance sheet. Size to the second question, not the first.

**Economic moat: narrow, and stable.** In mining the only durable moat is position on the cost curve, and Ero's is mixed: Tucumã at $1.97 a pound is genuinely competitive, Caraíba at $2.79 is not, and the blended $2.39 places the company in the back half of the global cost curve. Returns support the "narrow but real" reading rather than a wide one — return on invested capital of about 13.8% and return on equity of about 31.2% on the trailing year, but struck at record metal prices, so the level flatters the durability. The trend is stable to slightly improving: Tucumã's growing share of the mix pulls the consolidated cost down, and the Caraíba shaft due in 2027 should help further. There is a second, softer moat in the land position — Tucumã is described as the only sulphide mill on the western side of the Carajás district — which has real strategic value but does not show up in current returns.

**Secular threat: manageable.** Copper is on the winning side of electrification, not the losing side; there is no structural demand-destruction force impairing terminal value here. The real long-run risks are ordinary and cyclical rather than terminal: single-country concentration in Brazil, local cost inflation and currency swings, orebody depletion, and the capital call that a Furnas construction decision would eventually bring. Cheapness, where it appears, would be a cycle question and not a melting-ice-cube question — but at today's price the more relevant observation is that the shares are not cheap.
