# Fortuna Mining Corp. (FSM) — Forensic Dossier

*Prepared 2026-08-04. Price used throughout: US$9.075 per share on the New York Stock Exchange (Fortuna is dual-listed; the Toronto listing trades as FVI in Canadian dollars). Sources: five consecutive quarterly earnings calls from May 2025 through May 2026, the company's own news releases dated April through July 2026, and financial data from the screener and Financial Modeling Prep.*

---

## 1. Narrative arc

Fortuna's story over these five calls is a deliberate, and largely honest, act of shrinking in order to grow.

In **May 2025** the company was a five-mine silver-and-gold producer with two problem assets. Management spent that call announcing their disposal: San Jose in Mexico ("our highest cost mine and was nearing the end of its mineral reserves") and Yaramoko in Burkina Faso, sold for $70 million cash plus a $57.5 million pre-closing dividend, with the rationale stated plainly — "a prudent exit from a country where we are no longer investing in mineral exploration and where the business and security environment continues to present challenges." The emphasis was cash flow records and cost control.

By **August 2025** the framing had changed to a rebuild. Chief executive Jorge Ganoza opened by conceding the arithmetic: 2024 production was a record 460,000 gold-equivalent ounces, "with the sale of these mines, our annualized production is now roughly 330,000 ounces." He then introduced the number that has anchored every call since — "rebuilding production to 0.5 million ounces per year... is fully under our control" — and named the two assets that would do it: expanding the Séguéla mine in Côte d'Ivoire and building the Diamba Sud project in Senegal.

**November 2025** added the financial proof: the Diamba Sud preliminary economic assessment (an early-stage economic study) landed in mid-October with a 72% after-tax return at a $2,750 gold price, and net cash had climbed to $266 million.

**February 2026** was the most promotional call of the set. Reserves were up, the Diamba budget was set at $100 million for the year, a Séguéla plant expansion toward 200,000 ounces was floated, and the chief executive aggregated every resource category into a single headline figure — "the aggregate is over 7 million ounces" — while acknowledging in the same breath that "the regulators don't like" that arithmetic.

**May 2026** returned to delivery: record sales of $342 million, record free cash flow of $174 million, record adjusted profit of $111 million, and $493 million of net cash. The growth vocabulary tightened from "24 months" to naming the two studies due in May.

What was quietly dropped: silver. Fortuna renamed itself from Fortuna Silver Mines and the segment data confirms the substance of the change — gold doré (unrefined gold bars) is essentially 100% of the revenue line reported for 2025. Mexico is also being wound down; by May 2026 the chief executive said flatly, "We will likely be reducing dramatically our presence in Mexico." What was added: a new frontier, with an earn-in agreement in Guyana announced April 2026 and interest expressed in Suriname.

The one place the story is materially richer than the trajectory justifies is the headline ounce target. The one place it is poorer than the numbers deserve is cash generation: revenue actually *fell* 9.6% in 2025 (to $960 million, because two mines were sold), yet gross profit *rose* 36% and free cash flow rose 82%. That is a better outcome than the narrative gives itself credit for.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, and increasingly backed by evidence.**

- *May 2025* — defensive-constructive. The quarter was spent explaining disposals. "This was a timely and strategic divestment."
- *August 2025* — the confidence inflection, and the first promotional flourish: "In 20 years, I have never seen Fortuna as strong as it is today." Notably, he had used almost the same line on the prior call.
- *November 2025* — specific and operational. Costs discussed mine by mine with directions of travel: Lindero trending down toward $1,500, Séguéla trending up toward $1,738 on capital timing.
- *February 2026* — the most expansive. "Fortuna's growth to over 0.5 million ounces is visible, controlled and executable." Also the resource-aggregation moment.
- *May 2026* — confident but with a defensive tell: the chief executive pre-empted the cost line before any analyst could raise it, opening the cost discussion himself with "I want to briefly address all-in sustaining cost in the first quarter: $2,107 per gold equivalent ounce," and attributing $122 of it to royalties and share-based pay. Getting out in front of a bad number is better practice than hiding it, but the need to do so is itself the signal.

Guidance has become *more* specific over time, not less — the opposite of the pattern that usually precedes trouble.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Reliability: High, with one operational exception.**

What they promised and delivered:

- Séguéla exceeded the upper end of its production guidance in **both 2024 and 2025** (152,420 ounces in 2025, 4% above the top of the range), and beat the mine plan in every single quarter across these five calls.
- The Diamba Sud sequence was promised in May 2025 as "by mid-2026, we are in a position to be able to call a construction decision." Delivered: preliminary economic study October 2025, environmental decree **June 15, 2026**, feasibility study **June 29, 2026**, technical report filed **July 13, 2026**. That is a dated promise kept almost to the month, in a sector where such promises routinely slip by years.
- Consolidated 2025 production guidance was achieved (317,001 gold-equivalent ounces).
- Reserves grew 15% year-over-year *after* depletion, to 3.0 million ounces (April 23, 2026).

Where they missed:

- **Lindero** produced 87,489 ounces in 2025, about 6% below the bottom of guidance, entirely because of two mechanical failures in the fourth quarter. The engineering review found "structural fatigue risk in the primary crusher foundations," requiring a 35-day replacement in late March 2026. This is a maintenance lapse, not bad luck.
- **Caylloma** finished outside its guidance range in 2025 on metal-price ratios.
- **Consolidated all-in sustaining cost** in Q1 2026 came in above the full-year range.

No sandbagging pattern is visible; if anything Séguéla is guided conservatively and beats, while Lindero is guided optimistically and misses.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

Coverage is remarkably thin for a company of this size, and that shapes what gets asked. Only one price target printed in the last quarter — $14.50 from Eric Winmill on July 30, 2026, when the stock was $8.37. Five targets over the past year averaged $11.70.

- **The 500,000-ounce target draws open skepticism.** In the May 2025 call an unidentified analyst said, after the chief executive's confidence speech, "I don't disagree with you. I just don't understand how they're about to do that." Management's answer has been to name the projects, not to defend the timeline.
- **Cost trajectory is the recurring pressure point.** Mohamed Sidibe of National Bank pushed twice on the capital and all-in-cost cadence (August 2025), and the answers were about timing rather than level. The level has since drifted up.
- **What nobody is asking about**: the $50–70 million savings programme announced in August 2025 has never been revisited. Nor has anyone pressed on why consolidated all-in sustaining cost guidance rose while the two highest-cost mines were sold — the answer is royalties on a higher gold price and heavier waste stripping, but it deserves scrutiny rather than assumption.
- **The friendly-questioner problem.** Several questioners across these calls are private investors and newsletter writers rather than analysts — one told management on the May 2025 call, "I've been buying all the way down on the sell-off... this is a fantastic deal as I'm concerned." A retail-heavy call is a call with less adversarial pressure on management.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Red flags**

- 🔴 **The headline target has quietly slipped about a year.** February 2026: 500,000 ounces "over the next 24 months." July 29, 2026 news release: "toward 500,000 ounces by 2028." The feasibility study puts Diamba's first gold in Q2 2028 and the expanded Séguéla plant ramping in the second half of 2028, which makes 2029 the first full year at the target.
- 🔴 **Resource aggregation.** February 2026: reserves, indicated and inferred resources summed to "over 7 million ounces," with the caveat that regulators disapprove — a promoter's number in an otherwise disciplined presentation. The defensible figure is 3.0 million ounces of proven and probable reserves.
- 🔴 **Séguéla's 2026 step-up landed at the low end** of the range management had been describing as "approximately 180,000 ounces."
- 🔴 **Two builds committed at an all-time-high gold price.** Diamba Sud at $397.5 million, the Séguéla expansion at $109 million, and roughly $25 million of extra capital to self-operate the Sunbird underground mine. Spending the windfall at the top of the cycle is the sector's besetting sin, and the chief executive's own February 2026 remark — "everybody now wants to build a gold mine and the delivery times on the critical equipment... are quickly going to come in high demand and shortage" — is an accurate description of the cost inflation he is walking into.
- 🔴 **A decade of dilution.** Weighted average shares went from 129 million in 2015 to 309 million in 2024, a 2.4-fold increase, principally the 2021 Roxgold acquisition and equity raised in 2017 and 2020.
- 🔴 **Two contractor fatalities at Séguéla** — one in February 2025, one disclosed in the July 2026 production release — against an otherwise outstanding lost-time-injury record.
- 🔴 **Lindero's crusher foundations** failed from structural fatigue, an engineering issue on the company's second-largest mine, in a jurisdiction (Argentina) with currency and repatriation friction that cost $10 million in foreign-exchange losses in the first nine months of 2025 alone.

**Green flags**

- 🟢 **They sold the bad mines before they became a problem**, in April and May 2025, for $84 million gross plus a $57.5 million dividend, and avoided roughly $50 million of closure spending plus years of management attention.
- 🟢 **The balance sheet transformation is the cleanest fact in the file**: net cash from $137 million to $493 million in four quarters, $816 million of total liquidity, and no equity issued since 2021.
- 🟢 **Dilution has reversed into meaningful buybacks** — roughly 3.5% of the company retired over the past nine months, with the largest tranche bought at an average $9.32, below our estimate of intrinsic value.
- 🟢 **Séguéla is a genuinely low-cost orebody**: $679 per ounce cash cost in 2025, plant running 40% above nameplate throughput, and reserves and resources still growing.
- 🟢 **Diamba Sud's feasibility economics are unusually good**: a 60% after-tax return and one-year payback at $3,500 gold, with average all-in sustaining cost of $1,056 per ounce over the first four years — materially below the company's current consolidated cost. If built, it improves Fortuna's position on the industry cost curve rather than merely adding ounces.
- 🟢 **Growth is self-funded**, with the entire 2026 capital programme covered by operating cash flow.

## 7. Hidden signals

- **The vocabulary shifted from ounces to value, and then held.** August 2025: "We have no need to chase ounces. We are chasing value." Fortuna has honoured this — no acquisitions since, only a 15% stake in Awalé Resources, an earn-in in Guyana, and joint ventures. For a company with $816 million of liquidity at a gold-price peak, *not* buying anything is the single most encouraging capital-allocation signal in the file.
- **The buyback tempo is the real disclosure.** $12.1 million, then $20.3 million, then $80.2 million across three quarters. Management is telling you, with cash rather than words, what it thinks the shares are worth.
- **Geographic rotation, stated openly.** Out of Burkina Faso (2025) and effectively out of Mexico ("we will likely be reducing dramatically our presence"), into Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Guinea and now the Guyana Shield. The chief executive is candid that this is the trade: "mining has always been a frontier business... What do we ask in exchange for taking the higher perceived geopolitical risk? ...Our time to cash flow is very short." That is an honest framing of a real risk, not a denial of it.
- **State participation is a permanent tax on both growth assets.** Côte d'Ivoire holds a 10% free-carried interest in Séguéla; Senegal takes 10% free-carried at Diamba Sud plus a 3.5% royalty and a 0.5% social fund contribution, with an option for a further 25% contributory interest. Any valuation must be built on the attributable share, not the headline.
- **The chief executive and chief financial officer are brothers and the company is co-founder-led.** That has produced consistency and long-horizon thinking here, but it is a governance concentration worth naming.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.**

Fortuna has done, in sequence, the four things a mining company is supposed to do and rarely does: sold its worst assets before they became liabilities, refused to acquire anything at the top of the cycle, built a large net cash position, and started buying back its own stock in size. The reserve base grew 15% after depletion. The one project it is building carries feasibility economics — a 60% after-tax return, one-year payback, and a first-four-year cost of $1,056 an ounce — that would improve the company's cost position rather than dilute it.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing:** the entire equity story now rests on two construction projects being executed simultaneously, in West Africa, into the most inflationary equipment and labour market the gold industry has seen in fifteen years — and the free cash flow that makes the shares look cheap today will be consumed by that capital programme through 2027 and into 2028. You are not buying a cash machine; you are buying a cash machine that has just committed roughly $530 million of announced growth capital, at a gold price that has never been higher. Judge the sizing on what happens to this company if gold returns to $3,000 an ounce mid-build, not on what the trailing numbers look like at $4,154.

**Economic moat: narrow, and stable.** The moat here is scale-and-cost — a specific orebody advantage rather than a franchise. Séguéla's $679-per-ounce cash cost and 40%-above-nameplate throughput are a real, durable cost advantage that competitors cannot replicate because they do not own that rock. But it is narrow: it rests on one mine out of three, and consolidated all-in sustaining cost is drifting *up* (2026 guidance $1,830–1,975 an ounce, above the industry's better operators) on royalties and waste stripping. Returns support the "stable, not eroding" read — return on invested capital of 17.1% and return on equity of 20.8% on a trailing basis, both rising, with gross margin expanding from 22.6% (2023) to 32.4% (2024) to 48.7% (2025). Diamba Sud would widen the moat if delivered on budget; a cost overrun would narrow it.

**Secular threat: manageable.** Nothing structural is displacing gold mining. The genuine terminal risks here are political rather than technological — resource nationalism in West Africa (Fortuna has already lived through it in Burkina Faso and Mexico) and the state participation and royalty escalation embedded in both growth jurisdictions. The cheapness at 3.1 times enterprise value to trailing earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation is therefore a **risk-and-cycle discount, not a melting-base value trap** — but it is also not a free lunch: the trailing earnings in that ratio are earned at a record metal price.
