MINING BOOK, LANE A - DIRECTOR'S MEMO, 11 AUGUST 2026

(a) How the names were scored, and what valuation was allowed to do

Four pillars of twenty-five points each, applied only to names that cleared the gates. First,
cost-curve position and the quality of the torque - how much cash profit moves for a 10% move in
the commodity, and whether that swing is taken from a low-cost or a marginal position. Second, the
contracting cycle and reserve life, which for a royalty company means the paying-asset book. Third,
capital discipline and the balance sheet - this sector's besetting sin is building at the top, so
last-cycle behaviour is scored, not just today's leverage. Fourth, a valuation guard.

The guard is a guard, not a ranking pillar. Every name starts with full marks and loses them only
for danger: a price well above the reconciled value of the parts, or earnings struck on a peak
commodity price that must be normalised before any cheapness is credited. It vetoed one name, capped
several, and in no case lifted a name above another that passed clean. Teck is the clearest
illustration - it carries the largest discount in the universe, about 29%, and it is a runner-up, not
a seat, because a discount cannot buy rank.

Catalysts played no part. This book has the event overlay fully removed: a live catalyst is neither
a plus nor a requirement, and the dated-event book is elsewhere.

One methodological point that changed several rankings materially. Where the debate verified
company-reported cost and margin figures against the automated screen and the two disagreed, the
company figure won, as the rubric requires. That correction cut Hudbay (the screened 66.5% margin
embeds a $322M non-cash impairment reversal; the honest figure is 51%), cut Southern Copper's
cost-curve credit (the near-zero cash cost is a by-product credit, not a mining achievement), and
lifted Rio Tinto by roughly ten points of margin (the screen summed four half-year periods instead of
two) and Vale (the screened 25% margin and 1.5 times net debt are both corrupted by December 2025
non-cash charges; the true figures are about 42% and 0.97 times).

(b) Excluded and capped

By the solvency gate:
  - No eligible name reads weak on funded debt, so the gate excluded nobody. Alcoa and Teck read

By the valuation guard:
  - Franco-Nevada - vetoed. A reconciled margin-of-safety reading of about minus 53%, past the level
    at which the reconciled fair value cannot reach the price. A superb business - a cash cost of $341
    per gold-equivalent ounce, roughly a fifth of a typical producer's all-in cost - at a price this
    book will not pay.
  - Wheaton Precious Metals - capped out at about minus 26%, and blocked independently by the
    three-name limit on the precious-metals chain.
  - Southern Copper - seated but cut to 0.40 units, the smallest seat, on a guard reading of about
    minus 10% and a price roughly 20% above the reconciled value of the parts.
  - Cameco - seated at 0.50 units on a guard reading of about minus 21%; kept for its contract book
    and because it is the only separate risk in the basket, with fresh money held back for evidence.
  - Agnico Eagle - trimmed to 0.50 units on a guard reading of about minus 10%, with earnings struck
    at a gold price that has already begun to fall.
  - Freeport-McMoRan, Ero Copper, Buenaventura and Fortuna Mining - all deducted for a price above
    the reconciled value or for a cost position their own disclosures show to be mid-curve; none
    seated.
  - Alcoa - not seated. Worst cost position in its cohort, an alumina business losing $96M a quarter,
    interest cover of 3.5 times. A taxonomy note for the map while we are here: it is a metal
    converter filed in the copper chain and benchmarked against orebody miners whose margins are
    structurally far higher, so its bottom-of-cohort band overstates the case against it - it is not
    seated on the merits either way.

By the headwind rule:
  - Nobody. Every chain reads neutral in the cycle sidecar, so no seat is clamped by the headwind
    rule and no headwind justification is required or given. The macro note's separate view that
    monetary metals are currently out of favour is a macro read; by this book's rules it cannot move
    a size, and it has not.

Split rule and physical anchors: every name in the input is a producer or a royalty company. No
equipment, services or utility name reached this desk, so there is nothing to report as a
map-merge failure, and every seat carries a specific physical anchor - ore mined, metal poured, or a
contractual claim on ounces produced by a named third party.

(c) Downside torque, seat by seat

Torque cuts both ways, and every seat whose case cites it carries the downside number here.

  - OR Royalties: plus-or-minus 9.7% cash profit per 10% gold move. Downside minus 9.7%, taken from
    a 96.8% cash margin - the lowest-cost position in the book, so the swing is survivable.
  - Rio Tinto: plus-or-minus 24.9%. Downside minus 24.9%, driven by iron ore rather than copper, and
    part of the recent copper cost cut is a gold by-product credit that reverses with the metal.
  - Royal Gold: plus-or-minus 12.7%. Downside minus 12.7% with no cost to cut - a royalty book has
    no operating lever.
  - Hudbay: plus-or-minus 11.3%. Downside minus 11.3%, absorbed from a verified negative cash cost
    per pound - the reason this torque is compounding leverage rather than a coin flip.
  - Agnico Eagle: plus-or-minus 13.2%. Downside minus 13.2%, and it is already happening: the
    realised price fell from about $4,900 to $4,483 between the March and June 2026 quarters.
  - Vale: plus-or-minus 31.9%. Downside minus 31.9% - the largest in the book - with no contracted
    book beneath it, and the beta behind it is an index regression, so read it softly.
  - Cameco: plus-or-minus 30.2%. Downside minus 30.2%, bounded only by roughly 230 million pounds of
    committed long-term contracts; the beta is an exchange-traded-fund regression, so read it softly.
  - Southern Copper: plus-or-minus 13.5%. Downside minus 13.5%, and it understates the true risk,
    because a copper fall would most likely arrive with a fall in the silver and molybdenum credits
    now producing the near-zero headline cost.

(d) Shared commodity factors, and how each was resolved

The shared global-growth and China-demand axis, decomposed first as required. Four of the eight seats
ride it: Hudbay and Southern Copper through copper, Rio Tinto and Vale through seaborne iron ore into
Chinese steel. The measured two-year weekly correlations say these are not four independent bets -
Hudbay against Southern Copper is 0.82, Vale against Southern Copper 0.74, Hudbay against Vale 0.61,
against a book-wide average pairwise figure of 0.57. A China demand shock or a global-growth
downgrade hits all four in the same week. Resolved by keeping with an explicit cap rather than by
diversifying, for the arithmetic reason set out below, and the cap is numeric: the four names are
capped at 2.25 units combined and the two copper names at 1.05 units combined. Southern Copper - the
lowest-scoring seat in the book and the name appearing in both measured breaches - is cut from 0.6 to
0.4 units, the smallest position here.

The gold and real-rate axis. Three of the eight seats ride it: Royal Gold, OR Royalties and Agnico
Eagle. These are the most damning numbers on the page - Royal Gold against Agnico Eagle 0.86, OR
against Agnico Eagle 0.86, Royal Gold against OR 0.84. Two of them are one bet, and the royalties are
emphatically not a diversifier of the producer; all three de-rate together on a real-rate move.
Resolved by keeping with a cap of 2.00 units combined, achieved by cutting Agnico Eagle - the
lowest-scoring of the three, the only one whose price sits above the reconciled value of its parts,
and the only one with no moat in its file - from 0.7 to 0.5 units.

Why diversification was not available on either axis. After the credibility exclusion, seventeen
names remain and they occupy only four chains: precious metals, copper, diversified miners and the
uranium fuel cycle. There is not one rare-earth or strategic-metals name in the input, and exactly one
uranium name, which is already seated. With a limit of three names per chain, and with the only
diversified-miners name that is not also a copper name being Vale, the arithmetic closes: the maximum
number of distinct seats reachable without a fourth copper name is seven, unless the precious-metals
chain carries three. An eight-name slate therefore forces a three-plus-three-plus-two-plus-one shape,
and no swap inside that shape removes an axis - it only exchanges one name on the axis for another.
That is why both resolutions are caps rather than swaps, and it is stated rather than glossed.

The uranium contracting cycle. One seat, Cameco, so no action is required; it is named because the
instruction requires every chain-specific axis to be reported. It is the one genuinely separate risk
in this basket, and the reason its size went up rather than down.

Export controls and rare earths. Zero seats, because the eligible universe contains no rare-earth or
strategic-metals name at all. Reported for completeness; no action is possible.

One standing note on gold. This system does not score the gold price anywhere, by design - the
momentum guard exists because a price-following rule would have bought the January 2026 top. Nothing
above ranks a seat because gold rose.

The prior apex, dated 4 August 2026, carried seven seats: Royal Gold 80, Hudbay 78, OR Royalties 77,

  - OR Royalties 77 to 83. Up six: the 8 July 2026 disclosure confirmed a 96.8% cash margin and 2030

  - Rio Tinto 68 to 82, promoted from reserve. Up fourteen, almost all of it a data correction - the
    screen had summed four half-year periods instead of two, understating the trailing margin by

    showing cash profit up 28% and free cash flow up 75%.
  - Royal Gold 80 to 81. Up one: the 21 July 2026 disclosure of $871 per gold-equivalent ounce
    against $4,506 realised confirmed the widest cash spread in the book.
  - Hudbay 78 to 76. Down two: the cost claim was verified and improved, but the screened margin

  - Agnico Eagle 76 to 73. Down three: the cost position is still the best in the senior tier, but the

  - Vale 70 to 73. Up three on net: the corrected margin (42%, not 25%) and net debt (0.97 times, not

  - Cameco 60 to 67. Up seven: the contract book at roughly 230 million pounds is the strongest

  - Southern Copper 66 to 65. Down one, and the composition changed more than the number: the reserve

Reserve list changes: Teck 74 to 77 and Rio Tinto promoted out of it; BHP added at 72 (the 16 July

(g) Bear rebuttal - the strongest reason each seat is wrong, written before final sizing

  - OR Royalties: cleaning the impossible screened margin lifts the multiple from about 16 to about
    20 times cash earnings, so the cheapest-looking royalty in the book is not actually cheap.
  - Rio Tinto: it is a 55%-iron-ore company being seated in the copper chain, and its own valuation
    discounts Chinese steel structurally - if that discount is too small, the largest part of the
    business is worth less than the base case says.
  - Royal Gold: it is the only royalty here carrying debt, it bought the Sandstorm book at a high gold
    price, and the two valuation readings on file disagree with each other.
  - Hudbay: the high-grade Pampacancha ore that supplied the gold credits behind the negative cash
    cost is depleted, so the first-quartile position may already be historical.
  - Agnico Eagle: the shares trade above the reconciled value of the parts on earnings struck at a
    gold price that has fallen 8% in a quarter, and the file records no moat.
  - Vale: cost guidance has now been raised twice in a year while margin compresses on rising revenue,
    and it carries the book's largest downside torque with no contracted cushion.
  - Cameco: unit cost of sales rose 36% year over year, guidance was raised, and the reconciled value
    sits about a third below the market price - the contract book is bounding a thesis that the
    numbers are currently working against.
  - Southern Copper: strip the silver and molybdenum credit and the mining cost rose 12%; the market
    is paying 20% above the reconciled value for a cost position that is borrowed from other metals.

(h) Macro - what it changed, and what it did not

The cited block reads a debt-cycle phase in which the bond market is imposing positive real rates, a
growth-rising and inflation-cooling quadrant, and a neutral risk regime, with a winner scoreboard
placing strategic metals first and the uranium fuel cycle last. It also carries an explicit warning
that the macro layer is degraded - the phase is a seeded prior, not yet earned by the state machine.
That warning is honoured here: unnamed families are treated as neutral, no headwind is read into the
gap, and because a data gap must never tighten the book, nothing was cut on the strength of it.

What it changed. Three things, all of them the sanctioned channels. First, posture: the stance for
this run is cautious, which reflects the phase read alongside the fact that six of the eight seats
trade at or above the reconciled value of their parts. Second, the entry bar: in this quadrant a
fresh entry wants a discount of at least 20% to reconciled value, and no seat in this book clears
that - which is why five of the eight are marked to wait for weakness, two to scale in, and Cameco
to wait for a specific piece of evidence rather than a price. Third, the horizon: eighteen months
rather than twelve, because a phase in which real rates are being imposed lengthens the time a
cost-curve advantage needs to show up in cash rather than in the multiple. Fourth, the per-seat
phase commentary, which is judgement and nothing more - Rio Tinto's Simandou and lithium builds and
Cameco's raised unit costs are both named there as sitting against the phase, and both seats were
taken anyway.

What it did not change. Membership: no name is in or out of this book because of the phase, the
quadrant, or its rank on the scoreboard - the seats were chosen on the four pillars and the gates,
and the shape was forced by the chain caps and the eligible universe. Conviction: no score moved on a
macro citation, and no decision rationale above cites one. Sizing: not one unit was set or moved by
the macro read - the only things that moved sizes were name-specific dated facts (Hudbay's margin
correction, Agnico's falling realised price, Vale's raised cost guidance, Southern Copper's
by-product credit) and the two correlation caps. The clearest test of this is precious metals: the
macro block calls it a headwind family and ranks it second on the scoreboard, and neither fact
touched a single unit - the gold cluster was cut on measured correlation and on Agnico's own price
against its own value, not on the phase. Copper is the mirror image: the block calls it a tailwind
and the top-confidence family, and Southern Copper was still cut to the smallest seat in the book.

Sizing note for the audit trail. This book publishes equal-weighted, so the eight seats each carry
12.5% of published weight regardless of the units above. The chain exposure reported alongside this
memo is therefore the published one: precious metals and copper each carry three of eight seats and
so 37.5%, diversified miners two seats and 25%, the uranium fuel cycle one seat and 12.5%. Both
three-seat chains sit above the 30% weight bound by arithmetic rather than by drift - under equal
weighting three of eight seats is always 37.5% - and both are inside the three-name limit that
actually binds. Measured on the size units instead, precious metals is 42.1%, copper 35.8%,
diversified miners 25.3% and the uranium fuel cycle 10.5%. Those two breaches are stated rather than
engineered away: bringing them under 30% would require sizing Vale and Cameco at roughly double every
other seat, which would misrepresent conviction in the very record that is meant to decide whether
equal weighting is ever switched off.
