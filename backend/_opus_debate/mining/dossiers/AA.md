# Forensic Dossier — Alcoa Corporation (AA)
**Prepared 2026-08-04 · Price used: $46.88 (NYSE, US dollars, quote 2026-08-04) · Market value about $12.4B**

Alcoa mines bauxite, refines it into alumina, and smelts alumina into primary aluminum. It is
a converter with an orebody attached, and that distinction runs through everything below.
Source material: five consecutive earnings calls (first quarter 2025 through first quarter
2026) supplied in the bundle, plus the second quarter 2026 call and release of 2026-07-16,
the 2026-06-30 acquisition announcement, and the screener's financial block.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

**Five quarters ago the story was survival and repair. Today it is scale and acquisition.
That is a genuine change in kind, not degree, and it happened in about eight weeks.**

Through 2025 every call opened on the same three subjects: safety, operational stability, and
debt reduction. In the first quarter of 2025 the chief financial officer laid out a formal
target — adjusted debt of $2.1B to $2.5B, cash of $1.0B to $1.5B, and therefore adjusted net
debt (borrowings plus pension obligations, less cash) of $1.0B to $1.5B — and framed the whole
capital plan around reaching it: "we want to maintain investment-grade leverage metrics
throughout all business cycles." Adjusted net debt was $2.1B at that moment. Every subsequent
call reported progress against that single number: $1.7B by the second quarter of 2025, $1.5B
at year-end 2025, $1.8B in the first quarter of 2026 on a seasonal working-capital build, and
finally $1.446B at 2026-06-30. Target reached.

Alongside it ran a second, quieter story: fixing plants. The Alumar smelter in Brazil crawled
from 91% of capacity (first quarter 2025) to 92% (second quarter 2025) with the chief executive
volunteering "I've told you that before... take that with a grain of salt." The San Ciprián
smelter in Spain was knocked over by a national blackout, restarted, reached 65% by year-end
2025, and completed on 2026-04-07. Kwinana was closed permanently. Lista and Portland were
restarted. By the second quarter of 2026 the restart programme was essentially finished and
management could say the sentence it had been unable to say for four years: everything runs.

Then on 2026-06-30 Alcoa announced the largest transaction in its ten-year history as an
independent company — South32's bauxite, alumina and aluminum interests (the Boddington mine
and Worsley refinery in Western Australia, the Hillside smelter and idled Bayside site in South
Africa, and stakes in the Mineração Rio do Norte bauxite mine and the Alumar refinery and
smelter joint ventures in Brazil; the Mozal smelter in Mozambique is excluded) for $3.1B in
cash plus about 17.0 million new Alcoa shares worth roughly $1.0B, and a contingent value right
worth up to a further $750M. Implied enterprise value about $4.7B including $0.6B of assumed
net debt. The deal adds roughly 5.2 million tonnes of annual alumina capacity — a 53% increase —
and about 900 thousand tonnes of primary aluminum, a 37% increase.

**Growth drivers introduced:** critical minerals (a gallium plant at Wagerup reaching final
investment decision on 2026-07-14, largely funded by the Australian, Japanese and United States
governments, with Alcoa contributing only $24M); land monetisation (former smelter sites sold to
data-centre developers, targeted at $500M to $1B of proceeds between now and 2030); value-added
casting capacity (a $65M Mosjøen expansion announced May 2026).

**Growth drivers quietly dropped:** the ELYSIS inert-anode technology, once the centrepiece of
the low-carbon story, was pushed out explicitly — "we're not looking to do anything with ELYSIS
this decade" (fourth quarter 2025), with groundbreaking, not completion, post-2030. Greenfield
expansion of any kind was formally ruled out in the same call: "we've not found anywhere around
the world that provides a sufficiently low energy price for sufficient returns on a greenfield
plant." That statement is the intellectual foundation of the acquisition seven months later —
if you cannot build, you buy.

**Where the story is poorer than the trajectory justifies:** management does not oversell. The
second-quarter earnings shortfall was explained mechanically — a 15-day pricing lag that could
not capture a sharp late-June decline — rather than dressed up. The $900M synergy figure was
pre-emptively defended as "not high level consultant projections," and the near-term component
was sized at a deliberately small $50M of run-rate savings in year one. That restraint is a
credibility asset.

**Where it is richer:** the phrase "improve our position on the global alumina and aluminum cost
curves" is asserted for the acquisition with no published cost-curve number attached. Alcoa
publishes no all-in sustaining cost per tonne. The only cost-curve evidence an outsider can
verify is segment margin — and on that evidence the refining half of the company is currently
above the marginal cost of production, not below it.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rising — and specifically, rising from defensive to offensive across the 2025/2026 boundary.**

- *2025 Q1 — defensive.* Dominated by tariffs, restricted cash at San Ciprián ("about $75 million more there"), and a refusal to model: "we're not completely following the logic... take that logic offline with Louis."
- *2025 Q2 — defensive, with a foothold.* The Ma'aden joint-venture stake sold for $1.35B; a five-year Australian tax dispute won. But the aluminum shipment guide was cut and San Ciprián had been knocked over by a blackout.
- *2025 Q3 — sombre.* A workplace fatality at Alumar, the first since 2020, opened the call. Kwinana closed permanently at a $895M charge. Adjusted result was a $6M loss. Yet this is also the quarter with the Massena long-term power contract and a $60M furnace commitment — the first forward-leaning capital decision in the set.
- *2026 Q1 — offensive.* "Let me start with the headline. We had a strong start to 2026 driven by execution." Return on equity 21.9%. The chief executive volunteers that he is "excited about getting into our target net debt level."
- *2026 Q2 — offensive and expansionary.* Record revenue, record segment margin, and the largest acquisition in company history, defended in detail and unprompted.

**Specificity increased throughout.** By 2026 management was quantifying individual line items to
$5M precision (diesel and fuel oil "$5 million favorable in the third quarter... based on $90
per barrel fuel oil"), naming the caustic soda lag at six months and the carbon lag at one
quarter, and publishing a locked-box estimate ("more than $200 million as of 06/30/2026") and a
ticking-fee range ($80M to $100M) that no one had asked for. That is the opposite of evasion.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Score: Medium-High.**

**Delivered:** every balance-sheet commitment. The $1.0B-$1.5B adjusted net debt target set in
the first quarter of 2025 was hit in the second quarter of 2026, on schedule, with each
intermediate quarter's number disclosed. Both callable note tranches were redeemed as promised.
San Ciprián's restart, once resumed, was completed "on time and on budget" on 2026-04-07 exactly
as guided at year-end 2025.

**Missed:** volumes, repeatedly, and always at a refinery or a restart. Alumar took four
quarters to move from 91% to 92%. 2025 aluminum shipments were cut. 2026 alumina production and
shipments were cut in the second quarter of 2026 after Pinjarra suffered an oxalate outbreak
compounded by a cyclone-driven gas curtailment, and management stated plainly the lost volume
will not be recovered. Depreciation guidance was revised twice, in opposite directions.

**Sandbagging:** none detectable — if anything the reverse. The 2025 fourth-quarter carbon-dioxide
compensation benefit in Spain and Norway was *larger* than management had embedded ("we honestly
thought it would be a bit less"), and they said so. A sandbagger takes credit for that.

**Reconciliation with the beat rate.** The screener records five beats in seven quarters (71%),
which fits: the price-driven line beats, the volume-driven line misses. The second quarter of
2026 adjusted earnings per share of $2.12 against $2.32 expected is the cleanest illustration —
operations delivered, the metal price moved under them in the last fortnight.

**The one credibility item now moving the wrong way.** For four consecutive calls the Western
Australia mining approvals timeline was reaffirmed verbatim: ministerial approval by year-end
2026. On 2026-07-16 that changed. Having spent five weeks in Australia and met the stakeholders
directly, the chief executive said his confidence in the outcome was unchanged but "the timing
could extend beyond our original expectations," and disclosed contingency plans — six months of
delay absorbed with no impact on supply, quality or cost, and beyond that a modification of
mining and refinery flow rates to avoid an ore gap. Volunteering the slip early, with the
mitigation attached, is the credible way to do it. It is still the first crack in a
previously firm commitment, and it sits on the single asset the whole refining business depends
on.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**Capital returns — asked every quarter for six quarters, never answered.** Glyn Lawcock
(Barrenjoey) opened the subject in the second quarter of 2025: "your thoughts on timing for when
we may hear some words on capital management." Timna Tanners (Wells Fargo) returned to it in the
first quarter of 2026 with "potentially a bigger amount of free cash flow — any updated thoughts."
The answer never changed: reach the leverage target first, then "we'll look across our capital
allocation priorities." Analysts were being told the reward for five years of deleveraging was
coming. The reward, when it arrived in June 2026, was a $3.1B acquisition. The dividend has sat
at $0.10 a quarter — about $105M a year — throughout, most recently declared 2026-07-30. There
is no buyback, and none was executed while the stock fell from $84 to $47.

**Alumina cost recovery — pushed, and it kept getting worse.** Carlos De Alba (Morgan Stanley)
pressed in the second quarter of 2026 on exactly which portion of the alumina shortfall was
volume and which was un-normalised Pinjarra cost. The answer was precise and reassuring (the
full $30M Pinjarra recovery is inside the third-quarter guide). But the segment has now
deteriorated for two consecutive quarters, from $40M of losses to $96M.

**Power in South Africa — deflected, legitimately.** John Tumazos asked what happens when
Hillside's power contract expires, noting that other South African users pay roughly six times
the smelter's rate. The reply — "5 years out on a transaction that we haven't closed yet is
difficult to speculate" — is fair on timing and evasive on substance. Hillside is the single
largest smelter Alcoa is acquiring, and its economics rest on a legacy tariff from a utility
with a well-documented history of raising industrial prices.

**What analysts worry about that management is not addressing:** whether the aluminum price
underpinning record margins is a war premium. Timna Tanners asked directly. The answer —
"sentiment," fundamentals unchanged, 3 to 3.5 million tonnes still offline — may well be right.
It is also exactly what a management team committing $3.1B of cash at that price would say.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

🔴 **The deleverage-then-lever pattern, executed inside eight weeks.** Adjusted net debt reached
$1.446B on 2026-06-30, the top of the target band. The acquisition was announced the same day.
Cash consideration of $3.1B is roughly twice the entire net debt position management spent five
years eliminating, and it is fixed while the revenue supporting it is not. Management's guardrail
— leverage "not to exceed 2.0x" — is explicitly qualified as "based on recent pricing," and
recent pricing includes a benchmark that averaged about $3,550 per tonne in the second quarter
against roughly $2,500-2,600 through all of 2025.

🔴 **Buying at the top of the metal cycle.** The aluminum benchmark reached roughly $4,046 per
tonne intraday in late May 2026 and the shares peaked at $84.38. The deal was struck five weeks
later. Buying producing assets when your own product is at a decade high is the sector's
signature error.

🔴 **Half the company is loss-making.** Alumina segment adjusted operating profit before
depreciation: −$40M (2026 Q1), −$96M (2026 Q2). The acquisition increases alumina capacity by 53%.

🔴 **The Western Australia approvals timeline slipped** (2026 Q2), on the orebody that feeds
Pinjarra and Wagerup.

🔴 **A live regulatory threat to the land-monetisation story.** New York's governor signed a
data-centre moratorium in July 2026, days before the call, while the Massena East sale was in
final papering. Management had "no complete assessment."

🔴 **Insiders are not buying.** Open-market activity over the measured window: zero purchases,
one sale of 4,600 shares. Across a 44% drawdown from the May high, not one officer or director
bought a share in the open market.

🟢 **The delevering itself was real and completed on the stated schedule** — no covenant drama,
no equity raised, notes redeemed at par.

🟢 **Deal structure genuinely shares the price risk.** $1.0B of the consideration is Alcoa stock
(so South32 keeps commodity exposure), the contingent value right pays South32 *more* only if
alumina or aluminum exceed strike prices — costing Alcoa only in states of the world where it is
earning more — and a locked box accruing from 2026-04-01 (already above $200M by 2026-06-30)
returns interim cash flow to Alcoa. Both Moody's and S&P affirmed ratings on the pro-forma basis.

🟢 **Labour is locked down to 2030** — multi-year agreements concluded in the second quarter of
2026 with the Australian Workers' Union in Western Australia, the United Steelworkers at both
United States smelters, ABI in Quebec, plus Norway and Alumar in Brazil.

🟢 **The restart programme is finished.** San Ciprián complete, Alumar at ~95%, Lista at nameplate,
Portland at its best level since separation. Four smelters and one refinery set year-to-date
production records in the second quarter of 2026. Restart capital is behind the company.

🟢 **The gallium plant is other people's money** — three governments fund it; Alcoa's total
contribution is $24M, already paid, with an offtake share.

🟢 **Energy and gas are contracted forward:** Woodside domestic gas 2027-2030 (June 2026),
Statkraft power for Lista (June 2026), a long-term Massena contract (2025 Q3).

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**The vocabulary flipped from "target" to "opportunity."** For five quarters management used
the language of a balance-sheet patient. In the first quarter of 2026 the chief financial officer
said the quiet part: "we expect to have growth options that will compete with shareholder
returns in the rest of the year." That sentence, delivered in April, is the acquisition being
telegraphed three months ahead — and it tells you shareholder returns lost the competition
before analysts knew there was one.

**"Below replacement cost" is doing heavy lifting.** The acquisition is justified on the ground
that new smelting and refining capacity is prohibitively expensive — a point management had
already made concretely in the first quarter of 2025 (a 4 million tonne United States build
would need 6 gigawatts and roughly $30B, over at least five years). The logic is sound. It is
also the logic that justifies paying almost any price for existing assets, and it is unfalsifiable
in the moment.

**Depreciation was raised on "changes in asset lives at certain bauxite mining operations."**
Pressed by Chris LaFemina (Jefferies) on whether mine lives had shortened, the chief financial
officer's answer was audibly incomplete: "it's lives of certain assets... and there was one more
that's now escaping me. But it's not the mine life itself that's shorter." That is the single
least convincing answer in the entire five-call set, and it concerns reserve life.

**Demand language softened in a specific place.** Building and construction weakness was flagged
in both Europe and North America (2026 Q2), and in Europe "a shortening up of the order books as
far as being able to see how far out customers are looking." Order-book visibility contracting is
an early demand signal, and it was disclosed inside an otherwise triumphant quarter.

**Nobody is buying the metal thesis at face value except management.** Zacks placed the shares on
its strong-sell list on 2026-07-09, three weeks before the record quarter. The two most recent
analyst targets — Morgan Stanley at $53 (2026-07-08) and Argus at $55 (2026-07-22) — sit far
below the $65.50 consensus and the $75 high, which were set on older, higher tape.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.** Four consecutive quarters of rising adjusted operating profit
before depreciation ($270M → $546M → $595M → $901M), a repaired balance sheet, a completed restart
programme, contracted power and labour, and record segment margins. On operations alone this is
the best Alcoa has looked since separation.

**The single thing to weigh before sizing.** Not the metal, and not the acquisition's strategic
logic — both are defensible. It is the *sequencing*. Alcoa has converted a $1.4B net-debt
company into a roughly $4.5B net-debt company by contract, with the cash payment due at a closing
in the first half of 2027, at a moment when about a third of its earning power derives from a
Gulf supply outage and a 50% United States import tariff, neither of which Alcoa controls and
both of which management concedes could reverse on sentiment. The obligation is fixed and dated;
the cash flow that services it is neither. Every dollar of position size should be underwritten
against a metal price at $2,500 per tonne, not at today's $3,200.

**Economic moat: NARROW. Moat trend: STABLE.**
The moat type is scale-cost, and it is real but not wide. Its genuine components are a
multi-decade Western Australian bauxite position that no one can replicate, captive and
long-contracted hydro and gas power in Norway, Quebec, Iceland and Brazil, and a flexible
cast-house network that converts commodity ingot into premium value-added product — the last of
which management demonstrated in the second quarter of 2026 by converting roughly 30 thousand
tonnes of prime metal into higher-margin product and lifting value-added volumes accordingly.
Against that, Alcoa is a price-taker in two commodities, publishes no all-in cost per tonne, and
its refining half is currently losing money at prevailing benchmark prices — which is what
being at, rather than below, the marginal cost of production looks like.

The returns evidence supports stable-to-widening rather than eroding: return on equity of 26.4%
for the first half of 2026 against 16.4% for full-year 2025, gross margin from 10.0% to 25.2%
over four quarters, and returns on invested capital rising off a low base. But most of that is
price. The honest read is a narrow moat holding its width, with the widening claim
(a better cost-curve position after the acquisition) asserted rather than evidenced.

**Secular threat: MANAGEABLE — and directionally favourable.** Aluminum is on the winning side of
the energy transition, not the losing one: grid build-out, electrification, lightweighting and
the substitution away from expensive copper all pull demand, and management sees roughly 7 million
tonnes of non-China primary demand growth and 18 million tonnes of alumina demand growth over the
next decade. The plausible structural risks are Chinese supply discipline breaking (China is
already running slightly above its own 45 million tonne cap), Indonesian and Guinean refining
capacity being built out, and the eventual removal of the Section 232 tariff that is currently
inflating United States regional premiums. Those are real, but they compress margin — they do not
shrink the terminal base. Nothing here threatens the existence of the earnings stream.

**Therefore the cheapness is a fear-print rather than a value trap** — but a fear-print on
earnings that are visibly at a cycle high. The shares at roughly $46.88 sit about 44% below the
May 2026 high of $84.38 and near 29% of the 52-week range, at roughly 5.7 times trailing
company-adjusted operating profit before depreciation and under 4 times the current guided
run-rate. The discount is genuine; the denominator is the question.
