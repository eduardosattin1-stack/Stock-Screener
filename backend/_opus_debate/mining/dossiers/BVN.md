# Forensic Dossier — Compañía de Minas Buenaventura S.A.A. (BVN)

*Prepared 2026-08-04. Source material: five consecutive earnings calls (2025-07-25 through
2026-07-31), the screener's financial block, company press releases dated 2026-07-16 and
2026-07-30, and independent verification described in section 2. Price used throughout:
$32.00 per American Depositary Share on the New York Stock Exchange, the live quote on
2026-08-04. Buenaventura also trades in Lima as BUE.LM; every number here is in the
New York listing and in US dollars, which is also the company's reporting currency.*

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

Across these five calls the story moves through three distinct phases, and the shift is
easy to miss because the headline numbers improved every single quarter.

**Phase one (mid-2025): "we are finishing San Gabriel."** In July 2025 the entire
management pitch is a construction pitch. San Gabriel — a new underground gold mine in
southern Peru — is "88% overall completion," the first gold bar is promised for the
fourth quarter of 2025, and the total build cost is held firmly at "$720 million to $750
million." Chief executive Leandro García frames the project as a national event: "for the
countries the only project that is going to be beginning production will be inaugurated
in this year." The existing mines are described as steady; the growth story is entirely
the new mine.

**Phase two (late 2025 to early 2026): "we are a cash-generation story."** By October 2025
the emphasis quietly changes. San Gabriel is still front and centre at "96% overall
progress," but the new talking points are the balance sheet and shareholder returns —
the 2026 bond redeemed at par, leverage down to 0.41 times, a dividend resumed. By
February 2026 that becomes explicit: the board lifts the payout from "not less than 20%
of the net income of the year" to "40% of the net income of the previous year." The
company is now selling itself as a cash machine rather than a builder.

**Phase three (2026): "the ramp-up is harder than we said."** From April 2026 onward the
narrative contracts. San Gabriel has poured metal but cannot hold throughput. The language
turns granular and defensive — moisture in the ore, clay in the crusher, filtration
performance, a tailings valley that is "V shaped" so "the bottom part of the valley is very
narrow." Alongside it a second theme appears and then stalls: a strategic review of three
older mines (Orcopampa, Tambomayo and Julcani) that has been "close to a final decision"
since February 2026 and was still undecided on the July 2026 call.

Two growth drivers were introduced and then softened. Trapiche, a copper development
project, received its environmental permit in early 2026 and management immediately
walked expectations back — projects vice-president Renzo Macher said in July 2026 they
will spend "the next year, year and a half in understanding if it is a business." And the
asset sales, first floated at the 2025 investor day, have been re-opened rather than
executed: commercial vice-president Aldo Massa in February 2026 said "this increase in
precious metals prices really makes you think a lot if you want to sell or not an asset
right now."

## 2. CLAIMS vs FINANCIALS (forensic)

The financial trajectory is genuinely excellent. The question is how much of it management
produced and how much the metal price produced.

**The single most important forensic point.** Buenaventura's first-half 2026 results were
earned at an average realised silver price of **$94.53 per ounce** and an average realised
gold price of **$4,599 per ounce** (company production release, 2026-07-16; second-quarter
realisations were $89.52 and $4,342). As of today silver trades at **$60.18** and gold at
**$4,157.60**. On a like-for-like basket, roughly half of Buenaventura's gross metal value
comes from silver, so today's prices sit about **19% below** the basket that produced the
first half. Annualising the first half and repricing at today's metals puts direct-operations
profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation at roughly **$0.95–1.05 billion**,
versus the trailing-twelve-month figure of $1.22 billion on the company's own definition and
$1.52 billion on the screener's broader definition. The trailing numbers are a peak-price
photograph, not a run rate. Management does not hedge — the chief financial officer confirmed
on 2026-04-30: "we prefer not to hedge for the time being" — so there is no contractual
cushion under this.

**Where the deterministic proxy disagrees with the company.** The screener's cost-curve
proxy places Buenaventura in the highest-cost quartile of its eight-name peer group (12th
percentile) despite a 68.8% trailing profit margin. That reading does not survive a peer
check. Buenaventura's first-half 2026 direct-operations margin was 57.5% ($663.4 million on
$1,153.6 million), against 55.7% for Pan American Silver and 53.7% for Hecla Mining on the
same trailing basis. Buenaventura sits at or slightly above the median of its own named
competitors, not in the high-cost tail. The proxy is wrong on this name, and the reader
should discount it.

A second proxy disagreement matters for risk. The screener's price-sensitivity figure says
a 10% move in metal prices swings profit by 9.5%. With a 55–58% margin and roughly two-thirds
of revenue commodity-linked, the true swing is closer to 12–18%. **The proxy understates
Buenaventura's operating leverage by roughly a third to a half — in both directions.**

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Declining, and measurably so.**

- *2025-07-25 — high confidence, low specificity.* On the last construction permit: "We
  don't foresee a greater risk in that permit." On ramp-up speed, when analyst Tanya
  Jakusconek asked why a small mine would take a year: "No, during 2026, we are not going
  to take 1 year."
- *2025-10-31 — still confident.* "We are confident that we will be granted of all, and we
  will end this year with the production of 2 bars." Delivered — the first bar came in
  December 2025.
- *2026-02-27 — first defensive turn.* Asked why capital spending had jumped since the
  investor day, the projects vice-president answered with weather: "we are currently in the
  middle of the rainy season. So you keep finding things that can be improved."
- *2026-04-30 — granular and operational.* "we have some challenges with the high moisture
  of the ore getting stuck on the conveyor belt."
- *2026-07-31 — explicitly hedged.* "As production and sales volumes remain below expected
  steady state levels, current unit costs are not yet representative of the operation's long
  term cost profile." Steady state has moved to "the middle of 2027."

The vocabulary migrated from offence (completion percentages, national milestones, dividend
increases) to defence (moisture control, filtration performance, metallurgical optimisation,
El Niño preparation). That is the classic signature of a ramp-up that is not going to plan.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Split verdict — high on the base business, low on the growth project.**

*The base business is reliable.* Silver, copper, zinc and lead output has tracked guidance
quarter after quarter, permitting milestones landed roughly when promised (the Coimolache
operating permit in September 2025, the San Gabriel water licence in April 2026, the Yumpag
throughput increase to 1,200 tonnes per day in July 2026), and the balance-sheet commitments
were kept — the 2026 bond was redeemed at par exactly as promised.

*The growth project is not.* The San Gabriel gold guidance path reads 70,000–90,000 ounces
(October 2025) → roughly 48,000–55,000 (February 2026) → 25,000–30,000 (July 2026). That is
a cut of roughly two-thirds within nine months, against a target set after the plant was 96%
built. Steady state has slipped from "second half of 2026" (July 2025) to "middle of 2027."
Capital spending for 2026 has slipped from $385–415 million to about $500 million in five
months.

There is no sandbagging here. Management guides to the optimistic end and then revises. On
the base business that habit costs little; on the new mine it has cost credibility.

**Reliability score: Medium.** High for the producing assets, Low for anything
forward-looking on San Gabriel.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

Two analysts — Carlos De Alba of Morgan Stanley and Tanya Jakusconek (Scotiabank) — carry
almost every call, and both circled the same three unanswered questions.

1. **Ramp-up speed.** Jakusconek asked in July 2025, with visible scepticism, why a small
   mine would need a year to ramp: "Can someone explain to me why it would take so long for
   such a small mine to ramp up?" She was told it would not. It has taken longer than a year
   and is still not done. Her instinct was right and management's answer was wrong.
2. **Capital spending discipline.** De Alba in February 2026 pressed directly on the gap
   between the investor-day number and the new guide, and asked whether the extra work had
   only been "discovered… as the ramp-up started." The answer was rain and earthworks. He
   accepted it with a flat "But anything major? Okay." The question has not been properly
   answered since.
3. **The asset sales.** Jakusconek asked in February 2026 where the disposals of Orcopampa,
   Tambomayo and Julcani stood; De Alba was still asking in July 2026. The answer has been
   the same for six months — a decision is close. A process that keeps not concluding is
   itself information.

What analysts are not pressing on, and should be: Buenaventura does not publish a
consolidated all-in sustaining cost per ounce the way its peers do. It reports "all-in
sustaining cost expressed in terms of copper" plus a per-unit cost-applicable-to-sales table.
That makes cross-company cost comparison genuinely hard and is the weakest part of the
disclosure.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

**Red flags**

- 🔴 **The three-step guidance cut at San Gabriel** (October 2025 → February 2026 → July
  2026), for a plant that was already 96% built when the first target was set. Root causes —
  tailings filtration and lower-than-expected metallurgical recovery — are process problems,
  which are slower and more expensive to fix than schedule problems.
- 🔴 **Capital spending overrun disclosed piecemeal**: $385–415 million became roughly $500
  million, plus $12 million for El Niño preparation and $20–25 million more for San Gabriel.
  Each increment is small; the pattern is what matters.
- 🔴 **The payout ratio was doubled at the price peak.** The board lifted the dividend from
  20% to 40% of prior-year net income in February 2026, with gold near $4,500 and silver near
  $100, and the chief executive explicitly tied it to "as the prices are going well." Raising
  distributions at the top of a commodity cycle is the sector's most reliable mistake. In
  mitigation, it is a percentage policy rather than a fixed dollar commitment, and the company
  has not bought back stock at the highs.
- 🔴 **No hedging, by stated policy**, into a silver price that has already fallen from a
  $94.53 first-half realisation to $60.18 today.
- 🔴 **No consolidated all-in sustaining cost disclosure**, unlike every named peer.
- 🔴 **Zero open-market insider buying or selling** across roughly the last 100 filings,
  including the period when the shares traded at $17.34. Not a sale signal, but no
  management conviction either.
- 🔴 **Single-country concentration in Peru**, with a presidential run-off unresolved as of
  the April 2026 call and management conceding only that "there should be some voices" asking
  for higher taxes and royalties.

**Green flags**

- 🟢 **The balance sheet is genuinely clean and independently verified**: $758.9 million of
  cash against $692 million of total debt at 30 June 2026, a net cash position for the first
  time in years, and the 2026 bond redeemed at par in July 2025.
- 🟢 **The Cerro Verde dividend stream is large and real.** Buenaventura holds 19.58% of the
  Cerro Verde copper mine and has received $274.1 million of dividends from it so far in
  2026, including $117.5 million on 24 July 2026 — after the quarter closed, so not yet in
  the reported cash balance.
- 🟢 **Permitting execution has been good**, in a country where it usually is not: Coimolache
  September 2025, San Gabriel operating permit and water licence, Yumpag to 1,200 tonnes per
  day, El Brocal to 17,000 tonnes per day, and the Trapiche environmental approval.
- 🟢 **A credible, dated cost-reduction path at the silver flagship.** Yumpag's operating cost
  should fall 15–17% from first-half levels by year-end 2026 through higher throughput and a
  switch from diesel generators to the national grid. Separately, the price-based escalator
  in the silver concentrate contracts resets from a $35 per ounce base to a $50 base from
  September, which mechanically reduces the deductions that inflated first-half unit costs.
- 🟢 **Management does not overstate the ramp.** The July 2026 admission that current San
  Gabriel unit costs "are not yet representative" is candid, not promotional.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- **"Evaluating our operating portfolio" became a real process, then stalled.** In July 2025
  it was a throwaway line. By February 2026 three named mines were formally under review. By
  July 2026 the decision was still pending, with the reason given as the higher metal price.
  That reasoning is backwards for a seller — high prices are when you sell late-life assets,
  not when you reconsider.
- **The commercial vocabulary changed.** From late 2025 the calls fill with "commercial
  deductions," "non-payable value" and "escalators." Commercial vice-president Aldo Massa
  explained it plainly in February 2026: silver concentrate below 2,500 grams per tonne is
  paid at 60–70% of contained metal, above that at 90–95%. Buenaventura has been producing
  more low-grade concentrate. This is a quiet grade-quality story hiding inside a cost line.
- **A sequential revenue turn is already visible.** Revenue fell from $622.5 million in the
  first quarter of 2026 to $529.0 million in the second, a 15% sequential decline, while
  volumes were broadly stable. That is the metal price arriving in the accounts.
- **Management turnover is modest but real.** The sustainability vice-president of 25 years
  departed after the April 2026 call, and the chairman's name changes across the transcripts.
  Neither is alarming on its own.
- **A large strategic shareholder sits on the register.** Antofagasta plc acquired an
  approximately 19% equity interest in Buenaventura in December 2023. It has never been
  discussed on any of these five calls — a notable silence.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STABLE.** Not strengthening, despite the record headline profits, because
essentially all of the improvement came from the metal price rather than from the company;
and not deteriorating, because the base mines are running to plan, the balance sheet moved
to net cash, and permitting has gone well.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing.** Buenaventura's reported earnings
power is a photograph taken at the top of the silver market. The first half was earned at
$94.53 per ounce of silver; silver is $60.18 today. Repricing the business at today's metals
takes direct-operations profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation from
about $1.33 billion annualised to roughly $1.0 billion — which happens to land at the very
top of the company's own full-year guide, not above it. Any position must be sized against
the repriced number, not the trailing one, and against an explicitly unhedged book.

**Economic moat: NARROW.** In mining the only durable advantage is the orebody and the
position on the cost curve. Buenaventura has two real ones — the Yumpag silver deposit,
which produced 2.32 million ounces in a single quarter at attractive grades, and the 19.58%
holding in Cerro Verde, a top-tier long-life copper mine operated by Freeport-McMoRan.
Against that, the legacy gold mines (Orcopampa, Tambomayo, Julcani) are late-life and up for
sale, and San Gabriel's economics are unproven. Return on invested capital of 13.3% and
return on equity of 27.6% are healthy but are themselves price-inflated.

**Moat trend: STABLE.** Yumpag's cost is falling on throughput and grid power; San Gabriel
adds reserve life (1.8 million ounces of proven and probable gold reserves, a potential
16-year life) even if it adds it late and expensively; the legacy mines that are depleting
are the ones being sold. The pieces roughly offset.

**Secular threat: NONE that impairs terminal value.** Silver has an industrial demand base
that is growing, gold is a monetary asset, and copper is the single best-supported
commodity in the energy transition. There is no structural force eroding what Buenaventura
sells. The risk here is cyclical price risk and Peruvian political risk, both of which are
severe but neither of which is terminal. The cheapness is therefore not a melting-ice-cube
problem — but it is substantially a peak-earnings problem, which is a different trap with a
similar shape.
