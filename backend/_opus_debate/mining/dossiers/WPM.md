# Forensic Dossier — Wheaton Precious Metals Corp. (WPM)

**Prepared:** 2026-08-04 · **Listing used:** New York Stock Exchange, US dollars · **Price used:** $115.99
**Transcripts reviewed:** Q1 2025 (2025-05-09), Q2 2025 (2025-08-08), Q3 2025 (2025-11-07), Q4/FY 2025 (2026-03-13), Q1 2026 (2026-05-08)

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

Wheaton is a streaming company: it hands a mine operator cash up front, and in exchange receives the right to buy a fixed slice of that mine's gold or silver forever at a pre-agreed low price. It digs nothing and operates nothing. Across these five calls the story moved through three distinct chapters.

**Chapter one (Q1–Q3 2025) — "the catalyst-rich year."** The emphasis was on converting a construction pipeline into production. Randy Smallwood, then chief executive, framed 2025 as "a catalyst-rich year with four development projects scheduled to come online." The proof points arrived on schedule: Blackwater poured first gold in January and reached commercial production on 2 May 2025; Goose poured first gold in Q2 and reached commercial production on 6 October 2025. The growth promise in this chapter was "approximately 40% production growth by 2029, reaching 870,000 gold-equivalent ounces."

**Chapter two (Q4 2025, reported 2026-03-13) — the handover and the big cheque.** Two things changed at once. Smallwood announced he would step down as chief executive on 31 March 2026 after fifteen years, moving to Chair, with Haytham Hodaly — previously the corporate-development chief who sourced the deals — taking over. And the growth target was raised sharply: from 40% growth to 870,000 ounces by 2029, to "50% to 1.2 million gold equivalent ounces by 2030." The step-up was funded by the largest streaming transaction ever completed: an additional silver stream on the Antamina mine in Peru, bought from BHP for $4.3 billion.

**Chapter three (Q1 2026) — execution under a falling metal.** Hodaly's first call as chief executive reported records across the board and reiterated the 1.2-million-ounce target. What the narrative does *not* address is that the metal underpinning the $4.3 billion purchase had, by the time he spoke, already fallen by roughly a third from the level at which the deal was struck.

**What was quietly dropped.** The 870,000-ounce-by-2029 figure disappeared, replaced by the larger 1.2-million-by-2030 number — an upgrade, but one substantially *bought* rather than grown. Roughly 18% of 2030 production is now expected to come from the single asset they paid $4.3 billion for. Also dropped: in November 2025 Hodaly said of the company's equity holdings in partners, "we're not looking to divest any of those positions." By 31 March 2026 the long-term investment line had fallen from $457 million to $54 million.

**The single most important number the bundle gets wrong.** The input metrics state "NET CASH 2.16B ... a genuine sum-of-the-parts add-back." That was true on 31 March 2026 and is stale. The Antamina purchase closed on 1 April 2026 — the first day of the following quarter — and the company disclosed the funding mix: roughly $1.9 billion of cash on hand, a roughly $0.9 billion draw on the previously undrawn $2.0 billion revolving facility, and a **new $1.5 billion non-revolving term loan with a two-year maturity**, priced at the secured overnight financing rate plus 110–150 basis points. The chief financial officer confirmed the scale on the 2026-05-08 call: "Q2 is heavy — we will disburse about $4.6 billion including the Antamina acquisition."

Wheaton therefore ended June 2026 with roughly $2.4 billion of drawn debt against a much-reduced cash balance — an estimated **net debt position of about $1.7 billion**, not net cash of $2.16 billion. That is a swing of roughly $3.9 billion, or about $8.50 per share, and any valuation that adds back the stale net-cash figure overstates fair value by that amount.

**No dilution — the sector's classic tell is absent.** Diluted shares outstanding went 454.4M (Q1 2025) → 454.7M → 454.8M → 454.8M → 455.0M (Q1 2026). Growth of 0.1% over five quarters. Wheaton funded the largest deal in streaming history without issuing a share. In a sector where serial equity issuance is the norm, this is a genuine and important mark in its favour.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rating: Rising, then subtly guarded.**

- *Q1 2025:* Confident and operationally specific — "several of our core assets exceeding production expectations."
- *Q2 2025:* Confidence rising with the metal. "Our forecasted organic growth profile of 40% production growth by 2029 enables us to pursue opportunities ... without compromising on quality."
- *Q3 2025:* The most assertive of the five. Smallwood took a direct swipe at the competing business model — the streaming model has a "unique ability to generate predictable levered cash flows ... an advantage not offered by the traditional royalty model, which requires full upfront payments and lacks embedded leverage." Competitive positioning language of this kind usually appears when a company feels it is winning.
- *Q4 2025:* Peak confidence. A record year, an 18% dividend increase, the largest deal in the industry's history, and a chief-executive handover framed as "a position of incredible strength."
- *Q1 2026:* Still confident but noticeably more defensive in construction. Hodaly leads with the fact that growth does not depend on more deals: "Importantly, Wheaton's growth is not dependent on additional transactions." That is a reassurance offered before it was asked for — a tell that management knows the balance sheet has less room than it did.

Guidance became *more* specific over time, not less, which is a positive sign. The 2026 range (860,000–940,000 ounces) came with a half-by-half split (45% first half, 55% second) and asset-level drivers.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Score: High.**

The 2025 cycle is a complete, checkable loop. Guidance of 600,000–670,000 ounces was set in Q1 2025 and reiterated without change in Q2 and Q3 — no creeping revisions. The delivered figure was 690,000 ounces, above the top of the range. Management named the cause plainly: "due to strong outperformance across several assets."

Smaller promises also landed. Blackwater commercial production was forecast for the first half and declared on 2 May 2025. Goose was forecast for the second half and declared on 6 October 2025. The Salobo III expansion test was passed in March 2025 and the $144 million completion payment was made in April as described.

There is a mild pattern of conservatism rather than sandbagging: the company beat the top of its range by 3% and the midpoint by 9%, and Smallwood volunteered on 2025-11-07 that the existing portfolio "made that forecast look very conservative." Forecasting that your own forecast is conservative is a soft form of guidance inflation, but it proved accurate.

The one area where guidance is genuinely hard to check is the produced-but-not-yet-delivered balance — the gap between metal produced at a partner mine and metal actually delivered to Wheaton. It has now built for five consecutive quarters (136,000 ounces in Q1 2025 to 184,000 in Q1 2026). Management's explanations have been consistent and mechanical (shipment timing, Brazilian logistics around Carnival, new mines filling their pipelines), and the range guidance has widened honestly from "two to three months" to "2.5 to 3.5 months." This is disclosed, not hidden — but it is a working-capital drag that has run one way for over a year.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**Where analysts pushed hardest, and how management answered:**

- **The deliveries gap.** Cosmos Chiu (CIBC) has raised it on three separate calls, noting on 2026-05-08 that it "increased again in Q1, the fifth consecutive quarter." Management answers the mechanics fully but has never conceded it is a persistent drag rather than timing noise. This is the most-repeated unresolved question in the set.
- **Whether the Antamina price was sensible.** John Tumazos put the valuation question directly on 2026-03-13: silver producers trade at roughly "$8 per ounce of silver reserve and resource," so why not buy a producer instead? The answer from Lau was about margins — streams avoid the capital and operating costs a producer carries — which is true but sidesteps the price actually paid. Nobody on any call asked management to state the internal rate of return on the $4.3 billion.
- **Royalty burden on partners.** Derick Ma (TD Cowen) pressed on how much of a mine's economics Wheaton takes. Hodaly answered with an unusually specific and reassuring number — "optimally, we'd like to stay under 20%, 25%" — and explained the streams are structured so that "even if commodity prices dropped 20%, 30%, 40%, we are not overburdening the assets." This was a good answer to an important question, and it is load-bearing for the downside case.
- **Tax structure.** Chiu asked on 2026-05-08 about Canadian legislation enacted 26 March 2026 amending transfer-pricing rules. Lau's answer was firm and specific: the settlement with the Canada Revenue Agency applies through 2025, the Antamina purchase was funded through the Cayman subsidiary which "borrowed at that level and [has] all the cash flows and their own management team and board," and "we do not expect changes." Direct, not evasive — but this is a company whose low tax rate depends on structure surviving scrutiny, and legislation is moving.
- **What nobody asked.** Not one analyst across five calls asked what happens to the Antamina economics if silver falls back toward $40. Given the metal had already halved by the Q1 2026 call, that silence is itself notable.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

### 🔴 Red flags

- **The timing of the largest cheque in company history.** Silver reached its all-time high of $121.74 per ounce on 29 January 2026. Wheaton signed the $4.3 billion Antamina silver stream on **16 February 2026 — eighteen days later**. Silver trades at $60.16 today, down roughly 50% from that peak. The company paid a top-of-cycle price for a genuinely excellent asset. Under the terms disclosed — 33.75% of BHP's payable silver until 100 million ounces are delivered, then 22.5% for life of mine, with Wheaton paying 20% of spot — the deal generates roughly $340 million of annual cash margin at today's silver price against $4.3 billion deployed. That is a high-single-digit cash yield on a depleting entitlement, and a thin return for the largest capital commitment the company has ever made.
- **A fortress balance sheet spent at the top.** Net cash of $2.16 billion became roughly $1.7 billion of net debt in a single quarter, including a $1.5 billion term loan that matures in about two years. The covenant (net total debt to capitalisation below 0.60x) is not remotely threatened, but the optionality is gone: Wheaton entered this cycle able to buy anything and now has to fund from cash flow.
- **An 18% dividend increase declared at the peak** (Q4 2025, to $0.195 per share quarterly) — announced in the same breath as the record year and the record deal. Roughly $355 million a year of committed outflow, raised at the top of the cycle.
- **Promotional risk language.** "Premier low-risk choice," "one of the best lower-risk opportunities," repeated in the closing remarks of four of five calls. The stock's sensitivity to gold is 1.47x and it has fallen ~30% from its high. "Lower-risk *than a miner*" is defensible; "low-risk" is marketing.
- **A stated position reversed without comment.** The equity book Hodaly said in November 2025 they were "not looking to divest" shrank from $457 million to $54 million by 31 March 2026 — the quarter in which they needed cash for Antamina. No explanation was offered on the Q1 2026 call.

### 🟢 Green flags

- **No dilution.** Share count essentially flat for five quarters through the largest deal in industry history. This is the single most important negative test in this sector, and Wheaton passes it cleanly.
- **Guidance delivered above the top of the range** in 2025, with no mid-year revisions.
- **Best-in-class returns.** Return on invested capital of 18.1% and return on equity of 21.3%, the highest in the royalty and streaming peer group (Franco-Nevada 14.3%, Triple Flag 11.6%, Royal Gold 7.6%).
- **Downside genuinely engineered into the contracts.** Capping the take at 20–25% of an asset's economics, and structuring streams so the percentage falls as grades fall, protects partners at low prices — which protects Wheaton from the mine shutting.
- **Named, high-quality counterparties.** Vale (Salobo), BHP and Glencore (Antamina), Newmont (Peñasquito), Hudbay (Constancia), Ivanhoe (Platreef), B2Gold (Goose), Artemis (Blackwater). These are definitive purchase agreements with real operators, not memoranda of understanding with juniors.
- **The growth is contracted and largely de-risked.** Management's claim that the six near-term projects "have received their key permits, are fully funded and are either nearing or already well into construction" is consistent with the specific project-by-project updates given across the calls.
- **Reserve replacement demonstrated.** At Antamina, "over 95% of silver reserves have been replaced through resource conversion and exploration success" since 2015, on a land package exceeding 1,000 square kilometres.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

- **The new chief executive is the deal-maker.** Hodaly ran corporate development and sourced Antamina. A company that promotes its acquirer to the top job in the same quarter it writes its largest cheque has told you where its centre of gravity sits. His first call nonetheless opened with a defensive reassurance — growth "is not dependent on additional transactions" — which reads as an awareness that the market may now worry about the opposite.
- **A subtle shift in how growth is justified.** Through 2025 growth was described as organic and already-owned. From Q4 2025 the framing became "unmatched growth within the sector" delivered partly by purchase. The word "accretive" appears in nearly every capital-allocation answer, but no accretion figure is ever given.
- **Streaming versus royalties became a talking point.** Smallwood's Q3 2025 remark that the royalty model "requires full upfront payments and lacks embedded leverage" is a direct shot at Franco-Nevada. It matters for interpreting the cost proxies: a stream pays an ongoing 20% of spot, a royalty pays nothing ongoing. Wheaton's ~87% margin looks *lower* than a royalty peer's near-100% not because its cost position is worse, but because the contract form differs.
- **A quiet admission that the cycle is late.** Asked on 2026-03-13 when large copper-porphyry projects would need funding, Neil Burns agreed with the analyst that "they will take a few years to come about." Hodaly then said Wheaton expects "close to $10 billion in total in free cash flow" over three years and will "be looking to deploy that cash." The company is telling you it will have far more cash than near-term opportunities.
- **A small, honest disclosure that cuts against them.** The San Dimas stream's silver-to-gold conversion ratio moved from 70:1 to 90:1, and reverting requires silver to trade below 70:1 for six continuous months. Smallwood volunteered that he is "a little more bullish on silver than I am on gold" — a personal directional view from a chief executive, disclosed on the record.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.** The operating business is unambiguously getting better. Production rose 22% year over year in Q1 2026, revenue rose 89%, margins expanded from 67.8% to 77.5% of revenue, guidance was beaten, and the contracted path runs from roughly 900,000 ounces this year to 1.2 million by 2030 with the projects permitted, funded and mostly under construction. None of that is in doubt.

**The single most important thing to weigh before sizing.** Not the quality of the business — that is high and verified. It is that **the balance-sheet cushion this name is normally credited for no longer exists, and it was spent at the top of the metal it bought.** The director must size against roughly $1.7 billion of net debt, not the $2.16 billion of net cash the input bundle carries. The company converted its optionality into a single, enormous, life-of-mine claim on silver at within three weeks of silver's all-time high. If silver mean-reverts upward over a decade the decision looks visionary; at today's price it earns a high-single-digit cash yield on a depleting entitlement, and the equity carries a 1.47x sensitivity to a metal the current cycle read treats as facing a headwind.

**Economic moat: WIDE. Trend: STABLE.** The moat is a genuine intangible-and-contractual one: perpetual, life-of-mine purchase agreements on first- and second-quartile assets that cannot be competed away, held with counterparties that cannot easily replace the capital. The evidence supports width rather than mere assertion — return on invested capital of 18.1% is the highest in the peer group and rose alongside expanding margins, which is the signature of a moat holding, not eroding. It is *stable* rather than widening because the Antamina purchase demonstrates the moat's boundary: Wheaton had to pay a full, competitive, top-of-market price to add scale. The franchise protects the cash flows it already owns; it does not let the company buy new ones cheaply.

**Secular threat: none.** There is no structural force impairing the terminal value here. Precious-metals streaming is not exposed to artificial-intelligence displacement, payment disintermediation, channel shift or reimbursement compression. The contracts are perpetual and the counterparties are diversified across seven-plus producing assets and four continents. The risk in this name is *cyclical* — the price of gold and silver — and cyclical risk is emphatically not the same thing as terminal impairment. Any cheapness or dearness here is a function of where the metal sits, not of a shrinking base. Correspondingly, the current de-rating is not a fear-print offering mispriced value either: at $115.99 the shares trade at roughly 21x trailing enterprise value to operating earnings, the *highest* multiple in the royalty and streaming group, and at approximately my estimate of fair value rather than below it.
