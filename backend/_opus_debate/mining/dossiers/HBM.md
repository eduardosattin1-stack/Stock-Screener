# Forensic Dossier — Hudbay Minerals Inc. (HBM)

**Prepared 2026-08-04. Price used throughout: $24.77 per share on the New York Stock Exchange, in US dollars.** Hudbay is dual-listed (Toronto and New York); every figure in this dossier is the US-dollar New York line, which is also the currency the company reports in.

Transcripts reviewed: Q1 2025 (2025-05-12), Q2 2025 (2025-08-13), Q3 2025 (2025-11-12), Q4/full-year 2025 (2026-02-20), Q1 2026 (2026-05-01). Financial evidence: the screener's trailing-twelve-month block, the company's own filed quarterly statements through the June 2026 quarter, and the second-quarter 2026 results release of 2026-07-29.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

The story Hudbay told across these five calls changed in a very specific way: it went from **paying off debt** to **spending money again**, and the handover happened almost exactly at the top of the copper price.

In early 2025 the language was defensive and disciplined. Management talked about the "3P financial plan," about "prudent balance sheet management," about buying back its own bonds at a discount, about a leverage ratio of 0.6 times. The growth pipeline existed but was described as something to be *earned* — Copper World would be built once the balance sheet could carry it.

By the August 2025 call the pivot point arrived: Mitsubishi Corporation agreed to pay $600 million for a 30% stake in the Copper World project in Arizona. Management immediately reframed the company. The 3P plan was declared "retired." The vocabulary switched from repair to ambition — "generational investments," "unlock significant value," "we are in a really enviable position."

By the February 2026 call the transformation was complete and the superlatives arrived: the "gold standard in the copper space." By May 2026 the chief operating officer was telling an analyst that "in the next five years, we could potentially triple copper production." In June 2026 the company issued 46.8 million of its own shares — roughly 11.7% of the company — to buy Arizona Sonoran Copper and its Cactus project, and described the result as "the third largest copper district in North America."

Nothing was quietly dropped, which is itself a good sign. Every project introduced across these calls — the pebble crusher in Peru, the SAG mill conversion in British Columbia, New Ingerbelle, the 1901 deposit in Manitoba, Mason — was still being discussed with the same numbers a year later. What *changed* is the number of them, and the total dollars committed. This is a company that has moved from harvesting cash to deploying it, and investors should price it accordingly.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rating: Rising, and now arguably too high.**

- *Q1 2025:* measured, operational. "We are extremely well positioned to deliver our full-year 2025 consolidated production and cost guidance." Specific, bounded, delivered.
- *Q2 2025:* the balance sheet reaches "its best position in well over a decade." Confidence rises but stays anchored to a fact.
- *Q3 2025:* the most honest call in the set. Adjusted earnings of $0.03 per share, wildfires, blockades, a $60 million shipment stuck in port on ocean swells. Management led with "a quarter of resilience" and still *improved* cost guidance. Defensive framing, offensive result.
- *Q4 2025:* the tone breaks upward. "Transformative year," "third consecutive year of record financial performance," and the first appearance of "the gold standard in the copper space."
- *Q1 2026:* fully offensive. "Record revenue, record adjusted EBITDA, and record adjusted earnings." A confident answer that Manitoba's labour shortage "is just a blip, and we are working through it."

Guidance has become **more** specific over time, not less — the three-year production outlook published in March 2026 gives year-by-year copper and gold ranges through 2028. That is the opposite of a management team hedging.

The one genuine tonal warning is vocabulary. "The gold standard in copper," "the highest grade undeveloped open-pit copper oxide project in the world," "the third largest copper district in North America," "that itself is a company maker" (on Manitoba exploration). None of these are lies. All of them are the kind of phrasing that appears in mining companies at the point where the share price has already done the work.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Score: High.** This is the strongest part of the file and it is not close.

Track the cost guidance across a single year:

| Date | 2025 consolidated cash cost guidance |
|---|---|
| Original (early 2025) | $0.80 – $1.00 per pound |
| Q2 2025 (2025-08-13) | $0.65 – $0.85 per pound |
| Q3 2025 (2025-11-12) | $0.15 – $0.35 per pound |
| Outcome (2026-02-20) | Outperformed the twice-improved range |

And again in 2026:

| Date | 2026 consolidated cash cost guidance |
|---|---|
| Original | negative $0.30 – negative $0.10 per pound |
| Q2 2026 (2026-07-29) | negative $0.45 – negative $0.25 per pound |
| Year-to-date actual | approximately negative $1.00 per pound |

Guidance is revised in one direction — better — and then beaten. Production guidance has been met for eleven straight years on copper and five on gold, including a year with two months of evacuation. Where management said something would reverse, it reversed: the September 2025 quarter's stuck concentrate shipment ($60 million of value, blocked by ocean swells) did land in the December quarter, which printed record revenue of $733 million and $228 million of free cash flow. The wildfire insurance claim management refused to quantify in November 2025 came in at $25 million received in the December quarter.

There is a **sandbagging pattern**, and it is the investor-friendly kind: initial guidance is set conservatively and beaten. That is worth trusting — but it also means the *current* guidance is likely conservative too, which matters when you are trying to work out whether good news is already in the price.

Two blemishes. Revenue in the June 2026 quarter came in at $631.3 million against roughly $652 million expected — a miss, driven by another Peruvian port closure that deferred about 10,000 dry metric tonnes of concentrate into July. That is the **second port-driven sales deferral in four quarters**, which makes it a pattern rather than weather. And the Copper World definitive feasibility study slipped from "mid-2026" to "early in the fourth quarter."

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**The buyback — asked twice, never answered.** Bryce Adams of Desjardins asked in Q2 2025 whether the newly-approved share repurchase programme was a priority. Eugene Lei called it "good practice for companies of our size" and "good housekeeping." Adams pressed: "So it's good housekeeping, but not a priority?" A year later Lawson Winder of Bank of America asked the same question directly — would 2026 see more buyback activity than 2025, when the programme "was not acted upon at all"? Lei again declined: "We are not committing to any set dollar amount of share buybacks at this time." **The company has had the authority to buy its own shares for over a year, through a period when the stock traded as low as $9.04, and has not used it once.** Instead it issued 46.8 million shares to buy someone else's project. That is the single sharpest capital-allocation observation in this file.

**Copper World Phase 2 permitting — deflected twice.** Dalton Baretto (Canaccord) in Q1 2025 and Fahad Tariq (Jefferies) in Q2 2025 both asked what could accelerate the second phase. Both times: "the time for advancing Phase 2 is not really now."

**Copper World capital intensity — the question that turned into bad news.** Analysts pushed on this repeatedly. On the Q2 2026 call management conceded the number is going up.

**What analysts are not pressing hard enough on:** the by-product dependency. Nobody on these calls asked management what the cash cost looks like if gold and silver fall. That is the load-bearing question, and it is unasked.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

🔴 **Stock-funded acquisition at the top of the cycle.** 46.8 million shares (about 11.7% of the company) issued on 2026-06-24 at a 30% premium to Arizona Sonoran's prior close, for a project whose own updated pre-feasibility study is not due until the second half of 2027. Enterprise value of the deal: approximately $1.28 billion. This is the mining sector's most reliable way of destroying value, executed by a management team that had until then been exemplary — and executed with copper within 1% of its 52-week high.

🔴 **The buyback that never fired.** Authorised 2025, renewed 2026, used zero times, including at $9.04.

🔴 **Copper World capital creep, pre-announced** (Q2 2026 call). The definitive feasibility study lands early in the fourth quarter of 2026 with a number above the 2023 estimate of $1.3 billion.

🔴 **The "negative cash cost" is a gold and silver achievement, not a copper one.** Gold is 39–41% of gross revenue. As of 2026-08-04 gold trades at $4,151 per ounce — 26% below its 52-week high of $5,627 and below its 200-day average of $4,587 — and silver at $60 per ounce is roughly half its 52-week high of $121. The by-product credit engine that produces the headline negative cash cost is already deflating.

🔴 **Recurring Peruvian logistics and social risk.** Two port-driven sales deferrals in four quarters; a nine-day stoppage plus legal blockades in the September 2025 quarter; and the Consulta Previa consultation process at Maria Reyna and Caballito that management has been unable to date for over a year ("it's in the hands of the government").

🔴 **Promoter vocabulary is creeping in.** "The gold standard in copper," "company maker," "the highest grade undeveloped open-pit copper oxide project in the world."

🟢 **Cost guidance improved three times across two years and beaten each time.** Very few miners do this.

🟢 **Genuine deleveraging, executed cheaply.** From $526 million net debt (March 2025) to $80.5 million net cash (June 2026), with over $330 million retired and bonds repurchased *below par*.

🟢 **Reserve lives extended at all three producing mines** in the 2026-03-27 update: Peru to 2040 (488 million tonnes at 0.240% copper), Manitoba to 2041 (19.6 million tonnes, about 1.9 million ounces of gold), British Columbia to 2045 (345 million tonnes at 0.256% copper, 0.123 grams per tonne gold). A miner's real durability is the life of its orebody, and all three got longer, not shorter.

🟢 **Third-party validation of Copper World.** Mitsubishi paid $600 million for 30% — an arm's-length mark on an asset the market had been carrying at close to nothing — and the structure defers Hudbay's own equity contribution to 2028 at the earliest.

🟢 **Delivered guidance through genuine operational adversity** in 2025.

🟢 **First dividend increase in company history** (2026), plus a declared quarterly dividend of C$0.01 payable 2026-09-08. Small, but directionally right.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**Treatment and refining charges are now a tailwind, and almost nobody noticed.** On the Q4 2025 call, asked what charge assumption sits inside the cost guidance, Eugene Lei said: "our assumption is 0. So again, we're entering into deals that are lower — that are below 0." Smelters are effectively paying Hudbay to take its concentrate. That is a direct read on global concentrate scarcity and it is a real, quietly-disclosed margin tailwind — and a reason to believe the cost guidance is again conservative.

**A possible tariff windfall, disclosed in passing.** On the Q2 2026 call management flagged potential premiums of 3–12% for copper produced in the United States on the COMEX exchange versus the London Metal Exchange. Hudbay's entire American growth pipeline — Copper World, Cactus, Mason — sits on the right side of that.

**The oil sensitivity is small and was volunteered.** "For every $10 change in WTI oil, which we budget at $65 a barrel… it's about $0.04 per pound on the cash cost." Precise, checkable, and offered without being asked. That is the behaviour of a team that knows its own cost stack.

**The framing shift from "3P plan" to "generational investments."** The financial-repair vocabulary was retired the moment the balance sheet allowed it, and replaced by growth language, in the same twelve months that copper roughly doubled off its low. Language follows the commodity, not the other way round.

**The gold profile is quietly declining while the story gets louder.** The three-year outlook has consolidated gold falling from 217,000–272,000 ounces in 2026 to a three-year average of 243,000 ounces — a 9% decrease from 2025, on Pampacancha's depletion. Manitoba gold specifically steps down from 180,000–220,000 ounces in 2026 to 160,000–200,000 by 2028. Since gold is the by-product credit engine, the cost structure has a slow structural headwind built into management's own published plan.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Classification: STRENGTHENING.**

This is a genuinely good operating company. The cost position is first-quartile and verified — negative cash costs are not a presentation trick, they are what happens when a copper mine also produces 240,000 ounces of gold a year. The balance sheet went from levered to net cash without an equity raise. Guidance has been met or beaten with a consistency that is rare in this sector, including through a year that handed them wildfires and blockades. Reserve lives were extended at all three mines. Management discloses its own inconvenient arithmetic (the 20% project return behind the 90% marketed return; the oil sensitivity; the port deferrals) rather than hiding it.

**The single most important thing the director must weigh before sizing a position is this: the operating quality is real, and it is already in the price, and the price sits on a copper print of $6.65 per pound against a 200-day average of $5.81 and a 52-week low of $4.36.** The screener shows this company at 6.6 times enterprise value to EBITDA. That number is wrong — it is inflated by a $322 million non-cash impairment reversal. On the company's own trailing adjusted EBITDA of $1,271.6 million the multiple is 8.6 times, which is a *premium* to Teck Resources at 7.4 times and Ero Copper at 6.6 times. The apparent bargain is an artifact. What is left is a very good miner at a fair-to-full price, on peak commodity earnings, having just spent 11.7% of itself on a pre-feasibility-stage asset instead of buying back its own stock at $9.

**Economic moat: NARROW, trend STABLE.** The moat type is scale-cost — a genuine, defensible position low on the industry cost curve, protected by grade, by-product credits, and long reserve lives (14 to 19 years across the three mines). The evidence supports narrow rather than wide: gross margin expanded from 32.9% in the June 2025 quarter to 41.0% in the June 2026 quarter and cash costs keep improving, which is not an eroding franchise. But return on invested capital is only 6.0% and return on capital employed 10.3% against a return on equity of 18.4% — the capital base ($7.0 billion invested) is growing faster than the earnings it produces, because so much of it now sits in undeveloped Arizona ground. The moat is not widening; it is being spent.

**Secular threat: none.** Copper sits on the winning side of electrification, and there is no structural force impairing the terminal value of a low-cost orebody with reserves to 2040–2045. The risk here is entirely cyclical, not terminal — which is precisely why the cheapness question must be answered on the commodity price, not on the multiple. This is not a value trap. It is a good company at a full price on a high copper tape, and those two things fail in completely different ways.
