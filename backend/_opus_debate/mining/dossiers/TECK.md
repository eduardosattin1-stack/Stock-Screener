# Teck Resources — Forensic Dossier

Prepared 2026-08-04. Sources: five consecutive Teck earnings calls (2025-04-24, 2025-07-24,
2025-10-22, 2026-02-19, 2026-04-23), the screener metrics bundle, Teck's second-quarter 2026
results release of 2026-07-23, and dated company and press announcements verified on the web.
Teck reports in Canadian dollars; its New York listing trades in US dollars. Where a figure is
Canadian it is written C$, where it is American, US$.

---

## 1. Narrative arc

Across these five calls the story travels a long way, and the direction of travel is the single
most useful thing in the transcripts.

**Spring 2025 — "the ramp-up is nearly done."** Jonathan Price, the chief executive, opened the
first-quarter 2025 call with the macro (tariffs, a possible global downturn) and then asserted that
Quebrada Blanca, the huge new Chilean copper mine known as QB, would reach "full production ramp-up
by year-end." Two problems were named — an 18-day maintenance shutdown and a Chile-wide power outage —
and both were framed as external and temporary. The tailings management facility, the dam where waste
rock and sand are stored, was described as needing "additional mechanical movement" but with "no issues
with dam integrity." Annual guidance was "unchanged across all operations."

**Summer 2025 — the first honest admission.** By the second quarter the tailings problem stopped being
weather. Management said plainly that "the planned post QB2 construction pace of TMF development was
based on design assumptions for sand dredging rates, but have subsequently proven unachievable." That
is an engineering design miss, not a rainstorm. QB's annual target was cut from "around 230,000 tonnes"
to a 210,000–230,000 tonne range. On the same call the Highland Valley Copper mine-life extension was
sanctioned at C$2.1–2.4 billion, versus a prior estimate of C$1.8–2.0 billion — roughly 15–20% higher,
which analyst Bill Peterson of JPMorgan immediately flagged. A ship-loader at QB's port failed and was
expected out of service into the first half of 2026.

**Autumn 2025 — the reset, and the pivot.** Two things happened at once. First, the September 9, 2025
announcement of a merger of equals with Anglo American, which from that call forward becomes the
dominant subject. Second — and much more revealing about the operating business — a "comprehensive
operational review" with third-party technical experts, after which management said its plans would now
"embed assumptions based on demonstrated performance rather than design rates." That sentence is the
turning point of this dossier. It is a company admitting, in public, that it had been guiding off
engineering drawings rather than off what the plant actually did.

**Winter 2025 into 2026 — delivery.** The fourth quarter of 2025 was QB's strongest of the year at
55,000 tonnes, and full-year adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation
(EBITDA, a rough proxy for cash operating profit) rose 48% to C$4.3 billion. In the first quarter of
2026 EBITDA more than doubled to C$2.1 billion with margins of 53%, and by the second quarter of 2026
it reached C$2.193 billion on C$3.605 billion of revenue — a 61% margin. Three consecutive quarters of
stable QB output. Guidance reaffirmed. The narrative has shifted from "trust the design" to "here is
what we produced."

**What was quietly dropped, and what was quietly added.** Dropped: the language of doubling copper
production by the end of the decade, which was prominent in mid-2025 and is barely audible by 2026 —
Zafranal and San Nicolas are now described as feasibility work, and the US project (NewRange) was
waved off with "we have our hands full with other things right now." Added: the merger, the
QB–Collahuasi adjacency, and a small but interesting new item — the net profits interest royalty Teck
holds over Barrick's Fourmile gold discovery in Nevada, raised by an analyst in April 2026 and
acknowledged by Price as "a valuable asset" with "no specific plans" to monetise it.

**Where the deterministic screener proxies disagree with the company.** Two disagreements matter, and
both run in the same direction — the proxies are too pessimistic.

- **Leverage.** The input bundle carries a "live correction" asserting net debt of C$3.74 billion, built
  from a vendor balance sheet that books C$8.41 billion of long-term debt. Teck's own second-quarter
  2026 release shows long-term debt of C$3,425 million, current debt of C$418 million, and lease
  liabilities of C$965 million — C$4,808 million in total — against C$6,052 million of cash, for a net
  **cash** position of C$1,244 million. The vendor file mis-maps roughly C$5 billion of non-debt
  liabilities into borrowings. The company figure is corroborated by the transcripts, where a net cash
  position is described on every call from Q1 2025 forward. Use the company figure.
- **Margin.** The proxy trailing-twelve-month EBITDA margin of 46.2% sits below the 53% and 61% the
  company actually printed in the first two quarters of 2026, because the trailing window still carries
  the weak middle of 2025. The proxy understates today's cost position.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rising, and earned.** The evidence is in how specific the language becomes.

- Q1 2025 — defensive and hedged: "we do not expect announced tariffs to materially impact our
  business"; problems attributed to a "nationwide power outage" and "challenging weather."
- Q2 2025 — the tone drops. The word "unachievable" appears about the company's own design assumptions.
  Guidance is cut. Ian Anderson, asked about the ship-loader, gives a visibly uncomfortable answer about
  maritime permits.
- Q3 2025 — the tone is bifurcated: expansive and promotional about the merger ("I could not be more
  excited about it"), sober and technical about operations. Dale, on the tailings work, refuses to
  quantify: "it is still too early to tell in terms of what magnitude of improvement is."
- Q4 2025 — measurable claims return: fourth-quarter QB production of 55,000 tonnes, grades of 0.59%,
  a 50% margin, rock bench four complete.
- Q1 2026 — the most confident call of the set, and confident about the right things: mill availability
  of 92%, asset utilisation of 87%, recoveries of 83%, rock bench four complete and bench five due in
  the second quarter, "asset utilisation in the quarter remained above the range assumed in our 2026
  guidance."

The shift is from adjectives to numbers. That is what a genuine operational turn sounds like.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Medium, improving to good — with a clear before-and-after line at October 2025.**

The 2025 record is poor. The QB target was set at roughly 230,000 tonnes, guided to the low end in the
second quarter, formally cut to a 210,000–230,000 tonne range, and steady state slipped from end-2025
to end-2026. Highland Valley's sanctioned capital came in 15–20% above the figure given at the prior
strategy day. Management's stated causes — inflation, tariffs on construction materials, accelerated
mobile-equipment procurement — are plausible but were all foreseeable.

The 2026 record is good. Since the operational review, Teck has reaffirmed 2026–2028 production
guidance (January 2026), delivered against revised 2025 guidance, held Highland Valley capital at
C$2.1–2.4 billion through a peak spending year, and beaten earnings expectations materially — adjusted
earnings of US$1.39 per share in the second quarter of 2026 against a US$0.77 consensus, and C$1.93 per
share against a C$1.15 expectation on the Canadian line.

The honest reading is that this is a management team that has been beaten into conservatism and is now
sandbagging modestly — which is exactly what you want, but it is only three quarters old.

## 5. Analyst pressure points

- **Tailings, relentlessly.** Liam Fitzpatrick of Deutsche Bank in February 2026 went straight at the
  rock benches and asked, pointedly, "what's the confidence level at the moment that you won't have to
  build additional benches?" Anita Sarney of CIBC in April 2026 asked whether deposition capacity itself
  could become the bottleneck. Management's answers have improved but the question has not gone away in
  six quarters.
- **The accounting boundary between growth and sustaining capital.** Chris LaFemina of Jefferies pressed
  in July 2025 on why extra tailings spending was classified as sustaining rather than project capital —
  "I understand it's really just a question of semantics" — which is analyst code for a suspicion that
  project capital intensity is being flattered. Crystal Prystai, the chief financial officer, answered
  that the mine is producing copper so the spend is no longer growth. That answer is defensible and also
  convenient.
- **The Collahuasi joint venture economics.** LaFemina again, in October 2025: shareholders voted on the
  merger in December 2025 without knowing the economic split of the QB–Collahuasi combination that
  carries the advertised US$1.4 billion a year of EBITDA uplift. Price conceded "that is to be worked
  out." It is still to be worked out as of April 2026 — "nothing concrete to announce at this point."
- **Index membership.** Myles Allsop of UBS and Sarney both pushed on whether the combined company keeps
  its Canadian index status. The answer in April 2026 was "green shoots" and an ongoing consultation with
  no timeline. Unresolved.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Concerning**

- Serial slippage on the QB tailings facility through 2025, rooted in a design assumption that proved
  wrong rather than in weather (Q2 2025). The remaining sand-dam work must finish by end-2026 — the same
  kind of deadline that has moved before.
- Capital estimate inflation of 15–20% at Highland Valley between the strategy-day figure and sanction
  (Q2 2025), in a sector whose defining sin is exactly this.
- The advertised US$1.4 billion a year of QB–Collahuasi value rests on a commercial agreement that does
  not exist and whose split with Glencore, Anglo and Mitsui is unnegotiated (Q3 2025, still open Q2 2026).
- Buybacks were frozen on 2025-07-25 by the merger agreement, with roughly C$1.05 billion of the
  C$3.25 billion authorisation unused. The marginal buyer of the shares has been removed for the duration.
- Free cash flow conversion is poor at the moment: C$1.52 billion of trailing free cash flow against
  C$6.47 billion of trailing EBITDA, a 24% conversion, because Highland Valley is in its peak capital
  year (C$900 million to C$1.2 billion in 2026) with C$450–500 million of capitalised stripping on top.
- Two fatalities at Antamina in 2025, at a mine Teck does not operate but part-owns.
- Investor relations turnover: Emma Chapman, the long-standing head of investor relations, left in June
  2026 mid-transaction.

**Positive**

- The October 2025 operational review and its explicit adoption of "demonstrated performance rather than
  design rates" — a rare, verifiable governance correction.
- Three consecutive quarters of stable QB performance (Q4 2025, Q1 2026, Q2 2026) with production of
  55,000, 56,000 and 55,800 tonnes.
- Copper net cash unit cost of US$1.64 per pound in the second quarter of 2026, down from US$2.02.
- A genuine net cash balance sheet — C$1,244 million net cash and C$10.3 billion of liquidity at
  2026-06-30 — going into a heavy construction programme.
- Reserve and mine life extended where it counts: Highland Valley to 2046, QB with a tax stability
  agreement to 2037 and a resource management describes as supporting multiple expansions.
- The zinc business, long the forgotten half, is performing: Red Dog sales above guidance repeatedly, and
  Trail Operations restored to profitability with a July 2026 strategic investment agreement with the
  Canada Growth Fund to expand germanium, gallium and antimony output.
- The Fourmile royalty — a 10% net profits interest rising to 15% after six million ounces, over ground
  that overlaps Barrick's Nevada discovery — is carried at essentially nothing and is not in any
  published sum-of-the-parts.

## 7. Hidden signals

- **"Risk-adjusted" enters the vocabulary in October 2025** and never leaves. Plans become "reasonable,
  achievable, and more conservative." Contrast with April 2025's "we have several data points showing
  that we can and have already operated at these levels."
- **Growth language cools as the merger warms.** In July 2025 the mine-life extension was "foundational
  to our strategy to double copper production by the end of the decade." By 2026 the doubling target is
  absent and projects "compete for capital based on risk-adjusted returns" inside a larger portfolio.
  Capital discipline improving, or optionality quietly deferred to the acquirer — both readings are open.
- **A new operating voice.** Dale Webb speaks on tailings from October 2025 and answers narrowly and
  technically, declining to quantify improvement. That is the sound of a technical team that has been
  burned by a number and will not give another one until it is real.
- **The royalty book is larger than the market knows.** Price in April 2026: the Fourmile interest "is a
  reflection of what is actually quite a large portfolio of various royalties associated with prior
  exploration projects." Nobody has valued it publicly.
- **Sell-side coverage is quietly evaporating.** Only one price target printed in the last quarter
  (Deutsche Bank, US$68, 2026-07-02) against five in the past year averaging US$57. Analysts are moving
  their models to Anglo American.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict

**Trajectory: strengthening.** The operating business has been rebased honestly, is delivering against
the rebased plan, is generating record margins, and is doing so from a net cash balance sheet while
funding a major brownfield extension. The 2025 credibility damage was real and is being repaired with
evidence rather than assertion.

**The single thing to weigh before sizing.** This is a price-taker at a commodity high, not a
compounder at a discount. Copper touched US$6.65 per pound on 2026-08-04 — its 52-week high, 53% above
the US$4.36 low — and the whole copper cohort rallied 5–8% in that single session. Teck's own torque
measure says a 10% move in copper swings operating profit by about 14%, in both directions. Every one of
the margin, cost and cash-flow improvements catalogued above is amplified by that price and would be
amplified in reverse. The quality of the assets is not the question; the price of the metal embedded in
today's share price is.

**Moat.** Scale-cost, built on irreplaceable orebodies — Quebrada Blanca, Highland Valley, Red Dog,
Antamina. It is narrow rather than wide: Teck cannot set the price of copper or zinc, and its returns
follow the metal. Return on invested capital of about 5.5% and return on equity of about 9.6% on a
trailing basis are unremarkable, but those trail a depressed period; the current quarterly margin of 61%
and the fall in unit costs to US$1.64 per pound indicate the cost position is improving, not eroding, as
QB reaches design rates and Highland Valley's life is extended two decades. The moat is narrow and
widening at the margin.

**Secular threat.** Manageable, and arguably favourable. Copper is on the winning side of
electrification, grid build-out and data-centre power demand — the structural forces that impair
terminal value elsewhere increase it here. The genuine long-term risks are jurisdictional and
operational (Chilean permitting, tailings, Red Dog's finite life to around 2031), not the disappearance
of demand. The cheapness question is therefore purely cyclical: the shares are not a fear-print and not
a value trap, they are a well-run cyclical trading near the top of its commodity's range.
