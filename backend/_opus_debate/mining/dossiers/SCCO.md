# Southern Copper Corporation (SCCO) — Forensic Dossier

*Prepared 2026-08-04. Price used throughout: $195.475 per share on the New York Stock Exchange (US dollars), the live quote at the time of writing — up 5.1% on the day from the prior close of $185.91, which is the price the screener scan captured. Market value roughly $163.1 billion on about 834 million shares.*

Transcripts reviewed: the first-quarter 2025 call (2025-04-25), the second-quarter 2025 call (published 2026-01-22), the third-quarter 2025 call (2025-10-29), the fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 call (2026-01-28), and the second-quarter 2026 call (2026-07-22).

## 1. Narrative arc

The story management tells has changed shape three times across these five calls, and the direction of travel matters more than any single quarter.

**Early 2025 — a volume-growth story with a tariff sideshow.** In the first-quarter 2025 call the chief financial officer, Raul Jacob, led with production: 968,200 tons of copper planned for the year, "2,400 tons increase when compared to our plan." The capital programme was framed as "exceeds $15 billion" for the decade. The dominant uncertainty was the possible United States tariff on copper imports and the resulting gap between the two main copper exchanges — at its peak on 26 March 2025 the American COMEX price sat 17% above the London Metal Exchange price.

**Mid-to-late 2025 — the story quietly becomes a by-products story.** By the third-quarter 2025 call the headline had moved from tons of copper to cost per pound: "a cash cost of $0.42 per pound of copper in the third quarter of this year, one of the industry's lowest." Read carefully, that number is not a mining achievement. Copper production actually *fell* 7% that quarter. What produced the low cost was a $895 million credit from selling molybdenum, silver, zinc and sulphuric acid, which is subtracted from copper's cost line. Simultaneously, the company disclosed it had deliberately switched its flexible Buenavista concentrator away from copper and onto zinc and silver — sacrificing roughly 10,000 tons of copper a year because "there is much more value in focusing only on zinc."

**2026 — a price story wearing an operating story's clothes.** The second-quarter 2026 call opened with "record-breaking results in sales, adjusted EBITDA and net income" and a cash cost, after by-product credits, of $0.05 per pound. In the first quarter of 2026 the same figure was *negative* $0.11 — the company was effectively being paid to mine copper. The engine is price, not production: the London copper price averaged $6.04 per pound in the quarter versus $4.32 a year earlier (up 40%), silver averaged $73.49 an ounce versus $33.62 (up 118%), and molybdenum averaged $29.44 per pound versus $20.57 (up 43%). Copper output fell 3.5% year on year, with Peru down 12%.

**What was quietly dropped, and what was quietly added.** The 1.6 million ton long-term copper target survives every call, but its date has drifted — described as reachable "as we go towards 2030" in the third-quarter 2025 call, then as "by 2033, 2034" in the second-quarter 2026 call. Meanwhile the decade capital programme has inflated from "exceeds $15 billion" (first quarter 2025) to "exceeds $20.5 billion" (second quarter 2026), a 37% increase in eighteen months without a corresponding increase in the production target.

**A data correction the committee needs.** The screener bundle carries a trailing twelve-month free cash flow figure of $5.97 billion. That is overstated by roughly $884 million because the capital-expenditure sign for the June 2026 quarter is flipped in the source feed. Computed correctly from the four quarterly filings, operating cash flow of $6,736.9 million less capital spending of $1,655.6 million gives **$5.08 billion** of true trailing free cash flow. At the live price that is a 3.1% free-cash-flow yield, not 3.7%, and the $3.02 billion of cash dividends paid over the same twelve months consumed 59% of it while capital spending was rising 56%.

## 3. Tone and confidence trajectory

**Rating: Rising in tone, but the confidence is borrowed from the price tape rather than from the mine plan.**

- *Q1 2025 — measured, defensive on macro.* "We believe an intense commercial war between the U.S. and China will affect economic growth worldwide, consequently impacting copper demand." Careful, hedged, willing to name a risk.
- *Q3 2025 — assertive on cost.* "The combination of higher production volumes and better copper and by-product prices enabled us to achieve a cash cost of $0.42 per pound... one of the industry's lowest." Notice the honesty: management itself names prices as the cause. They are not hiding it.
- *Q4 2025 — refuses to forecast.* Asked whether price momentum would be sustained: "We were not forecasting copper prices. That is not our business. Our businesses to focus on controlling costs and producing as much copper as we can." This is the single most credible sentence in five calls. A promoter would have answered the question.
- *Q2 2026 — celebratory.* "Another exceptional quarter, registering record-breaking results." The macro caveat that opened every 2025 call has disappeared entirely.

The shift is from cautious-offensive to unreservedly-offensive, and it tracks the copper price almost exactly. What has *not* changed is the specificity: this management team gives hard numbers for production, costs, credits and project timing every single quarter, and does not retreat into adjectives. That is a meaningful credibility marker in a sector where the opposite is the norm.

## 4. Guidance credibility

**Reliability: High.**

| Guidance given | When | Delivered | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 968,200 tons copper for 2025 | Q1 2025 | Revised to 965,300 (Q2), 960,000 (Q3), delivered 956,270 | Missed by about 1%, but revised down in public, in stages, with a named cause (ore grades) |
| 27,400 tons molybdenum 2025 | Q1 2025 | 31,200 tons | Beat by 14% |
| 23 million ounces silver 2025 | Q1 2025 | 24 million ounces | Beat |
| 170,100 tons zinc 2025 | Q1 2025 | About 176,000 tons | Beat |
| 911,400 tons copper 2026 | Q4 2025 | Raised to 917,000 | Tracking ahead |

The pattern is a small, honestly-flagged shortfall on the main product and consistent beats on by-products. That is the opposite of the sector's usual habit of guiding by-products conservatively while promising copper growth that never arrives. The screener's earnings-beat record of six out of seven quarters is consistent with what the calls show.

**The one place credibility is weak is project timing, not operating guidance.** The El Pilar project is the clearest example. An analyst pressed on it directly in the second-quarter 2026 call: bought out of the Mercator bankruptcy in 2015 for $100 million, it took eleven years to reach board approval. Management's explanation — a technical review of copper recoveries, then permit renewals — is plausible and specific, but eleven years is eleven years. The Cuajone expansion has been "something that we're looking as a possibility" since the third-quarter 2025 call and is still not board-approved. The 1.6 million ton target has slipped from "towards 2030" to "2033, 2034."

## 5. Analyst pressure points

Three questions recur and are consistently not answered.

1. **The share of sales going to the American exchange.** Asked in the first-quarter 2025 call by a Goldman Sachs analyst — what percentage of contracts are spot, what is the duration of the rest, are COMEX customers renegotiating? Answer: "we are not commenting on this." Asked again by UBS in the fourth-quarter 2025 call — has the COMEX percentage changed? Answer: "We don't make comments on that, I'm so sorry." This is the single most valuable undisclosed number in the company. Southern Copper has been earning a premium because American copper prices sit above world prices on tariff expectations; investors cannot size that premium or its durability.

2. **Hedging.** HSBC asked in the third-quarter 2025 call whether the company would lock in the extraordinary silver and molybdenum prices ahead of the capital-spending phase. Answer: "We have not had any specific discussions on hedging." The company hedged once before, with a zero-cost collar during the Toquepala expansion, and management called it "a good idea." They are choosing not to repeat it while entering a larger build with higher metal prices. That is a deliberate decision to keep the whole commodity swing.

3. **The stock dividend.** Also HSBC, third quarter 2025: the cash dividend of $0.90 "is pretty good... I just don't see the rationale for stock dividend." The answer was revealing — "we couldn't, we wouldn't be able to pay the dividends that we have been paying without using a portion of the shares. $0.90 in cash is what the company can pay out." In other words, the headline distribution is larger than the cash the business generates for it, and the gap is filled by handing out treasury shares.

What analysts are *not* pressing on, and should: the three consecutive years of falling copper output, and what happens to reported unit costs when silver and molybdenum revert.

## 6. Red flags and green flags

**Red flags**

- *The headline cash cost is a by-product mirage.* Q2 2026 cash cost after credits was $0.05 per pound; before credits it was $2.29 and rising. Silver contributed roughly a third of the credit at an average price of $73.49 an ounce in the quarter. Silver now trades at $60.14 — 18% below that average, and less than half its 52-week high of $121.30. The Q3 2026 credit shrinks mechanically.
- *Volumes have fallen three years running,* with no relief until Tia Maria in the second half of 2027 (Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 calls). Peru was down 12% in the June 2026 quarter.
- *The capital programme grew 37% in eighteen months* — "exceeds $15 billion" (Q1 2025) to "exceeds $20.5 billion" (Q2 2026) — while the production target moved *later*.
- *The distribution is only partly cash.* The declared second-quarter 2026 dividend was "$1.10 per share cash dividend and 0.0120 stock dividend," with a combined stated value of $3.23 per share. Only $1.10 of that is cash. The stock portion is a pro-rata share hand-out from treasury: it costs the company nothing and gives shareholders nothing they did not already own, but it makes the headline yield look roughly three times larger than the cash actually paid.
- *That treasury reserve is finite.* Management disclosed about 65 million treasury shares remaining after the third-quarter 2025 payment. At roughly 10 million shares a quarter, the reserve is exhausted in about six quarters — around the end of 2027 — after which the company must either issue new shares or cut the headline payout. Share count has already risen from 792.5 million (first quarter 2025) to 829.1 million (second quarter 2026), up 4.6%.
- *Ownership concentration.* Grupo Mexico owns approximately 88.9%, leaving a free float near 11%. Minority holders have no practical governance recourse, and the small float amplifies price moves in both directions.
- *No hedging into a rising capital-spending phase* (Q3 2025 call), by explicit choice.

**Green flags**

- *Management does not forecast the copper price and says so.* "We were not forecasting copper prices. That is not our business" (Q4 2025). In a sector that runs on price promotion, this is a genuine credibility asset.
- *Mine plans are built at $3.30 per pound copper* (Q1 2025) — roughly half the current price. Long-term plans are not calibrated to a boom.
- *Production discipline over headline tonnage.* The decision to run the Buenavista concentrator on zinc rather than copper, sacrificing about 10,000 tons of copper because "the value is much more profitable," is exactly the value-maximising behaviour that miners usually abandon when copper is in the headlines.
- *Balance sheet.* Net debt of roughly $1.2 billion against $9.93 billion of trailing earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Cash and short-term investments of $7.33 billion at 30 June 2026. The June 2026 bond was oversubscribed 3.2 times at 5.35%.
- *Reserve base.* Widely reported as the largest among listed copper producers at roughly 51.1 million tons of contained copper, with a reserve life in excess of 35 years against an industry norm nearer 20 to 25 — a genuinely scarce asset that cannot be replicated.
- *The growth pipeline is wholly owned and self-funded.* Tia Maria (42% complete at 30 June 2026, 120,000 tons a year, estimated cash cost $1.16 per pound), El Pilar (36,000 tons, board-approved in the June 2026 quarter after eleven years), then Los Chancas, Michiquillay and El Arco. No joint-venture partners, no equity issuance required.
- *Insider behaviour is a mild negative, not a red flag.* Zero open-market purchases against 24 sales of 17,516 shares. In an 88.9%-controlled company, management share sales carry little signal either way.

## 7. Hidden signals

**The vocabulary moved from tonnage to unit cost exactly as tonnage started falling.** In the first-quarter 2025 call the production number came first and cost came fifth. By the third-quarter 2025 and second-quarter 2026 calls, the cost-per-pound figure is in the opening paragraph. This is not deception — the numbers are all disclosed — but it is a tell about which metric management would prefer the market anchor on.

**"Value maximisation" is being used to explain away declining copper.** The Buenavista zinc decision is defensible on its own terms and I credit it. But it also conveniently supplies a good reason for a copper shortfall in a year when copper output was going to disappoint anyway.

**A chief executive changed with no comment.** Oscar Gonzalez Rocha is named as chief executive on the 2025 calls; by the second-quarter 2026 call Leonardo Contreras — previously introduced only as a board member — is chief executive. No transition commentary, no strategic statement from the new voice, and the chief financial officer continues to deliver the entire call. Continuity is preserved; transparency around the change was not.

**The Peruvian political question was refused.** Asked in the second-quarter 2026 call whether the incoming administration's proposal to distribute up to 40% of the mining canon directly to residents in producing areas would help or complicate community agreements, management declined: "we will not comment on this until we see specifically what's the proposal." Given that community opposition delayed Tia Maria for roughly a decade, this is the risk that most deserved an answer.

## 8. Capital-allocation verdict (for the director)

**Trajectory: Stable.** The financial results are strengthening dramatically, but the operating base is not. Copper volumes have declined three years in a row, unit costs before by-product credits have risen 12% in five quarters, and every dollar of the earnings increase traces to metal prices the company explicitly refuses to forecast. Set against that, the balance sheet, the reserve base and the capital discipline are all genuinely improving, and the growth pipeline is real, funded and wholly owned.

**The single thing to weigh before sizing.** Every headline that makes this company look exceptional right now — record earnings, record margin, a near-zero cash cost — is a function of four commodity prices sitting at or near multi-year highs simultaneously. Copper trades at $6.652 a pound against a 200-day average of $5.81 and a 52-week low of $4.355. The deterministic torque estimate in the input bundle says a 10% move in the commodity swings earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation by about 13.5% — roughly $1.5 billion on the current run rate — and that arithmetic runs in both directions. The question is not whether this is a good company. It plainly is. The question is what fraction of today's price assumes today's prices are permanent.

**Economic moat: wide, and stable.** The moat type is scale-cost, anchored in irreplaceable orebodies: the largest reserve base among listed copper producers, a reserve life beyond 35 years, and a position in the lowest cost quartile that survives even when the by-product credits are stripped out (a pre-credit cost of $2.29 per pound against a copper price above $6 leaves an enormous margin at almost any plausible price). The returns evidence supports width rather than erosion — return on invested capital of 26.2% and return on equity of 49.4% on the trailing twelve months, both higher than the 19.9% and 36.6% recorded for the prior fiscal year, with gross margin expanding from 53.1% to 62.0% year on year in the June quarter. The one structural qualification is real but slow: ore grades are declining at Toquepala and Cuajone by management's own account, which is why three years of falling volumes and a rising pre-credit unit cost sit alongside those rising returns. The growth pipeline is designed to offset exactly that, and it is funded.

**Secular threat: none.** Copper is on the winning side of electrification, grid build-out and data-centre construction — the risk here is cyclical price, not terminal demand. There is no structural force impairing the long-run value of these orebodies. That distinction matters: this is not a cheap stock on a melting base, which would be a value trap. It is an excellent business whose share price has run ahead of any reasonable estimate of what the business is worth through a full cycle.
