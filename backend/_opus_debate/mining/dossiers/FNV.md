# Franco-Nevada Corporation (FNV) — Forensic Dossier

**Prepared:** 2026-08-04 · **Price used:** $221.30 (New York listing, US dollars; the company also trades in Toronto and reports in US dollars) · **Market value:** about $42.7 billion · **Transcripts reviewed:** Q1 2025 (2025-05-09), Q2 2025 (2025-08-11), Q3 2025 (2025-11-04), full-year 2025 (2026-03-11), Q1 2026 (2026-05-13)

Franco-Nevada is not a miner. It buys royalties and streams — contracts that entitle it to a slice of the metal or the revenue coming out of mines that other people build, operate and pay for. It owns 121 cash-flow-producing contracts and never writes an operating cheque. That distinction governs everything below.

---

## 1. NARRATIVE ARC

**The story did not shift. It compounded.** Across five calls the message is remarkably stable: buy long-dated claims on great ore bodies, stay debt-free, raise the dividend every year, and let the metal price do the rest. What changed over the eighteen months is the *scale* of deployment and the slow thaw of one frozen asset.

Three threads run through the period.

**Thread one — a deployment sprint.** In Q1 2025 management described a deal pipeline of "$100 million to $500 million" candidates. By the full-year 2025 call the tone had escalated to "2024 and 2025 have been 2 of our best ever years for capital deployment," listing six major acquisitions in eighteen months: Yanacocha (Peru), Cascabel (Ecuador), Sibanye's Western Limb platinum operations (South Africa), Porcupine (Ontario), Côté (Ontario) and the Arthur project (Nevada). Q1 2026 added four more — Casa Berardi with Orezone, i-80 Gold, Minerals 260 in Western Australia, and a third-party royalty on Banyan's AurMac. This is the single biggest change in the narrative, and Section 6 explains why it cuts both ways.

**Thread two — Cobre Panama, from write-off to live option.** The framing evolved carefully. Q1 2025: "President Mulino of Panama continues to indicate willingness to hold discussions." Q2 2025: the preservation plan approved, concentrate shipped, and the memorable line that Cobre is "the biggest free option that you can get in the royalty and streaming industry… we're not dependent on it in any way." Q3 2025: power plant pre-commissioned, environmental audit formally commenced. Q1 2026: coal shipments received, both power units restarted and feeding the Panamanian grid, stockpile processing approved, five interim audit reports "without any material deficiencies identified." Management has never once put a date on it and has never included it in guidance. That restraint is the most credible thing in these transcripts.

**Thread three — a quiet widening beyond gold.** Management repeatedly denied a strategy shift when analysts probed (Bernie Picchi, Q2 2025: "more aggressive business development, less interest in non-precious metals, greater focus on North America — is this a strategy shift?"). Paul Brink: "No, there isn't… we want to be the go-to gold stock." Yet the composition tells a subtler story: precious metals ran 79% of revenue in Q1 2025 and 85% in Q1 2026, while a legacy oil-and-gas royalty book that management barely mentioned in 2025 became a talking point in 2026 once crude spiked. Nothing was dropped; the emphasis simply follows the price.

**What was quietly dropped:** nothing material. That itself is unusual and worth noting.

## 2. CLAIMS vs FINANCIALS (forensic)

The financial record is available and it corroborates the narrative closely. Quarterly revenue and adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA — a rough proxy for cash operating profit):

| Quarter | Revenue | Adjusted EBITDA | Margin | Gold-equivalent ounces sold | Cash cost per ounce |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2025 | $368.4M | $321.9M | 87.4% | 126,585 | $304 |
| Q2 2025 | $369.4M | $365.7M | 99.0%* | 112,093 | — |
| Q3 2025 | $487.7M | $427.3M | 87.6% | 138,772 | $340 |
| Q4 2025 | $597.3M | $541.2M | 90.6% | 141,856 | — |
| Q1 2026 | $650.7M | $591.9M | 91.0% | 136,353 | $341 |

\*Q2 2025 is flattered by a $42.2M gain on the sale of accumulated gold bullion inventory.

**Claim: "we expect to benefit fully as the cost per GEO should not increase significantly" (Q1 2025).** *Verified.* Cash cost per gold-equivalent ounce moved from $304 (Q1 2025) to $341 (Q1 2026), a 12% rise, while the realised price per ounce roughly doubled. Margin per ounce went $2,500-plus (Q1 2025) → $3,116 (Q3 2025) → roughly $4,159 (Q1 2026). Over five years the company reports cash cost rising from $242 (2020) to $325 (2025) — a 34% increase against a gold price that roughly tripled. This is the single most important verified fact in the file: the cost base is close to fixed, so essentially every incremental dollar of metal price lands in operating profit.

**Claim: 2025 guidance would be met at the high end.** *Delivered.* Revised guidance was 495,000–525,000 total ounces; the company sold 519,106. Precious-metal guidance was 420,000–440,000; it sold 440,140, slightly above the top. No walk-back, no restatement.

**Claim: "the more than $1 billion in earnings is close to a 60% earnings margin" (full-year 2025).** *Verified.* Full-year 2025 net income was $1,032.1M on revenue of $1,822.8M — a 56.6% net margin. Management rounded in its own favour by roughly three percentage points; call that colour, not distortion.

**Claim: "we ended the quarter debt free" — repeatedly.** *Verified, and this is the cleanest item in the file.* Total debt at 2026-03-31 was zero; cash and short-term investments were $717.1M. The revolver was drawn twice (for Arthur in Q3 2025) and repaid inside a single quarter both times. Diluted shares outstanding went from 192.5 million (Q2 2024) to 193.2 million (Q1 2026) — growth of about 0.5% across eight quarters. For a sector whose besetting sin is serial equity issuance, that is close to a perfect record. There is no dilution pattern here to flag.

**Where the narrative runs richer than the numbers — organic growth.** Management called it "the most exciting growth outlook in the space" (Q2 2025) and "roughly 50% growth in GEOs over 5 years" (Q3 2025). Read the fine print: the published 2030 outlook is 555,000–615,000 ounces *excluding* a Cobre Panama restart, against 519,106 delivered in 2025. That is 7% to 18% over five years — roughly 1.5% to 3.5% a year. The headline growth number is almost entirely a Cobre restart plus metal price. The underlying portfolio grows slowly. This is the largest gap between story and arithmetic in the file, and it is a gap of emphasis rather than a misstatement — the company does publish both numbers.

**Where a deterministic screen and the company disagree.** Our proxy reads a trailing EBITDA margin of 97.8%. The company's own adjusted figure is 90.9% for 2025 and 91.0% for Q1 2026. The gap is real: the screen's EBITDA sweeps in one-off items the company excludes — a gain on the partial buy-back of the Cascabel stream, a refund from the Canada Revenue Agency, and gains on selling equity stakes. Both readings put Franco-Nevada in the top quartile of its cost cohort; the honest number is about 91%, not 98%.

## 3. TONE & CONFIDENCE TRAJECTORY

**Rating: Rising, then levelling — and notably free of the defensive vocabulary the sector usually reaches for.**

- **Q1 2025 (rising):** "Today, we're reporting our best financial results ever… and that without any contributions from Cobre Panama." Offensive framing, but with a hedge built in.
- **Q2 2025 (rising, most expansive):** "Combined the acquisition over the last 2 years have transformed our longer-term growth outlook… you have the most exciting growth outlook in the space." This is the high-water mark of promotional language across the five calls.
- **Q3 2025 (rising, more specific):** "For the third time this year, we're announcing record quarterly results." Brink offered a genuinely useful piece of forward thinking on reserve cut-off grades: "let's just assume we're at $4,000 gold in 3 years' time… even if the gold price stayed flat in that scenario, our stock price would be worth a lot more because you get a huge amount of ounces that get moved into reserves." That is a testable mechanism, not a slogan.
- **Full-year 2025 (rising, first promotional stretch):** the deal-cost boast — 820,000 royalty ounces added since year-end at an average cost of $770 an ounce, "an undiscounted value of over $4 billion at today's gold prices."
- **Q1 2026 (confident, and the most promotional single line in the file):** "ounces that are 100% attributable to Franco have a value of $124 billion at current gold prices. That's just shy of triple our current market cap."

Guidance became *more* specific over the period, not less — the company moved to a fixed $4,500 gold conversion price for 2026 ounce reporting and explained the mechanics clearly when Tanya Jakusconek pressed on it. No shift from offensive to defensive language occurred. Notably, no call contains cost-inflation hand-wringing, because the model does not have that exposure.

## 4. GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

**Score: High.**

The one full guidance cycle we can audit inside this window closed cleanly: 2025 revised guidance of 495,000–525,000 ounces produced 519,106; the precious-metal sub-range was slightly exceeded. Management also disclosed guidance assumptions rather than hiding them — $3,250 gold and $37 silver for 2025, $70 oil for 2026, with an explicit sensitivity ("an increase of $10 relative to our assumed WTI price of $70 per barrel would be expected to increase our oil revenue by approximately 12%").

There is a mild sandbagging pattern, and it is disclosed rather than concealed. Full-year 2025: "Our guidance this year is based on $70 per barrel oil. The last I saw WTI prices were $85 a barrel. If that sustained our 2026 guidance may be too conservative." Excluding Cobre Panama from guidance entirely while calling it "all upside" is the same behaviour: set the bar where you can clear it, then say so out loud.

The counterweight is Argentina. Asked in Q3 2025 how many ounces Taca Taca and San Jorge contribute to the 2029 outlook, Sandip Rana answered "on a combined basis, it's about 5,000 GEOs" — roughly 1% of production from two assets that occupy a disproportionate share of the growth narrative. Credit for answering honestly; note the gap between airtime and contribution.

One clerical wobble worth flagging so a reader does not trip on it: the 2026-03-11 transcript opens with the investor-relations host saying "year-end 2022 results." It is a transcription artefact, not a management error.

## 5. ANALYST PRESSURE POINTS

**Cobre Panama timing — asked every single quarter, never dated.** Tanya Jakusconek in Q1 2025 pushed hardest: what is happening on site, is there a timeline for selling the concentrate, what would make you drop the arbitration? Brink: "No timeline yet, Tanya. Really three moving pieces there." He also corrected her framing precisely — "first, it's suspending rather than dropping" — which reads as care rather than evasion. In the eighteen months since, every operational milestone he described has actually occurred. This is a non-answer that aged well.

**Special dividends — asked twice, refused twice.** John Tumazos (Q1 2025) and Heiko Ihle (Q1 2026, noting it had "come up in 2 investor calls over the past call it month") both pressed for a bigger payout against record cash flow. Rana's answer did not move: "sustainable and progressive… I don't think you'll see any sort of special dividend coming from Franco." Refusing to promise cash to shareholders while sitting on record cash flow is the opposite of promotional behaviour, and it is the strongest single credibility signal in the file.

**Deal sizing — repeatedly asked, repeatedly fuzzed.** Jakusconek tried three times across three calls to get a range. Brink (Q1 2025): "Large is a spectrum from zero to plus $500 million." Eaun Gray (full-year 2025): "it's really hard to handicap." Jakusconek's own retort — "you can run the truck through the 0 to $4.3 billion" — is fair. This one is a genuine non-answer.

**What analysts are not asking, and should be.** Nobody in five calls asked whether buying aggressively into record metal prices contradicts management's own stated cycle framework (Section 6). Nobody asked what happens to the contract book if a host-country court decides an Ontario-law stream does not bind it — a question that stopped being hypothetical in June 2026 (Section 6). And nobody pressed on the gap between the "45%–50% growth" headline and the low-single-digit organic outlook underneath it.

## 6. RED FLAGS / GREEN FLAGS

### 🔴 Red flags

**Capital deployed at the top of the cycle, against management's own framework.** This is the most important item in the dossier. In Q2 2025 Brink laid out the discipline explicitly: "you do want to have a gut sense of where you are in the cycle… Where do you want to spend a lot of money — that's at the bottom of the cycle." The company then spent through the highest gold prices in history, ten major transactions in twenty-one months. Management's defence is the cost per ounce acquired — 820,000 royalty ounces at an average $770 an ounce against a spot price above $4,000. That defence is quantitative and it is not weak. But the plain fact stands: the incremental dollar is being invested into a record tape by a management team whose own stated preference is to invest into a trough. The return on invested capital of about 14.3% sits below Wheaton Precious Metals' roughly 18.1%, which is what deploying $4-plus billion at 2024–2026 prices looks like on the ledger.

**The $124 billion ounce statistic (Q1 2026).** "Ounces that are 100% attributable to Franco have a value of $124 billion at current gold prices. That's just shy of triple our current market cap." This is a gross, undiscounted, un-risked, un-costed count of every measured, indicated and inferred ounce under Franco's claims, set beside a market capitalisation. Inferred ounces are the least reliable resource category; none of these ounces carries a mine plan, a permit or a discount rate. Placing that number next to the market value invites exactly the inference it should not support. It is the clearest promoter tell in five otherwise disciplined calls.

**Marking the equity book to a hot tape in prepared remarks.** "Needless to say, we're delighted with the roughly triple in value of the shares from our purchase price" (Q1 2025, Discovery Silver); "we've seen the discovery share prices increased tenfold" (full-year 2025); "their share price is up 50% in the couple of weeks since the transaction was announced" (full-year 2025, Minerals 260). The gains are real and the balance sheet carries $1.33 billion of long-term investments to prove it. But quoting two-week share-price moves as evidence of a financing model working is momentum, not underwriting — and it works in reverse when juniors sell off.

**Enforceability of the contract book is no longer theoretical.** On 10 June 2026 the Commercial Court of Ouagadougou nullified the 2014 gold purchase agreement covering Burkina Faso's Karma mine and ordered Franco-Nevada to pay roughly $9.3 million to the operator, Riverstone Karma SA. The company responded on 16 June 2026 that the agreement is governed by Ontario law, that it considers the judgment invalid, and that it is pursuing remedies in Ontario and other jurisdictions. The direct dollar exposure is immaterial. The precedent is not. A meaningful share of the portfolio sits in Peru (Antapaccay, Yanacocha, Condestable), Chile (Candelaria), South Africa (Western Limb), Ecuador (Cascabel), Panama and Argentina. The whole royalty premise is that a contract binds regardless of who operates the mine or who governs the country. One court has now said otherwise.

**Reported EBITDA quality slipped in Q1 2026.** The quarter's headline profit includes a gain on the partial buy-back of the Cascabel stream and a refund from the Canada Revenue Agency. The company's adjusted figure strips both — but a screen reading the raw statement gets a margin of 97.8% instead of the true 91.0%.

### 🟢 Green flags

**No dilution, none.** Diluted share count rose 0.5% across eight quarters while the company deployed billions. Every acquisition was funded from cash flow or a revolver repaid within a quarter. In a sector defined by serial equity issuance, this is the strongest structural positive available.

**Consistent refusal to over-promise on Cobre Panama.** Fully impaired at the 2023 shutdown, excluded from guidance in every quarter since, and framed as "all upside" rather than as a base-case recovery. If it restarts, the earnings arrive unexpected; if it does not, no guidance breaks.

**Reserve life disclosed with numbers, not adjectives (Q1 2026).** "In aggregate, for our mining portfolio at current production rates, M&I resources would support 34 years of mining and inferred resources a further 12 years," across 121 cash-flow-producing assets, with an asset-by-asset mine-life table published in the annual handbook. Very few resource companies publish this granularly.

**Dividend discipline held under pressure.** Nineteen consecutive annual increases, a 16% raise in January 2026, and two flat refusals of a special dividend when analysts asked.

**No hedging, transparently stated.** "We do not hedge. So we sell our gold at spot" (Q2 2025). Investors get the metal exposure they think they are buying.

**Portfolio concentration falling.** "No 1 asset generates more than 13% of revenue" (full-year 2025), improving to no asset above 10% in Q3 2025. Genuine diversification, not a slogan.

**Governance transition handled cleanly.** David Harquail stepped down as Chair after eighteen years, succeeded by Tom Albanese (formerly chief executive of Rio Tinto and Vedanta, and previously Franco-Nevada's lead independent director). An internal, experienced, telegraphed handover — the low-risk version of a chair transition.

## 7. HIDDEN SIGNALS

**A new business line entered the vocabulary without being announced as one.** Q3 2025: "In the last few years, we've added new avenue to grow our company. That is finding good teams and good projects and not just providing royalty or stream financing, but being their financial banker." Franco-Nevada is increasingly taking equity and loan positions alongside its royalties — G Mining, Discovery Silver, i-80, Minerals 260, EMX. The balance sheet shows it: long-term investments rose from $1.139 billion (2025-12-31) to $1.335 billion (2026-03-31), with $506 million of unrealised gains sitting in accumulated other comprehensive income. Two consequences follow. First, a portion of "royalty company" value is now a marked-to-market junior-equity book with far higher beta than the contracts. Second, the gains largely bypass reported earnings — Rana noted in Q3 2025 that a $67.4 million gain on Discovery shares "would have generated an additional earnings per share of $0.30" but was booked to other comprehensive income instead.

**Tax structure moved from background to foreground.** By Q1 2026 the company was fielding questions on a settled Canada Revenue Agency dispute (deposits returned with interest), new Canadian transfer-pricing rules effective 2026 forward, and a second revolving facility opened at the Barbados subsidiary ($750 million, alongside $1.5 billion at the parent). Rana's answer — "we think we've got a very good structure in place" — is a fair answer to a question that did not exist eighteen months earlier. Watch it.

**The oil royalty book resurfaced on price, not on strategy.** Barely mentioned in 2025; by Q1 2026 Brink was calling it a differentiator: "at current prices, oil and liquids can contribute meaningfully to our revenue mix," with West Texas Intermediate crude "hovering around $100 per barrel" following the late-February events in Iran. Crude trades at $75.74 as of today — essentially back to the $70 guidance assumption. The tailwind flagged in May has largely dissipated by August; it will show up in the Q2 print and then fade.

**A subtle change in how deals are framed.** Early calls stressed asset quality. Later calls stress *jurisdiction*: "most of that has been in Canada, the U.S. and Australia. We think those are super jurisdictions" (full-year 2025), with Brink adding that in riskier places "you've got to think about the discount rate… but in particular, we think about the payback." The company was already repricing sovereign risk months before a Burkinabè court validated the concern.

**New voices, same message.** Bonavie Tek replaced Candida Hayden in investor relations in Q1 2026; Eaun Gray (chief investment officer) took a larger speaking role from the full-year 2025 call. Neither changed the substance or the tone — a sign the messaging is institutional rather than personality-driven.

## 8. CAPITAL-ALLOCATION VERDICT (for the director)

**Trajectory: STRENGTHENING.** Revenue grew 77% year over year in Q1 2026 to a record $650.7 million; adjusted operating profit grew 84%; the balance sheet is debt-free with $717 million of cash; guidance was met at the top of its range; share count is flat; ten quality assets were added in twenty-one months without issuing stock. Nothing in the file is deteriorating.

**The single thing the director must weigh before sizing:** *you are being asked to pay a full price for peak-cycle earnings.* The trailing twelve months captured an average gold price around $4,880 in the March quarter. Gold trades at $4,155 today — about 15% below that average and roughly 22% below its March peak of $5,366. The stock has fallen 22.5% from its 52-week high of $285.67, almost exactly in line with the metal, which is what a two-year commodity beta of 1.02 predicts. So the earnings denominator is about to shrink while the multiple has not. Every other question here — Cobre, the deal pipeline, the growth outlook — is second-order to whether you are comfortable underwriting a superb business at roughly 21 times enterprise value to operating profit on an earnings base that a falling metal price is already eroding.

**Economic moat: WIDE. Moat trend: STABLE.**

The moat type is a *contractual and intangible* one with a genuine scale-cost advantage attached. Franco-Nevada's cash cost is $341 per gold-equivalent ounce — roughly a fifth of a typical gold producer's all-in sustaining cost. It sits at the far-left edge of the industry cost curve not by operating better but by not operating at all, and it holds perpetual, non-terminable claims on ore bodies it did not have to build. That position cannot be competed away by a rival raising capital; it can only be diluted by paying too much for the next contract.

The evidence says the moat is holding, not eroding. Return on equity is 18.7% and return on invested capital 14.3%, both rising. Gross margin expanded from 71.0% (Q1 2025) to 80.9% (Q1 2026) on revenue up 77% — the near-fixed cost per ounce converting price into profit. Margin per ounce roughly doubled over five quarters. Those are widening-moat readings. Two things hold the trend at *stable* rather than *widening*: return on invested capital still trails Wheaton Precious Metals' 18.1%, reflecting the price paid for recent additions, and the Karma judgment showed that contractual enforceability — the load-bearing assumption of the entire model — is not costless everywhere.

**Secular threat: MANAGEABLE.** No structural force is displacing gold royalties. There is no technology substitution, no disintermediation, no channel shift. The one genuine structural pressure is resource nationalism — Panama in 2023, Burkina Faso in 2026, with Mali, Niger and Mexico as the surrounding pattern — and Franco-Nevada is actively managing it by concentrating new deals in Canada, the United States and Australia while carrying a legacy book across Peru, Chile, South Africa, Ecuador and Argentina. It is a real, priceable risk that argues for a modest discount on the non-OECD share of the contract book. It does not impair terminal value.

**Cheapness is therefore not the question — the price is not cheap.** This is a clean, high-quality franchise trading at a full multiple on peak-cycle earnings, not a mispriced one and not a value trap. It is a business worth owning at the right price, and today's price is not obviously it.
